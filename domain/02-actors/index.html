    Actors, Accounts, and Self-Registration¶

    How a human being gets into the system, what an account is (and is not), and why registration is deliberately separate from membership.

    1. The core separation¶

       person ─────────── the human being. Exists even with no account and no membership.
     │
     ├── account ──── the ability to log in. Optional. Zero or more per person over time, one live.
     │
     └── membership ─ the state of being a member. Optional. Zero or more periods.

    These are three independent things. Any combination is legal:

    State Example
    person, no account, no membership 1994 roster entry imported from paper.
    person, account, no membership A parent who registered to manage their child.
    person, account, membership An active member.
    person, no account, membership An adult member who never uses the system; staff manage them.
    person, account, lapsed membership Someone who let their membership lapse but still logs in.

    The schema must not reject any of these. In particular there must be no requirement that a person has an email address, or that a member has an account — otherwise the historical import is impossible.

    2. Who registers, and why¶

    People register on their own to get access to the system; a person becomes a member only by requesting a membership, being accepted, and paying the yearly fee. So registration produces an account, not a membership. The person is then a registered non-member — able to log in, maintain their profile, view their own history, and submit a membership request.

    The split gives the system a clean place to hang capabilities that must work before membership exists:

    • A caretaker registering to manage a child before deciding whether to join themselves.
    • An adult applying to become a counselor, who needs to fill in an application first.
    • A returning member from the 90s reconnecting to their history.

    Registration paths¶

    Path Actor Result
    Self-registration adult, or a minor with guardian involvement person + account
    Registration on behalf caretaker registers a minor who has no account person (child) + guardianship
    Staff-created account registrar creates one for an adult who is not self-sufficient with computers person + account, flagged created_by_staff
    Legacy import no account created person only, record_origin = 'legacy_import'
    Self-registration claiming history adult registers and asserts a past membership person + account + identity_hypothesis
    Sign-up colliding with an existing person adult signs up again through an address the system did not know, and strong-matches someone who already exists person + account, both held at pending_verification, + identity_hypothesis — and no merge, no link, no activation. The address typed survives as the credential it was offered as, and the hypothesis is what holds the account out of active (§3)

    Minor registration — the consent problem¶

    Assumption: a minor may not create an account unattended. The schema supports requiring a guardian's confirmation before a minor's account becomes usable, via account.guardian_consent_at. See ../open-questions.md for the age threshold and whether consent is required at all in your jurisdiction.

    3. Account state machine¶

    Status Meaning
    pending_verification Registered; email/phone not yet confirmed. Minimal capabilities.
    active Normal.
    suspended Temporarily blocked (security, disciplinary). Membership unaffected.
    closed Voluntarily or administratively closed. Retained for audit; login refused.

    Two deliberate choices:

    • Suspending an account does not suspend a membership. They are separate lifecycles; a person can be an active member with a suspended account (they'd interact by phone).
    • Closing an account does not delete the person or their history. The account row is closed and retained. person and all membership rows are untouched.

    And a gate this table cannot express. active has a second condition beyond verification: an account may not reach it while an unresolved identity_hypothesis names its person. One live account per person is the rule doing the underlying work — uniqueness says a person may not hold two live accounts, and the gate says the second arrival waits for a human rather than being merged into the first or quietly linked to it.

    Why a human, and not an automatic link: a merge made by machine turns one person's false positive into one person's record becoming another's. Making a queue state block a login buys two obligations, and both are checks rather than intentions:

    • A hypothesis that is blocking an account is age-checked, and it alerts. A queue that stalls is not a data-quality problem with no victim you can name: it is a specific person who cannot log in, whose only remaining channel is to telephone someone.
    • The message the person sees confirms nothing. Not that the name matched, not that the birth date did, not that anyone by that name exists — because a sign-up form that can answer "yes, that person exists" is an existence oracle, and this system's roster is children.

    The trigger is a strong match only — exact legacy member number, or exact birth date together with a normalised name. The scored band below that raises nothing at sign-up: blocking a stranger's registration on a fuzzy score is the failure this rule would most deserve (03-identity-and-legacy.md §4).

    Why: D-089.

    Credentials¶

    account holds authentication material. Two rules worth fixing now:

    1. The password hash lives in account_credential, not in account. This leaves room for multiple authentication methods (password, passkey, OAuth via a national Scout identity provider, staff-issued one-time links) without restructuring.
    2. Never share an account between a parent and a child. The temptation is real — "the parent will just log in as the kid" — but it destroys attributability. The correct mechanism is acting on behalf, which records who acted and as whom. An account shared between people makes every audit entry worthless, and in a youth organization the audit trail is the point.

    4. Contact points and identifiers¶

    A person's email, phone and address are rows, not columns. Each row carries its type, its purpose, its value, whether it is the primary one for that type and purpose, when it was verified, and the period it applied for.

    Why not just person.email:

    • People have several emails (personal, school, work) and which one is primary changes.
    • A caretaker's contact details are often the effective contact for a child; the system needs both the child's own contact and the adult's.
    • Addresses change, and history is kept: an address row with a validity period answers "where did this member live in 2003?", which a column cannot.
    • Changing an email must not lose the old one, because historical records may reference it.

    Purpose is the field that makes this useful operationally: login, membership, emergency, billing, guardian_contact. A person can have one address for billing and another for emergency contact, and the permission system can treat them differently — emergency contacts are visible to whoever holds the roster read on that camp; billing details are not.

    External identifiers¶

    One row per identifier held against a person: a scheme, a value, and when it was verified if it ever was. (scheme, value) is unique among the identifiers that are still current — a superseded number may be reused, a current one may not be shared.

    Identifiers issued outside the system belong here: a national Scout registration number, a government ID, an old membership card number from a previous system, a school ID. Keeping these separate from contact points is what gives the legacy import somewhere to put the old system's member numbers — which is precisely what makes reconnecting 1990–2010 members possible. See 03-identity-and-legacy.md.

    5. Acting on behalf¶

    Both read and write access for a caretaker are explicit:

    • A guardianship row establishes the relationship.
    • That row carries one right — none, view, or write. Being the parent implies nothing; a row at none grants literally nothing.

    The right is one value, not a capability list, compared against permission.right_kind ∈ read | write: none covers nothing, view covers the codes marked read, write covers both. A court order that lets one parent register the child for events but not read the rest of the record cannot be written, and neither can "roster visibility and nothing else": a guardian at view reads the child's record whole, because the record has no compartment inside it to be excepted from (D-074, D-083). What the right is, and keeps: explicit, dated, revocable, audited — one deliberate value per row rather than an inference from who someone is.

    write is not a license over the organization. The ceiling is the channel, not a rank: the guardianship channel is entered only when the check is about somebody other than the actor, so the widest right in the system reaches this child's record and cannot reach a roster, a fee schedule or another family — those checks are not about the child and the channel is never asked (D-079). Authority is per row, so a guardian of one child sees nothing of another's. A deny still refuses a guardian holding write, absolutely and regardless of scope — what is unavailable is a narrow deny, not the ability to say no. There is no per-resource admin wildcard either; a role that needs broad reach lists what it grants (D-068).

    History is kept: every change of the value is a new guardianship_permission row, and the value on the guardianship row is always the open one's.

    How a check resolves. A check about a dependent is evaluated against the guardianship row for the (guardian, dependent) pair and the right level it holds, not against roles. This keeps the two authority channels distinct:

    • Grants answer "what may this person do in the organization?"
    • Guardianship answers "what may this person do regarding this child?"

    An office confers nothing by being held (D-100), so holding one gives nobody a guardian's right over a child and leadership has no channel of its own: a leader reaches data the way every other non-guardian does, through grants. If the organization needs its leaders to see a camper's emergency contacts, that is said, as a grant of person.view, and the scope chosen is the entire decision — every member of the organization or the people on one event, with nothing between them (D-097), and person.view has no narrower cousin to reach for if the first one turns out to feel like what it is (08-person-data.md). Audited either way; never through a fictional guardianship.

    Recording acts on behalf¶

    Every operation performed on behalf of another person writes to access_log with both parties: actor_person_id is the parent, subject_person_id is the child, and on_behalf records that the actor acted for the subject rather than as themselves.

    This is not optional bookkeeping. When a parent changes a child's address or registers them for a camp, the record must show that the parent did it, not the child. Without this, the audit trail falsely attributes the parent's actions to the child.

    6. Effective contact for a minor¶

    A recurring operational need: "who do we call about this child?" The answer is usually a guardian, but not always the same one, and not always the one with the most permissions.

    Resolve it as three conditions on guardianship, applied in this order: the row's dependent is this child; the row is still open today, meaning valid_to is absent or later than today — a guardianship that has ended is not an answer, so the date is checked on the row rather than assumed; and is_primary_contact is set. The answer is the contact points of those guardians whose purpose is emergency.

    is_primary_contact on guardianship is separate from the guardian's right level on purpose: a guardian with no system access at all can still be the emergency contact. Do not require login capability to be reachable in an emergency.

    7. Open issues¶

    • Minimum age to hold an account; whether guardian consent is legally required.
    • Whether staff-created accounts are allowed for children, or only adults.

    All tracked in ../open-questions.md.
