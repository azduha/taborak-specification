    Domain Overview¶

    What the organization is, who interacts with the system, and how the core lifecycles flow. Read ../glossary.md first — this document uses its terms strictly.

    1. The organization¶

    A youth organization (Scout-like: uniformed groups, trained adult leadership, camps and weekly meetings) with:

    • Child members — youth participants, typically organized into units.
    • Adult members — counselors/leaders who run units and hold positions.
    • Caretakers — parents and legal guardians of child members. They may or may not be members themselves.
    • Non-member supporters — people who interact with the organization (donors, volunteers, alumni) without holding a membership.

    The organization has sub-units that nest and that have been reorganized repeatedly over four decades. Both facts are load-bearing and are handled in 04-organization-structure.md.

    2. Actors¶

    The system distinguishes actors by what they can do, not by what they are. The full treatment is in 02-actors.md; the summary:

    Actor Has account? Has membership? Can do
    Anonymous visitor no no see public info, submit a registration or a membership request
    Registered person yes no maintain own profile, submit/renew a request, view own history
    Member (child) yes yes own profile, unit membership, event signups, own history
    Member (adult) yes yes as above, plus whatever their role assignments permit
    Caretaker yes maybe view/act on their dependents — only as explicitly granted
    Staff / registrar yes maybe organization-wide administrative permissions
    Historical record no no — participation rows only nothing. Data only, never a login.

    The last row matters twice. Imported 1994 rosters create person rows with no account, and must never be blocked from import by a "users must have email addresses" rule. And they create no membership either: a roster evidences presence in a unit, and the corpus carries no payment evidence, so an imported person's history is unit_participation and a membership row appears only where a human recorded one on evidence (D-090). The earlier version of this table said "yes (closed)" in that cell, which was the membership-per-roster reflex the import rule exists to stop.

    3. The three lifecycles¶

    Almost everything in the system is one of three multi-step processes. They are separate because they succeed and fail independently: you can register without applying, apply without being accepted, be accepted without paying, and pay without ever joining a unit.

    3.1 Account lifecycle¶

                      ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │                                             │
 (none) ──register──> pending_verification ──verify──> active ──┴──> suspended
                                                            │              │
                                                            └──> closed <──┘

    An account is about authentication only. It says "this person can log in". It says nothing about membership. A person can hold a verified, active account for years and never become a member — a parent who only ever manages their child is exactly this case.

    One gate the arrows do not draw: an account may not reach active while an unresolved identity_hypothesis names its person, which is what a sign-up that strong-matches someone who already exists runs into (D-089). Verification is necessary and not sufficient; a human decision is the second condition, and because that makes a stalled queue a person who cannot log in, the blocking hypothesis is age-checked and alerts. One live account per person is the rule doing the underlying work (02-actors.md §3).

    3.2 Membership lifecycle¶

     (none)
   │
   ├─ request submitted ──> under_review ──accept──> approved ──payment confirmed──> active member
   │                             │                                                    │
   │                             └──reject──> rejected                                 │
   │                                                                                   │
   │                                     ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │                                     │                                             │
   │                                     ├──> lapsed (fee expired at the yearly date)  │
   │                                     ├──> terminated (cause, with reason)          │
   │                                     └──> suspended (temporary hold)               │
   │                                                                                   │
   │      ┌──────────────── renewal (no new application) ────────────────────────────┘
   │      │        inside the renewal window → the new period opens at the expiry date
   │      │        after it                  → the new period opens at the payment date
   │      ▼
   │   active member again
   │
   └──────────────────────── re-apply ─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

    Every transition creates or closes a row; nothing is overwritten. A person who was a member 1990–2010 and again from 2024 has two membership rows and the gap is visible as data, not inferred from a date.

    Note the shape of the lapse. There is no completed state: it existed only for ageing out, and membership is ageless (D-038). And the lapse happens on a date, to everyone at once — the yearly expiry — which is why the renewal path matters more here than the word "grace" suggests (D-040).

    Detailed in 05-membership-lifecycle.md.

    3.3 Authorization lifecycle¶

     (none) ──grant──> active ──expire/revoke──> inactive
                     │
                     └──delegate──> delegated grant (with end date, provenance recorded)

    Every grant has a start, an end, a granter, and a reason. Nothing is permanent by default and nothing is revoked by deletion. Detailed in 06-authorization-model.md.

    4. The relationships between people¶

    Three distinct relationships, deliberately not merged:

    Relationship Direction Example Grants access?
    Guardianship guardian → dependent parent → child No by itself — only the right_level (none / view / write) carried on the live guardianship row. Since D-074 that value is the whole authority; guardianship_permission keeps only its history.
    Membership member → the organization anyone → the root unit No.
    Participation participant → sub-unit child → Pack 12 No.
    Position held person → position in a sub-unit adult → scoutmaster of Pack 12 No. Only via role_assignment.

    Membership and participation are two rows, not one (D-041). A person is a member of the organization and a participant in a pack — different facts with different lifetimes, which is why someone who stops attending on Thursdays is still a paid-up member, and someone who is paid up may take part in nothing.

    The recurring theme: participation is not authority. A person can be deeply involved — a parent of three members, a 20-year veteran, a pack's founder — and still have no administrative access. Access comes only from an explicit grant. This separation is what makes the permission system auditable and what prevents the classic failure where a relationship ends (custody changes, a leader steps down) but access silently persists.

    The counselor-who-is-also-a-parent case¶

    You named this case explicitly. It is not special:

    • One person row.
    • One open membership — member, like everyone else's.
    • A position_assignment making them a leader of their pack.
    • One unit_participation row for the pack they lead.
    • Two guardianship rows (to their own children).
    • Separate guardianship_permission rows for their parental capabilities.
    • role_assignment rows for their leadership authority.

    Note there is no counselor membership type. Being a counselor is the position, not a kind of membership (D-037) — and this person's membership is identical to that of the child they lead, which is exactly the point.

    Their leadership role grants authority over the unit's members. Their guardianship grants authority over their own children. These are different scopes and the system keeps them separate — critically, being a leader does not give them access to other people's children's records beyond what their leadership role explicitly permits.

    This last point is a safety property, not a convenience. Child-protection rules generally require that a leader's access to minors' personal data be bounded by their function. Keeping role_assignment and guardianship_permission as separate tables makes that boundary enforceable and reportable.

    5. Multiplicity, in both directions¶

    You specified: one parent can manage multiple children; multiple children can be managed by the same parent. The model generalizes both:

    • One guardian → many dependents (many guardianship rows).
    • Many guardians → one dependent (both parents, stepparents, legal guardians — the schema makes no assumption that a child has exactly two, or exactly one).
    • A guardian's authority over each dependent is per-dependent, so a parent can hold full permissions for one child and only read access for another (a realistic custody arrangement).

    The same person can also be a guardian of someone and a dependent of someone else — the relation is a general graph, not a two-level hierarchy. This costs nothing to allow and avoids a rewrite when a member turns 18 and becomes guardian to a sibling.

    6. The four decades of history¶

    This is the constraint that most shapes the schema, so it is worth stating plainly.

    The system must answer, correctly and indefinitely:

    • "Who was in Pack 12 on 1 June 1998?"
    • "Was this person a member when they attended that camp in 2003?"
    • "This person registered in 2026 claiming they were a member in the 90s — is that true?"
    • "What was this unit called in 2001, and what is its successor unit today?"

    Consequences, all of which the data model implements:

    1. Everything temporal is a period, never a boolean or a single date.
    2. Nothing is hard-deleted, except by three named acts, each of which is somebody's decision rather than a cleanup job. Merges leave tombstones and units that closed keep their rows. Against that: erasing a person redacts their values wherever they were copied, including in the audit payload, immediately and at any age; payload reduction empties an audit row's values 24 months after they were written and leaves the row standing, so the trail keeps answering who changed this, when, and were they allowed to indefinitely and stops answering and to what (D-088); and reverting an import batch removes the rows that batch created. Reduction is not redaction — one answers to a person and is absolute, the other answers to a calendar and is bounded — and the accounting skeleton (membership, fees, payments) is deliberately outside all three: it is kept indefinitely as the organization's own history. Assumption: that "forever" has a legal basis is exactly the thing this document cannot establish for itself; the claim is recorded as an assumption awaiting review, not as settled law.
    3. Names change. Units get renamed and renumbered; people change names. Names live in child tables with validity periods, not as columns that get overwritten.
    4. Imported data is low-confidence. A 1994 roster has names, dates, and on most rows a legacy member number — which keys the organization's paper, not a session, and is exactly what is missing from the records that most need matching. This bullet used to say "no reliable unique key", written before the corpus had been described. Import still therefore proposes identities and a human confirms them, and where a number is present the proposal is a lookup rather than a guess. See 03-identity-and-legacy.md.

    7. What this design deliberately does not decide yet¶

    These are referenced by the schema but not designed, pending your specification:

    (Events came off this list: they were specified, in 07-events.md and data-model/08-events.md, along with the thirteen codes in the catalog.)

    • Fees and payments — a provisional shape exists in ../data-model/07-finance.md because membership activation depends on it. Expect to rewrite it.
    • Notifications, documents, reporting, API/UI. (Health records went off this list in round 7 — there is no health module, because D-083 found no category to build one around; the facts live in the personal-attribute layer, domain/08.)
