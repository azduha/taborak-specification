    Domain Overview¶

    What the organization is, who interacts with the system, and how the core lifecycles flow. Read ../glossary.md first — this document uses its terms strictly.

    1. The organization¶

    A youth organization (Scout-like: uniformed groups, trained adult leadership, camps and weekly meetings) with:

    • Child members — youth participants, who join one another in groups.
    • Adult members — counselors and leaders who run the programs, hold offices, and put people into groups.
    • Caretakers — parents and legal guardians of child members. They may or may not be members themselves.
    • Non-member supporters — people who interact with the organization (donors, volunteers, alumni) without holding a membership.

    "Child" and "adult" are how the organization talks about people. They are not two kinds of membership: there is one membership type and it is ageless, so "is this person a child?" is a question about person.birth_date and the date being asked about (§2). Only three things downstream key off the difference: guardianship, eligibility an organizer writes for one event, and the mirroring rule for messages.

    The system models no internal structure of the organization. There is one unit, it is a scope rather than a row, and the nested bodies the organization has been reorganized into over four decades are not stored, not versioned and not imported — an absence decided on purpose, whose cost is written down in D-097. What remains of "the inside of the organization" is groups: named sets of people with a lifetime, in 04-organization-structure.md. A reader arriving expecting a hierarchy should learn that here, on the first page, because the absence changes every later answer about scope.

    2. Actors¶

    The system distinguishes actors by what they can do, not by what they are. The full treatment is in 02-actors.md; the summary:

    Actor Has account? Has membership? Can do
    Anonymous visitor no no see public info, submit a registration or a membership request
    Registered person yes no maintain own profile, submit/renew a request, view own history
    Member yes yes own profile, event signups, own history
    Caretaker yes maybe view and act on their dependents — only as the guardianship row allows
    Staff / registrar yes maybe organization-wide administrative permissions
    Historical record no a bounded period, if the export states one nothing. Data only, never a login.

    A minor is not an actor row. One row above is a member, and whether that member is a minor today is person.birth_date against the date of the check — a fact about a person that changes on its own, never a kind of account. What a minor genuinely cannot do alone is a guardianship question (02-actors.md).

    Imported rows create person rows with no account, and import must never be blocked by a "users must have email addresses" rule. They also create membership rows: bounded ones, both ends dated, only for the periods the people export states, data_confidence = 'low', marked with the batch that wrote them (D-093). An imported period is never open-ended and never inferred from presence — an open membership satisfies is_current_member(), so inferring one would make every 1998 attendee a current member today. No sub-unit row is ever imported, because neither export names a sub-unit (03-identity-and-legacy.md §7).

    3. The three lifecycles¶

    Almost everything in the system is one of three multi-step processes. They are separate because they succeed and fail independently: you can register without applying, apply without being accepted, be accepted without paying, and pay without ever joining a group.

    3.1 Account lifecycle¶

                      ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │                                             │
 (none) ──register──> pending_verification ──verify──> active ──┴──> suspended
                                                            │              │
                                                            └──> closed <──┘

    An account is about authentication only. It says "this person can log in". It says nothing about membership. A person can hold a verified, active account for years and never become a member — a parent who only ever manages their child is exactly this case.

    One gate the arrows do not draw: an account may not reach active while an unresolved identity_hypothesis names its person, which is what a sign-up that strong-matches someone who already exists runs into (D-089). Verification is necessary and not sufficient; a human decision is the second condition. Because that makes a stalled queue a person who cannot log in, a hypothesis that blocks activation is age-checked and alerts. One live account per person is the rule doing the underlying work (02-actors.md §3).

    3.2 Membership lifecycle¶

     (none)
   │
   ├─ request submitted ──> under_review ──accept──> approved ──payment confirmed──> active member
   │                             │                                                    │
   │                             └──reject──> rejected                                 │
   │                                                                                   │
   │                                     ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │                                     ├──> lapsed (fee expired at the yearly date)   │
   │                                     ├──> terminated (cause, with reason)          │
   │                                     └──> suspended (temporary hold)               │
   │                                                                                   │
   │      ┌──────────────── renewal (no new application) ────────────────────────────┘
   │      │        inside the renewal window → the new period opens at the expiry date
   │      │        after it                  → the new period opens at the payment date
   │      ▼
   │   active member again
   │
   └──────────────────────── re-apply ─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

    Every transition creates or closes a row; nothing is overwritten. A person who was a member 1990–2010 and again from 2024 has two membership rows, and the gap is visible as data rather than inferred from a date.

    Two properties of the lapse are worth naming before the detail. There is no completed status: nothing ages out of a membership, so there is no state for having finished one. And the lapse happens to everyone at once, on a fixed date — the yearly expiry — which is why the renewal path matters more than the word "grace" suggests (D-040).

    Detailed in 05-membership-lifecycle.md.

    3.3 Authorization lifecycle¶

     (none) ──grant──> active ──end date reached / period closed──> inactive
                     │
                     └──delegate──> delegated grant (with end date, provenance recorded)

    Every grant has a start, an end, a granter, and a reason. Nothing is permanent by default and nothing is revoked by deletion — closing a period is the act, and the row stays. Detailed in 06-authorization-model.md.

    4. The relationships between people¶

    Four relationships, deliberately not merged:

    Relationship Direction Example Grants access?
    Guardianship guardian → dependent parent → child No by itself — only the right_level (none / view / write) carried on the live guardianship row is authority, and that row is the whole of it (D-074).
    Membership member → the organization anyone → the unit No.
    Being in a group person → group child → the Thursday group No — a person_group_member row is a dated roster entry.
    Holding an office person → role adult → counselors No — a role_holder row says who holds it, and an office confers nothing by being held.

    Being a member and being in a group are two rows, not one. A person is a member of the organization and on a group's roster: different facts, different lifetimes, and different guards on who may change them — which is why someone who stops attending on Thursdays is still a paid-up member, and someone who is paid up may turn up to nothing. The two rows are edited by different people for different reasons, which is the whole case for keeping them apart.

    The recurring theme: participation is not authority. A person can be deeply involved — a parent of three members, a twenty-year veteran, the founder of a group — and still have no administrative access. Access comes only from an explicit grant. This separation is what makes the permission system auditable, and what prevents the classic failure where a relationship ends (custody changes, a leader steps down) and access silently persists.

    The counselor who is also a parent¶

    This case is not special:

    • One person row.
    • One open membership, of the one membership type, member — the same shape as the child they lead.
    • One role_holder row: they hold the office counselors over a dated period.
    • A person_group_member row or two, dated, for the groups they actually turn up to.
    • Two guardianship rows (to their own children), each carrying its own right level.
    • Whatever access_grant rows somebody wrote whose grantee names them — as ROLE(counselors), or MEMBERS * ROLE(counselors) where membership is required of the holder.
    • No row of its own for "is a counselor". The role_holder row is all of it.

    There is no counselor membership type, and being a counselor is an office held for a period — not a kind of membership, and not a grade of person. This person's membership is identical to that of the child they lead, which is exactly the point.

    Their grants reach the unit's members, which is the whole organization: there is no smaller body to scope inside, so a unit-scope grant is the broadest thing a person can hold. Their guardianship reaches their own children through a different channel entirely — entered by the shape of the request (subject ≠ actor) rather than by any permission they were given (D-079), and capped by the right level that row records. Being a leader does not give them access to other people's children's records, and the reason is mechanical: no rule could imply it, so the access exists as rows or not at all.

    That is a safety property, not a convenience. Child-protection practice requires that a leader's access to minors' personal data be bounded by their function, and two properties carry it here: an office, a roster, a membership and a guardianship each confer nothing by being what they are (D-100), and a grant is one code, one scope, one effect per row (D-099), so "what exactly does this person have over minors?" is answered by reading rows rather than by evaluating a rank. What the design does not do is guarantee that the reach was written by the body it governs — see rule 5 in the overview. The boundary is enforceable and reportable; who is obliged to read the report is a convention, not a guard.

    5. Multiplicity, in both directions¶

    Specified directly: one parent can manage multiple children; multiple children can be managed by the same parent. The model generalizes both:

    • One guardian → many dependents (many guardianship rows).
    • Many guardians → one dependent (both parents, stepparents, legal guardians — no assumption that a child has exactly two, or exactly one).
    • A guardian's authority is per-dependent, so a parent can hold write for one child and view only for another — a realistic custody arrangement.

    The same person can be a guardian of someone and a dependent of someone else: the relation is a general graph, not a two-level hierarchy. This costs nothing to allow and avoids a rewrite when a member's sibling needs a guardian and the member is the one who has them.

    6. The four decades of history¶

    This is the constraint that most shapes the schema.

    The system must answer, correctly and indefinitely:

    • "Was this person a member when they attended that camp in 2003?"
    • "This person registered in 2026 claiming they were a member in the 90s — is that true?"
    • "Who was on this group's roster on 1 June 1998?" — if somebody recorded the roster; the import supplies no group history.

    It must not answer, and does not try to:

    • "Who was in Pack 12 on 1 June 1998?" and "what was that unit called in 2001, and what is its successor today?" There are no units, no nesting and no successor relations to traverse.

    Consequences, which the data model implements:

    1. Everything temporal is a period, never a boolean or a single date.
    2. Nothing is hard-deleted, except by three named acts, each somebody's decision rather than a cleanup job. Erasing a person redacts their values wherever they were copied, including in the audit payload, immediately and at any age. Payload reduction empties an audit row's values 24 months after they were written and leaves the row standing, so the trail keeps answering who changed this, when, and were they allowed to and stops answering and to what (D-088). Reverting an import batch removes the rows that batch created. Reduction is not redaction: one answers to a person and is absolute, the other answers to a calendar and is bounded. The accounting skeleton — membership, fees, payments — is deliberately outside all three and kept indefinitely as the organization's own history. Assumption: that this "forever" has a legal basis is exactly what this document cannot establish for itself; the claim is recorded as an assumption awaiting review, not as settled law.
    3. People's names change, so a name is a child-table row with a validity period and never a column that gets overwritten. A group's name is a column, and nothing replaces the deleted name-history table: a rename edits it and the old name is gone. What survives a rename is the identity beneath it, because grants and expressions store a kind and an id, never a name.
    4. Imported data is low-confidence. The people export carries a name, a birth date, and the period each person was a member of the organization. Where an export also carries the organization's legacy member number — the number its paper records are keyed on — that number is imported verbatim into person_identifier under the legacy scheme, is never reissued, and makes a match exact rather than probable: same number, same person. Every other identity claim the import makes is a proposal a human confirms. See 03-identity-and-legacy.md.

    7. What this design does not decide yet¶

    Referenced by the schema but not designed, pending your specification:

    • Fees and payments — a provisional shape exists in ../data-model/07-finance.md because membership activation depends on it. Expect to rewrite it.
    • Communication — its shape is decided (direct by default, broadcast an ordinary scoped permission, the recipient chooses the channel, a minor's guardians get a mirror of a direct message), and its codes are in the catalog. There is no message or message_recipient table yet, and no design for templates, opt-out granularity, retention or moderation.
    • Notifications — the renewal window made them dated rather than status-driven, which is a design note, not a design.
    • Documents, reporting, API and UI.
