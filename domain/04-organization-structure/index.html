    Groups¶

    One organization, and the named sets of people inside it.

    The vocabulary, first, because everything here leans on it.

    Word Means Stored as
    unit the organization itself. There is exactly one. It is a scope, never a row nothing — a code in grant_scope_type
    group a named set of people with explicitly dated members and a lifetime. A set, not a body person_group + person_group_member
    role an office: a small, visible set of holders — leaders, finance, IT role + role_holder
    audience an expression over named sets, which a grant is aimed at audience
    selector one operand of that expression: MEMBERS, GROUP(x), ROLE(x), … selector_type, audience_operand

    Roles and audiences belong to the authorization model (06-authorization-model.md, ../data-model/05-authorization.md) and are not re-specified here. This page owns the unit and the group.

    1. The organization is one unit, and the unit is a scope¶

    There is one unit and it is the organization. It has no parent, no children, no type, no code, no path, no depth, and no row. It is the non-item scope that grant_scope_type already carries — code unit, is_item_scoped = false — and a grant written at it has no scope_id. Nothing in the schema represents it, because there is nothing to represent: a second unit cannot appear, for the same reason a second answer to a question cannot appear in a field that does not exist.

    Membership is unchanged and is not a group. One membership per person, at the unit, paid for yearly:

    • "everyone in the organization" is one query against membership;
    • "everyone in the Thursday group" is one query against person_group_member.

    Two shapes, and neither one borrows the other's table. Renewal opens a new membership row rather than extending the old one (D-016).

    A grant at the unit reaches everything — every item of every type — because nothing stands between the unit and an item and there is no ownership relation to test. That is a sentence about the permission system; it is stated here only because it is what "scope" means now that "row" does not.

    Why the design carries no hierarchy: the legacy corpus names no units at all — two exports, people with their membership period and events with attendance, no unit column anywhere (D-092) — and a structure that describes nothing imported and restricts nothing carries no information (D-097).

    What that costs, said once¶

    These are properties the hierarchy was carrying, listed here rather than distributed through the page. They are not argued away.

    1. The unit is not a locus of accountability for a child's records. This is the one that matters. Nothing in the model reproduces "access to a body's children was granted by that body" — by the people who know them, who recruited its leader, and who can be asked. With one unit there is no "outside it" to be stopped from, so the rule has no subject rather than a weaker form. A permission held at the unit reaches every member, and person.view is the widest read permission in the system (D-083, D-086). What stands between a child and a stranger's read is a permission held by a named adult, an audit trail whose payloads expire after two years (D-088), and a report of who holds the write.
    2. There is no subtree question. "Who was in the Thursday group in 2011?" stays answerable, because group member rows are dated (§5). "And everything nested under it" has no subject: a set of people has no sub-sets stored inside it.
    3. Restructuring is not a recorded event. A group that splits is an old group that ended and two that began, with no edge between them — and no table where such an edge could go (§4).
    4. Unit-scoped reports have nothing to report on. "A pack's roster", "a pack's events", "which units exist" are not hard questions, they are questions with no subject. The organizational-year reports that matter — membership and fees — live at the unit and are untouched.

    One invariant class now has no instance at all: shape 4 of F-10, a column that may not change once set, whose violation leaves every row individually valid.

    It is not a two-way door. This is the deletion of a dimension, not a flag. Adding structure back is a decision with a reason attached — most plausibly "the organization has outgrown one center's visibility" — and it would have to argue the accountability rule from its own premises, because the mechanism that made it work — a scope written by the body it covered — does not exist.

    2. What a group is, and everything a group is not¶

    A group is a person_group: a name, a description, its own lifetime, and explicitly dated members. It is a set of people, not a body. Somebody names it, somebody puts people in it, somebody ends it, and the record of who was in it when is the roster's own (§5). A weekly meeting group, a project crew, a trainers' pool, schuzka2026-1 — all the same kind of thing, and the system has no opinion about which ones deserve to exist. A person may be on several rosters at once; none of them is marked as the one to show first.

    Everything a body would have had, a group does not have.

    A group has Because
    no parent and no children there are no sub-units, and groups do not nest
    no type, and no vocabulary of types a group needs no word for what kind of set it is
    no code, no path, no depth a label carries no identity (§4); a set has no position to sit at
    no nesting rules there is nothing to nest
    no rename history the name is a column, and the old name is not kept (§4)
    no split, merger or succession edge restructuring is not an event (§1, cost 3)
    no leadership of its own see §6
    no roles an office is a role and a role is held at the unit; a group has none of its own, and no leadership column will be added to make one look derivable
    no fee membership is at the unit and is the only thing that costs money
    no permissions over its members see immediately below

    Membership of a group confers nothing, and tidier storage does not quietly change that. A member of a group cannot do anything: participation is not authority, and it stays not-authority when it moves into a table with better columns. Authority arrives only through a grant that names the group, which is §4 of ../data-model/02-organization.md.

    Where the line against an audience is drawn — the two are one row apart in shape and a mile apart in meaning: a group states its members, a selector picks them. A group's members are a list of people that is true whether or not any permission points at it. A leaf in an audience — a PERSON(y) row (D-105) — is an addition to an expression, meaningful only inside the expression it completes. A group exists to be talked about; a selector exists to be aimed at something. Merging them would make the permission table the system's roster.

    Where the line against a role is drawn, in one sentence, because it decides who may edit the list. A role is an office with a small, visible set of holders and is written by the most sensitive code family in the catalog (role.*, 🔒🔒); a group is company, and its roster is written by person_group.member.manage (⚠). One table with a kind column would give a single permission that both adds a child to the Thursday group and puts the holder among the administrators (D-100). GROUP(x) and ROLE(x) stay distinct in the grant text so the distinction is visible to whoever reads the grant.

    3. Joining a group is somebody else's act¶

    Nobody writes themselves onto a roster. The gate is person_group.member.manage, held at the unit — which reaches every group, because nothing stands between a unit-scope grant and an item — or granted on one group as an item, which is how "these two people may work this roster" is said without inventing a scope (D-098, D-099). There is no self-scoped variant and none will be added.

    Who enforces "do not place a child where nobody is responsible for them": whoever may write the member row in person_group_member, audited and naming who wrote it. A permission refuses every write by the wrong person regardless of the child's age (D-081).

    What that costs. The system cannot say which groups are for children. Nothing on a person_group answers that question and, consistent with "no special features", nothing will: no allows_minors, no is_children, no type to hang it on. Combined with no recorded background checks (D-043) and with cost 1 above, the safeguarding story of this module is a permission held by named adults, an audit trail that forgets values after two years, and a report. If that turns out to be too little — after an incident, or an insurer's question — the route back is a rule about who may be placed where, argued once as a decision with its enforcement layer named in D-081's terms, and not a resurrected column whose name implies it enforces something.

    A membership request is a request for membership of the unit, and carries no group. There is no requested_unit_id on a request: which group a new member lands in is a write on the group, not a preference on the request. The request flow in 05-membership-lifecycle.md ends at the unit; a roster is somebody else's decision afterwards.

    4. A name carries no identity¶

    A group name is a label, and a label is not the group: a group name that comes back after a decade is a new group, unless somebody records that it is not.

    Why the default matters: continuity is not a property of a label, it is a claim about the world, and only a human knows whether the crew refounded in 2026 is the crew of 2016. Left unstated, the question is answered anyway — by whoever writes the matching screen, who will match on name and let a thirty-year gap sit inside one group's life. So the default points at a new person_group row. A rename is an edit to one column and the previous name is not kept (§2).

    The continuity claim has nowhere to live. Groups keep no group-to-group edges — no split, no merger, no succession — so an assertion of continuity has no field to hold it (D-098). What the method guarantees is therefore the direction of the default, which is the half that was ever enforceable: a returning name is a new group, and two groups that are really one look like two unless a person writes that down somewhere. Where that sentence goes is not decided. The cheapest true answer available today is a note on the second group's member rows and on the group's description, which nothing reads and nothing links; the honest alternative is to accept that the organization's memory of its own continuity is prose. Status: assumption — this page does not choose, and the question is recorded in ../open-questions.md.

    Names are one namespace with roles and audiences. Because ROLE(x) and GROUP(x) do not have to disambiguate, a bare COUNSELORS resolves against roles and groups together, so names are unique across the tables, case/whitespace/separators are folded on write, and every selector_type.code is a reserved name — a group called MEMBERS is refused at the save rather than silently rewriting every expression in the system (D-101). audience.code is in the namespace too (D-103), and the pair of rules is worth holding apart: a bare name never resolves to an audience — the arm must be written AUDIENCE(veterans) — yet a group still may not borrow an audience's code, because a name that resolves two ways is a silent grant whatever the resolution rules say. No single constraint spans the three tables, so the write path carries the rule and an integrity check asserts that it does. What a grant stores is kind + id, never a name, so renaming a group never rewrites a grant. Mechanics: ../data-model/02-organization.md §1.

    5. A roster is history¶

    Member rows are dated, and that is what makes the roster answerable. person_group_member carries valid_from and valid_to per person, so joining, leaving and coming back are all periods on the same pair, and the roster has a history of its own instead of borrowing the audit trail's. "Who was in schuzka2026-1 on 14 March 2026?" is a point-in-time read over those rows. It survives the audit trail's 24-month payload expiry (D-088) because it does not depend on the trail — which is the entire reason the rows are dated rather than a live flag, and the reason a naming habit like schuzka2026-1 is a habit an organization may or may not keep rather than the mechanism history rests on.

    This is where a group is ahead of the operand that looks most like it. An audience named as an operand evaluates live, so its past shape survives only in the audit trail and stops surviving when the payloads expire; a group's set is its dated rows, so it keeps a past the audit trail cannot take away. "Who could see this child's data in 2024?" is answerable when the grant pointed at a group and is not answerable when it pointed at a nested audience (D-103). That asymmetry is a fact about which of the two is a roster, and it is the strongest reason the pair stays two tables.

    Two facts, both kept, and not to be conflated. The group's valid_from/valid_to says the group existed over that span; the member rows' periods say who was in it. An open group with dated members answers the historical question perfectly well, and an ended group's rows are not tidied up. Ending the group is not an edit anyone makes on their own: setting that end date is refused while a live grant, or a live audience reachable from one, names the group, because an empty set inside a subtraction widens a deny that the person ending the group did not write and would not look at again (the write path and the checks that watch it are ../data-model/02-organization.md §3).

    A person who left in 2020 and came back in 2024 is two rows, never one row edited in place, because the edit destroys the answer to "was she in the group in 2019?" (D-016, D-002).

    Moving from one group to another closes one row and opens another: never rewrite a member row to point at a different group. Rewriting destroys "where was this child in 2003?", and unlike most tempting edits this one leaves no evidence that anything happened. Mechanics: ../data-model/02-organization.md §4.

    No member row is ever inferred. The corpus names no groups any more than it names units (D-092), so the one-off import owes no group rows and no member rows at all: every person_group_member row in this system is typed by a person, which is also the only way one could be true.

    6. Appointments¶

    An appointment is a role_holder row: a role, a person, a period, who granted it, a note. Written by role.assign and removed by role.unassign, always held at the unit, because there is one scope and nowhere else to hold an office. It confers nothing by itself: the codes an office carries are the live grants whose expression names ROLE(x) (../data-model/05-authorization.md §3–§4). "That these two may manage the counselors roster" is a grant on the role, the same move a group gets. There is no position, no slot, no max_holders, and no is_leadership flag: nothing in the system records a seat that exists unfilled.

    This module holds no eligibility rule of any kind. There is no requires_membership and no min_age on an office, and no gate in the resolver: membership is an operand, written into the grant.

    The expression that says each thing, in the form D-101's canonical rendering stores it — every compound operand bracketed, a leaf left bare:

    The rule to express The expression
    the office applies only to current members (MEMBERS * ROLE(x))
    the office applies whether or not the holder is a member ROLE(x), written without MEMBERS
    one named person is exempt from the membership requirement (MEMBERS * ROLE(x)) + PERSON(y)

    PERSON(y) is the only way to name a human being in an expression. A set of named people is written PERSON(a) + PERSON(b), and there is no second spelling of it (D-105). A person who must be excepted is subtracted as themselves, and the exemption belongs + PERSON(y) inside the very grant that carries the rule, so the exception is read next to the thing it exempts. The registry of named operands lives in one table — the selector registry — and this page does not recite its row count.

    The posture is opt-in per grant, so a grant written as ROLE(TREASURERS) with no MEMBERS authorizes a lapsed member silently and forever. The controls are a standing report of live grants that name a ROLE(...) operand without MEMBERS, seeded grants that carry MEMBERS, and an import count — not the resolver. Nothing forces a holder row to end either; the report is the control (D-071). All of that belongs to the authorization model and is named here only because these were this module's workflows: 06-authorization-model.md.

    Three consequences, stated plainly.

    • "The leader of the Thursday group" has no referent. A group has no leadership and no roles (§2), so the sentence means a person on that roster who also holds some office, and nothing links the two. The office is ROLE(leaders) and means what its name says.
    • Elected versus appointed is not stored data. It survives only as roles the organization invents (elected_council beside appointed_it) — or not at all.
    • A vacancy is not a reportable state. "This seat has been empty since March" needs a slot that exists unfilled. An empty office is the absence of rows in role_holder, and absence has no start date on the office. A rule-filled role stays refused: F-12's answer is no, role_holder names one person, because an office record that cannot answer "who was treasurer in 2023" is not an office record (F-12).

    7. The module and the permission system, in one table¶

    Stated once here so that nothing below the line is re-specified in this page's voice. Full detail: 06-authorization-model.md and ../data-model/05-authorization.md.

    Question Answer Owner
    Where is authority held? at the unit, or on exactly one item — nothing in between D-097, D-099
    What is a group to the resolver? an item in grant_scope_type, and a GROUP(x) leaf in an expression D-098, D-101
    Does being in a group grant anything? no. A set is not a grant D-005, D-098, D-102 for ORGANIZERS
    What may a role do? read the live grants whose expression names ROLE(x) — a role confers nothing D-100
    How is precedence decided? allowed iff some live allow applies and no live deny applies. There is no level and no locality D-099
    Does a unit-scope grant reach an item? yes, every item of every type. A grant on one item reaches that item only D-099, D-102
    Is there a membership gate in the resolver? no. MEMBERS is an operand, evaluated live on the checked date D-100
    Can the center be locked out of a body's data? the question has no subject: there is one body and its people are the only people there are D-097

    There is no ancestor walk, because there is no tree: no unit_ancestors(), no unit_descendants(), no path-consistency check. The permission check matches scope exactly — the unit, or the item.

    8. Open issues¶

    Open, and tracked in ../open-questions.md:

    • Where a claimed group continuity is recorded. The method says continuity is asserted, never matched (§4); no table accepts the assertion. Until somebody rules, the default is the whole rule: a returning name is a new group.
    • Whether a group's previous name should be kept at all. It is not, deliberately, as part of "no special features". If the organization turns out to rename groups often, the answer is a decision and not a column someone adds quietly.
    • Whether an ended group should be hidden from pickers. A person_group with valid_to set has no stated behaviour either way yet.
    • F-10, which layer writes the no-overlap invariant on member rows. It is named in ../data-model/02-organization.md §2 and remains unanswered.

    Decisions behind this page: D-002, D-005, D-016, D-043, D-081, D-083, D-086, D-088, D-092, D-096, D-097, D-098, D-099, D-100, D-101, D-102, D-103, D-105, D-071.
