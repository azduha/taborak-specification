    Data Model — Relationships¶

    guardianship, and the single right level that travels with it.

    Domain rationale: ../domain/02-actors.md.

    This document describes requirements. It contains no DDL (D-072); the ORM decides how each rule is enforced.

    1. guardianship¶

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id internal identifier Never appears in a URL or an external reference
    guardian_person_id reference to person, required One side of the relationship
    dependent_person_id reference to person, required The other side. Must not equal the guardian
    relationship enum: parent / legal_guardian / foster_parent / step_parent / grandparent / sibling / other, required sibling and other exist because the organization will meet cases that do not fit, and forcing them into parent corrupts the data. A 19-year-old legal guardian of a 12-year-old sibling is real
    right_level enum: none / view / write The whole of the guardian's authority (D-074). Current value, denormalised for the read that happens on every authorization check; history in guardianship_permission (§2)
    valid_from date, required
    valid_to date, optional NULL = current. Set by a person — a party to the row (§1 "Guardianship ends"), or a staff member asked to record it. Never by a job: the system takes no position on majority (D-069)
    is_primary_contact boolean, required, default false Who the organization calls in an emergency. Independent of right_level — a grandparent with no system access at all can still be the emergency contact. See ../domain/02-actors.md §6
    is_legal_decision_maker boolean, required, default false Distinguishes who may make legal decisions — consent for medical treatment, travel — from who provides day-to-day care. Often different people
    lives_with boolean, optional Deliberately optional: false means guardianship without cohabitation (shared custody, a guardian in another city), which is a different fact from unknown. Relevant to contact routing, never to authority
    custody_arrangement free text A description, not a computable fact, and kept that way on purpose (§7)
    source enum: self / staff / import / court_order Decides the starting right_level (§2 defaults, D-046)
    verified_at timestamp, optional
    verified_by reference to person, optional
    evidence_reference free text Where the proof of guardianship is filed. For legal guardianship this matters: the organization must be able to show that it checked
    ended_reason_code free text / reason code
    ended_note free text
    created_at, updated_at timestamps
    created_by, updated_by reference to person
    import_batch_id reference to import batch Legacy rows only

    Rules

    • A person may not be a guardian of themselves: the two person references must differ.
    • valid_to, when present, must be after valid_from.
    • At most one current guardianship per (guardian, dependent) pair. Their periods may not overlap. Past ones are unlimited and expected: a court order lapses and is renewed.
    • A dependent may have any number of simultaneous guardians, each with its own row and its own right_level.
    • is_primary_contact is not unique per dependent. A child can have two primary contacts.

    Indexes

    • Lookups in both directions by person and valid_to — "who is this person's guardian" and "who does this person guard" are equally common.
    • A partial index on current rows only, and a second on current rows that are primary contacts: the emergency-contact lookup must not scan history.

    The relation is a general graph, not a two-level hierarchy¶

    The requirements permit:

    • One guardian → many dependents. Multiple rows with the same guardian. This is "one parent can manage multiple children".
    • Many guardians → one dependent. Both parents, brothers' grandparents, a legal guardian plus a step-parent. Nothing assumes a child has exactly two guardians.
    • A person being both. Person A is guardian of B and dependent of C. Costs nothing to allow, and avoids a rewrite when a member reaches majority and becomes guardian to a younger sibling.

    You specified the first two explicitly. The third is free.

    Per-dependent authority¶

    Authority is per guardianship row, never per person. A parent can hold write for one child and view for another — a realistic shared-custody arrangement, and one the model must represent without inventing a second parent identity.

    What changed with D-074 is the grain, not the location. Per row it used to be a set of capability codes; per row it is now one of three values. See §2 for what that costs.

    Guardianship ends — and who is allowed to end it¶

    valid_to is set when:

    • a party to the row ends it. The guardian, or the adult who is the subject of it (D-073);
    • a court order changes the arrangement, which staff record;
    • the guardian dies — and note the organization does not record death (D-034), so this too arrives as a person ending a row, with whatever reason the organization uses.

    Staff may always do it when asked. Staff may never be the only way it happens.

    The parties are principals, not third parties with a grant. A guardianship row is not the guardian's record, and it is not the subject's record. It is a record of a relationship between them, and both hold it.

    This claim used to borrow its justification from a rule that no longer exists. It was written as scope authority — ~~the rule that authority over a nested thing originates at that thing~~ (D-031), "applied to a household instead of a unit" — which was a good analogy right up until D-097 deleted the nesting it was borrowed from, along with the crossing mechanism that made it a rule rather than a sentiment. The claim does not need the borrowing. A relationship row belongs to the people in it is a statement about this channel and stands on its own, and it is the reason neither party needs a grant to act on the row. What the deleted rule was really doing on this page was answering who may end it, and that answer is already given locally, a paragraph above: a party to the row, or staff acting on a request — with staff never the only way it can happen.

    It is not the ownership channel, and the distinction is worth keeping. Ownership is your own record. Ending a guardianship over yourself is not editing your own record; it is dissolving a relationship you are one half of. Mechanically this is the guardianship channel gaining a direction: it already asks "may guardian G act on D's records?", and it now also answers "may a party act on the guardianship row itself?", for exactly three acts — ending it, adding another guardian, and changing the right level. The resolver still has four channels (D-010, and the channels' dispatch changed in D-079).

    The three acts a party may take, and no others:

    Act Guardian Subject (adult) Staff
    End the row ✅ ✅ ✅
    Add another guardian over the same dependent ✅ ❌ ✅
    Change right_level downward ✅ ✅ ✅
    Change right_level upward ✅ ❌ ✅
    Edit the dependent's records per right_level own record per role

    Read the last row against the first four, because an implementation will get this backwards. The four acts above are not gated by right_level: a guardian whose right is view, or even none, may still end the guardianship, and a person who has never been a guardian at all may not. The level ranks what you may do to the child's records, which is the fifth row and nothing else — it is not a general competence over the relationship, and reading it as one would mean that lowering someone's access to a medical file also locked them out of leaving the arrangement. The party direction follows the same rule as the record direction: it can only say yes (D-079). Being a party grants the act; not being one leaves the actor's own guardianship.* role permissions to be tried, which is how staff in the table above get their ✅s. An explicit deny still runs first and beats both.

    The asymmetry, stated plainly. A guardian may appoint a guardian: anyone with authority over a child can give a second person that same authority with no approval from the organization. The ordinary case is a mother adding the father. The design does not prevent the non-ordinary case; it attributes it instead — who added whom, when, from which account, is audited, and the other party is notified. That is a deterrent and an evidence trail, not a wall. See E-6.

    The system does not notice birthdays in this connection. No job, no review queue, no computed candidate date (D-069, reversing D-024 and closing E-2).

    Why not, including why the gentler version was also refused. The legal reasoning is unchanged and still correct: in many jurisdictions a parent's authority over health and school records does not end at 18, and in some it ends at a different age, so any computed date is a claim the system cannot support. What D-069 adds is that a review queue makes the same claim more quietly. A queue asserts this arrangement is now suspect, in every case, for every family, including the ones where the adult child actively wants their parent to keep handling the paperwork. Deferring a decision is not declining to make one, and the queue was a decision.

    D-069 refused, D-073 supplies. Read as one decision in two halves: a legal relationship between two people ends when one of those people says so, and the organization's job is to be told rather than to permit it. Without D-073, D-069 was a refusal with no alternative; the exit it assumed had no door.

    What is lost, precisely: a prompt, not a record. valid_from/valid_to mean "who is listed as my guardian, and since when?" is a query with a complete answer including the date each ended. What replaces the prompt is the report in §6, run by a person on purpose.

    is_primary_contact vs is_legal_decision_maker¶

    Two distinct questions that a single "is primary parent" flag conflates:

    Question Column
    Who do we phone at 2am when the child is injured at camp? is_primary_contact
    Who may sign the consent form for that treatment? is_legal_decision_maker

    They are often different people, and conflating them means either the wrong person is called or an invalid consent is accepted. Both are separate flags rather than one is_primary, because a child can have two primary contacts and one decision maker.

    2. The guardian's right: none, view, or write¶

    A guardian either cannot see the child's records, can see them, or can change them. That is the entire vocabulary (D-074).

    guardianship_permission survives as the history of that value, not as a capability list. One row per change, exactly one row open at a time, so "what was this guardian allowed to see in March 2024?" stays answerable — the same current-value-plus-temporal-history shape that person uses for names.

    Field Kind Notes
    guardianship_id reference to guardianship, required Scoped to the row, never to the (guardian, dependent) pair
    right_level enum: none / view / write, required The value being recorded
    valid_from date, required
    valid_to date, optional NULL = current. Ending this row without ending the guardianship changes the right; a new row starts it again
    changed_reason free text e.g. default for self-registered guardianship, court order 2026-03-14, guardian request
    changed_by reference to person, optional NULL when the default was applied by the application at creation

    Rules

    • Exactly one row per guardianship may be open at a time.
    • Every change is a row; the value on guardianship.right_level is always the open row's value. If the two disagree, the row is authoritative and the copy is wrong.
    • Scoping to the guardianship row and not to the person pair is deliberate (D-020): when a guardianship ends and a new one begins — a renewed court order, a lapse and re-establishment — the rights must not silently carry over. A new relationship is a new row and a new, deliberate level.

    Three values, two kinds, and no scale¶

    This section used to argue that the guardian rights were not a second authorization model by mapping them onto the shared level ladder — none → 0, view → 10, write → 20, so the resolver would never need to know which ladder it was standing on. The ladder is deleted (D-099), so that argument is gone and the requirement underneath it is still met, by a different mechanism: the level is compared against a column on the permission, permission.right_kind ∈ read | write — none covers nothing, view covers the codes marked read, write covers both. There is nothing to order and no rank to keep in step, because three values against two kinds is a lookup, not a scale. The column exists for exactly this comparison and for no other customer in the system (05-authorization.md §1).

    What the ladder was really buying survives, and it never was the numbers. A guardian was never a staff member, and that was expressed by write landing on contribute rather than manage. It is now expressed by the shape of the request: the guardianship channel is entered only when the check is about somebody other than the actor (D-079), so the widest guardian right in the system reaches the child's record and cannot reach a roster, a fee schedule or another family — not because its rank was too low, but because those checks are not about the child and the channel is never asked. Losing the ladder costs the comparison; the boundary moved into the one place that was always enforcing it.

    What the collapse gives up, written down rather than discovered later¶

    Before D-074, a guardianship carried a set of capability codes — dependent.profile.view, dependent.medical.view, dependent.consent.give, dependent.fee.pay, and thirteen others, with dependent.admin as a shorthand for all of them. Every dependent.* code is deleted.

    The consequence is specific and should not be softened: it is no longer expressible that this guardian may register the child for events but may not read the medical record. The case the old model existed for — a court order restricting one parent's access to medical information while their other access continues — cannot be represented. A grandmother who was granted roster visibility and nothing else is now a view guardian, which means she can see the fee history and the medical file too.

    What still holds:

    • write is not manage, and since D-099 there is no manage to be short of: the ceiling on a guardian is the channel, which answers only for checks about this child. A guardian cannot do organization-wide things because those checks are not about the child, not because a rank was too low.
    • Authority is per row, so a guardian of one child sees nothing of another's.
    • The organization-wide deny channel is untouched: a guardianship row granting write can still be refused by a deny, because deny is absolute and scope-independent (D-032). What was lost is a narrow deny — "not medical" — not the ability to say no.

    If the split turns out to matter in operation it is a new decision, not a permission to be re-added quietly (E-7).

    Defaults still depend on how the guardianship arose¶

    D-046 survives, with one value where it used to have a set:

    source Starting right_level Why
    self — a parent registering their own child write They created the record and are asserting the relationship. There is nobody better placed to hold authority over it
    staff, import — recorded by someone else view, chosen at creation and raisable A person who did not assert the relationship is not handed authority by a default
    court_order view, and raised only by the person who filed the order The document says what the authority is; the system should not guess above it

    Applied by the code that creates the row, at creation — and this is the correct layer, not a compromise. A default depends on why the row is being created, which is a fact about the user's intent that no database rule can inspect. What must not happen is the default being applied only by a form: an import that omits it produces a view guardian where the person meant full rights, and nothing objects. And nothing in §6 catches this either, which is worth being plain about: a default that was never applied leaves no inconsistent row behind — only a quiet view where a write was intended, indistinguishable from a deliberate restriction. That gap is a consequence of §2's collapse. Before D-074 an omitted bundle of grants was visible as an empty set, and an empty set is reportable where view is not. What closes it is not a report but the shape of the write path: one place creates guardianships, it reads source, and it does not accept a right_level from a caller who is not entitled to choose one. The staff and court_order paths need a human choosing a value, and a default that fires before anybody is asked is not a default, it is a decision made on someone's behalf — the same refusal D-069 and D-076 make elsewhere.

    Why source and changed_reason are both kept. The same word — "guardian" — now means two different starting scopes depending on paperwork, and the record has to say which was chosen and why. A guardian with read-only access and a guardian with full access are indistinguishable in the domain language and must not be in the data.

    3. There is no household table¶

    Removed (D-057). An address is a person_contact row of type postal (01-identity.md §6), which already exists, already keeps history (valid_from / valid_to), and already carries a purpose — 'billing' for the address an invoice goes to. A separate household table was duplicating it under a different key, and the duplication had gone stale: its postal_address column was documented as denormalised from the billing contact's postal contact, with nothing keeping the copy true.

    Shared address still grants no access¶

    Living at the same address does not grant any permission over another person at that address. A stepparent with no guardianship row has no access to the children's records. A grandparent at the address has no access. This is deliberate and it is a safeguarding property, not an oversight.

    This was true when household existed (D-019) and it is true now, for a simple reason worth stating so nobody re-derives it: the access never came from the household tables. All authority over a child's records comes from guardianship (§1–2). Removing a table that granted nothing cannot grant anything.

    What replaced it¶

    Household feature Now
    Billing address person_contact of type postal with purpose billing
    "Which children lived at this address in 2003?" A query over the temporal person_contact history (domain/03 §4)
    One invoice for three siblings The fee is flat and per person (D-037); there is no family price to invoice
    fee_obligation.household_id Column and its index removed (07-finance.md §2)

    What was genuinely lost, recorded rather than glossed: a household was a group with a name and its own lifetime. Two people at one address are now related by a query, not by a row, so there is nowhere to hang "the Novák family" as a first-class thing and no valid_from for the grouping itself. Accepted, because nothing was using it — no code set fee_obligation.household_id, and household.name had no consumer. If family-level communication or a family discount is ever wanted, this is the decision to revisit, and the shape to bring back is a group with a period, not a fake person.

    Why not a family person row¶

    Some systems model a household as a fake person so that memberships and payments can attach to it. Do not. It creates rows in person that are not people, which breaks identity matching, produces nonsense in name searches, and — because it is a person — can receive a membership, an account, and eventually permissions.

    4. How the resolver uses these tables¶

    The guardianship channel is step 4 of the resolver (../domain/06-authorization-model.md).

    How the channel is selected — a change D-074 forces, and it is an improvement. It used to be selected by the permission code's namespace: a code beginning dependent. routed here, and the point of routing by prefix was that no caller gets to choose which channel applies, which is what stops one authorization rule being implemented twice, once wrong. With no dependent.* codes there is no prefix, so the channel is now selected by a fact about the request: the person the action is about is not the person performing it. Nobody picks a channel; the shape of the call picks it. And it catches what the prefix missed — a parent editing their child's address under a person.* code previously did not route through guardianship at all, because the code's name said otherwise.

    Asked about another person, it resolves in this fixed order, and it produces one of two outcomes: an allow, or no answer at all. It never answers "no".

    1. Is there a current guardianship row between this actor and this subject, at the date being asked about? If not, this step is not the channel's business and says nothing — the resolver continues to the actor's own roles and grants. Silence, not refusal (D-079).
    2. Is there an explicit deny covering this check? Deny wins here as everywhere else, and independently of how broad the underlying grant is (D-032) — but note that this deny is found by channel 0 of the resolver, before this step runs, so nothing in this list can override it. It is listed second only because it is the other reason a guardian with a sufficient right_level still gets nothing.
    3. Read the row's right_level and rank it: none 0, view 10, write 20.
    4. Compare against what the action needs. Reading a profile needs 10; editing a contact needs
    5. At or above the requirement → allow. Below it, or none → no answer, and the actor's own roles are tried.

    Why step 1 and step 4 fall through instead of refusing, when refusing is what this channel used to do. It used to be entered only by codes no role could hold, so its refusal was the whole answer. Now it is entered by ordinary codes that a role may also carry, and finality produces an absurd result the moment one person wears two hats: the parent who is also the event registrar, holding view over their own child, refused the right to mark attendance. The relationship should not be able to revoke a job. What is not given up: none still grants nothing (that is what D-074 promised, not a power to block other bases), and an organization that genuinely must stop a specific person touching a specific child's records uses an explicit deny, which runs first, everywhere, at any scope. The prohibition survives; it simply lives in the channel D-032 built for it rather than in a capability level.

    Two further properties:

    • An ended guardianship never opens this channel again. For every question, forever, the row is not current, so step 1 does not fire and no guardian authority flows. What a former guardian may still do is what any other member may do — through their own assignment, on its own scope, with the guardianship row doing nothing at all. That distinction did not exist while the channel was the only road to a child's records, and it is the honest version now.
    • The date is a parameter of the question, not read from the clock. So "could this parent have seen this record on the day they did it?" stays answerable, which is what auditing a past action requires. As-of must be threaded into every step above, including the deny lookup — a check that reads today's denies against a 2024 action invents history.

    Asked about the guardianship row itself rather than through it, the same channel answers for a party to it, and only for the three acts in §1's table. That branch is not a fifth channel; it is this one, pointed the other way.

    5. Acting on behalf — recording it¶

    Every operation performed for a dependent must record both parties. This is not optional: without it, the audit trail attributes the parent's actions to the child.

    Every access-log row for an act-on-behalf carries, together:

    Field Records
    actor_person_id the guardian who acted
    subject_person_id the child whose record was affected
    on_behalf true, explicitly — not inferred from the two ids differing
    authorization_channel guardianship
    guardianship_id which row authorized it
    result allowed or refused

    Concretely, when a parent registers their child for an event, the record must answer:

    • Who did it? The parent.
    • For whom? The child.
    • By what authority? A specific guardianship row, and the right_level it held at the time.
    • Is that authority still valid? Re-checkable from the row's dates.

    A row that records only "the child registered" is not merely incomplete — it is wrong, and it is the kind of wrong that matters when someone later asks whether a parent consented to something.

    6. Invariants to verify¶

    These were executable queries. They are now requirements stated in prose, and nothing runs them (D-072).

    1. Adults still listed under a guardian. Every open guardianship whose subject is past majority by person.birth_date. Expected in the ordinary case and not an error — this is the periodic human review D-069 names as the entire control, and it exists precisely because no job acts on that date. Run it deliberately, never nightly: a report nobody reads is a queue, and the queue was withdrawn.
    2. Rights outliving the relationship. A guardianship_permission history row still open while its guardianship has ended. This is the exact failure that scoping to the row was meant to prevent; worth verifying because an application bug or a hand-edit can still create it.
    3. A dependent with no primary contact. A current guardianship set in which nobody is flagged as the emergency contact.
    4. A minor member or participant whom no guardian can see. Two shapes, one report, because they are the same gap arriving by different routes: no guardianship row at all, and a full set of guardians with every one of them at right_level = none. Age comes from person.birth_date — there is no membership category to read it from, because membership is ageless (D-038). This is a safeguarding gap, not a data-quality nit: the organization holds a member it cannot contact in an emergency and cannot obtain consent from. It should alert, not log. This check is one of the reasons guardianship is a required part of the model rather than an optional convenience.

    The second half was added by D-082, and it is not a hypothetical refinement. A direct message to a minor is mirrored to the guardians at view or write — deliberately not to a guardian at none, because none means the organization already decided that party sees nothing about the child. Without this half of the check, a child whose guardians are all none would look, in every report, like a child with guardians, while in fact nobody outside the child receives a copy of anything sent to them. A right level that silently removes the only safeguarding net is a right level nobody understands anymore, and this report is what keeps none meaning what it says. 5. A guardian younger than the dependent, where both birth dates are known. Almost always a data-entry error, and detectable only when both dates survived the import. 6. guardianship.right_level disagrees with its own open history row. A new invariant created by D-074's current-value-plus-history shape. If it is ever non-empty, the copy is wrong and the row is right.

    Deleted with the model that needed it: "a guardian permission outside the dependent.* namespace" — there is no dependent.* namespace any more. "Households with no address" went with D-057; the nearest surviving signal is a live billing purpose with no postal address behind it, which is a finance problem.

    Note what changed when membership became ageless. Check 4 used to read membership_type.category = 'child'; with one membership type (D-037) that column does not exist, and the question is now answered from person.birth_date. The signal is arguably better than it was: "this person is under 18 today" rather than "this person's membership row was categorised as a child when it was opened", which could be years out of date.

    7. Open issues¶

    • ~~Whether guardianship should auto-expire at majority, or always require review.~~ Neither — the system takes no position on majority, and the review queue this file recommended was withdrawn with it (§1, D-069).
    • ~~How does an adult end a guardianship over themselves?~~ They end it, themselves, as a party to the row — D-073.
    • New: a guardian may appoint a guardian, with no approval from the organization. Attributed and notified, not prevented. E-6.
    • New: one right level cannot separate the medical record from the profile, which is the case the deleted capability codes existed for. E-7.
    • Resolved: household is removed entirely — answered "drop household entirely", now D-057 (§3). Addresses live on person_contact.
    • Whether a guardian may act for a dependent who is also a member in their own right — e.g. can a parent register their adult child for an event, if the child consents? Recommend an explicit consent-based delegation rather than a guardianship, since the legal basis differs. The group version of this is now answered by audiences (D-042).
    • Whether custody_arrangement free text should be structured. Recommend not — custody orders vary too much to model, and a wrong model is worse than honest prose.
    • Confirm that the membership gate's guardianship carve-out is intended. A lapsed parent keeps their right_level (D-039), which means an unpaid parent and a paid one are indistinguishable inside that namespace. It is almost certainly right — guardianship comes from a legal relationship, not a membership — but it should be a stated choice, not a side effect of where the gate was placed.

    Resolved and no longer open, kept visible so the reasoning survives: the default grant at guardianship creation (now one level, chosen by source — D-046); whether fees are per person or per household (per person, flat — D-037, which retired the load-bearing half of household and led to its removal — D-057); and background checks (none are recorded — D-043).
