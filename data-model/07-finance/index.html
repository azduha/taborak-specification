    Data Model — Fees and Payments¶

    ⚠ SETTLED ON ARCHITECTURE, AND NOW ON POLICY TOO¶

    The banner's own left half has been overtaken, and it is kept here saying so. This heading read PROVISIONAL ON POLICY, SETTLED ON ARCHITECTURE from the round that drew the money path ahead of the answers, and the provision it stood for was the list §5 kept. Round 24 answered the last item on that list, so the page is not provisional on policy any more — while what is genuinely still outside it is not a question a page can answer either: what the country requires of an invoice's contents, which this module refuses to encode (D-197), and who the issuer is, which is configuration (D-198). Cost of the retitle: a reader who arrives from a citation of the old banner finds a different heading, which is the trade every renamed heading is — accepted here because a banner that says provisional over a page with nothing open is worse, it invites the reader to discount rules that are now decisions.

    How money moves is now decided. Money arrives from a bank API and is requested by a variable symbol and a QR code (D-075); money that cannot be matched is matched by nothing — it has no request attached, it gets no subject assigned by a guess at an amount and a date, and it is not a queue anybody is watching (D-076, D-180, D-196); the sentence this header used to print here — that unmatched money "waits for a person" — is the picture D-180 deleted, and it survived that round sitting in this block quote, which is where a header goes wrong: §2b and §2a were swept and this summary of them was not. There is no card gateway, no checkout and no dispute handling, and §2a is the consequence.

    What the money is for is answered. The fee is priced in koruna (D-155), it buys membership and nothing else (D-156), there is one live price edited in place, and the number on an obligation is the number a person wrote there (D-161, D-160). Nobody waives a fee and nothing refunds one inside the system (D-157, D-158).

    No question is left open on this page. §5 is where this document kept its open issues and the last of them were answered in round 24: what money arriving under or over its ask settles is the symbol and nothing else (D-196), invoices are compulsory so nobody issues one (D-197), who the invoice is from is configuration and the payer's identification is already modelled (D-198), and nothing leaves this system to an accounting system (D-199). What is not this module's to decide shrank in round 25. The money codes are shipped codes (D-195), and this module has never spent a code of reporting's: the six codes named in §5 are the whole of what money answers to, and a listing over them has never been one of them. Round 25 took the question of reporting's shape away rather than answering it — there is no report screen in this design and no reporting code, and what an organization calls a report over this module's money is a message of a type addressed to an audience. The over-or-under payment and the late fee are the two cases D-210 names as now reaching people instead of waiting for them (10 §8). §5 says what the six codes are.

    This document exists because membership activation depends on payment (../domain/05-membership-lifecycle.md), and because the yearly expiry and renewal window (D-040) need somewhere to read a due date from.

    1. What the domain requires¶

    Settled:

    • There is a yearly fee, flat, per person — it does not vary by age, by household, or by membership type, because there is one membership type (D-037).
    • Becoming a member requires paying it; the membership activates on payment (D-040).
    • The fee expires on a fixed organizational date, typically January. At that date the membership lapses — the person is not a member — and a renewal window follows during which they may renew without re-applying. A payment inside the window opens the new period at the expiry date, so the record shows no gap.
    • There is no grace status. The window is tracked as dates, and the resolver does not know it exists.
    • The fee is charged in koruna. One currency, and a second one is refused rather than deferred; the column stays because the refusal is cheap to reverse with it (D-155).
    • The fee buys membership — no insurance is bought with it, sold with it, or implied by it, so no date in this system is evidence of cover (D-156).
    • There is one live price, edited in place when a billing year is reopened, because what a person owed was never stored in the price list anyway (§2) (D-161).
    • What a person owes is whatever their obligation says. No proration rule and no waiver mechanism decide it: a reduced fee is an obligation written with a smaller amount_due, the same act that writes any other amount (D-160, D-157).
    • Money out is recorded exactly like money in — a refund is a payment in the opposite direction, and nobody initiates one inside this system (D-158).
    • What a person was asked to pay is never edited. A changed amount voids the live request and issues a new one under a new variable symbol, because the old figure is already printed on a QR code in somebody's hand; the liability underneath is edited in place, and no column holds a reduction — amount_discount is gone, and the balance lost its term with it (D-183).
    • Money is matched by its variable symbol and by nothing else. A bank row whose symbol resolves to a live request is attached in full, and the amount a payer actually sent is information rather than a condition — under or over, it is shown and never adjudicated (§2a, §2b, D-196). This is a trade and not a cleanup: the family that pays one amount against one child's symbol now has all of it land on that child, and §2b says so rather than softening it.
    • Every ask has an invoice over it, and nobody is asked whether it should. The country this organization is in requires the document, so the invoice row is written by the same write that creates its payment_request and there is no act of issuing left for a permission to authorize — invoice.issue is deleted (§2, D-197). The organization's half of the document's header is deployment configuration, the way the SMTP relay and the bank's credentials are, because there is no unit row and no organization table to hold it (D-097, D-198). A payment_request is still not an invoice and does not become one — D-187's renaming never happened, only its premise about a payer asking for the document went (D-187).

    Answered, since this section last spoke — and the answers are the ones that cost something. Nothing is exported to an accounting system: no export, no feed, no periodic journal, and no placeholder waiting for one; an external accounting package may appear in a later version and will be designed then, in a document that is not this one (D-199). What the law of this country asks of a fee arrived as one fact rather than as a rulebook — the document is compulsory — and this module encodes no jurisdiction's rules: it produces a row whose number is never reused, and the thing that turns that row into a filing is outside the corpus (D-197, D-198). And what a payment that does not fit its ask settles is decided: it is attached, in full, and the difference is shown (D-196).

    Settled, and the big one — the organization collects, but not through a card gateway: money is requested by variable symbol + QR code and arrives from the bank API already tagged (§2a, D-075).

    2. Minimal provisional shape¶

    Seven tables, deliberately small and easy to replace. This sentence printed four for two rounds while six were defined underneath it, which is what a typed count does; invoice is the seventh and the only one added since (D-187).

    fee_schedule — a price. One is live at a time, and it is edited in place when a billing year is reopened; there is no price history, and none is needed, because what a person owed was never in here.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    code text, required
    name text, required
    description text, optional
    membership_type_id reference to membership type, optional NULL = applies to any type, e.g. an organization-wide surcharge
    period_months integer, required Default 12
    amount money, required Zero or more
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK, and the only value this organization writes. Required and explicit anyway: a second currency is refused for now, and the column is what makes that refusal cheap to reverse (D-155)
    is_active boolean, required Default true
    created_at timestamp, audit time
    created_by reference to person, optional

    Nothing on a schedule is scoped to a body. A fee_schedule is scoped to a membership type — one row, so nothing in practice — and to nothing else at all: it is the price, current until somebody writes a different one into the same row. A fee is what it has always been charged as: flat, per person, at the unit (D-037). Why nothing narrower: D-095 had already ruled that entry below the membership carries no money — into the sub-units, there is no fee — so a supplementary fee for one body was a possibility no answer ever took. If a group ever needs a charge of its own, that is a policy question for §5 and a new shape of obligation, not a column to hold in reserve.

    Rules

    • code is unique. It covers all rows, not only active ones.
    • amount is zero or more.

    Indexes

    • The one lookup that matters is the live schedule for one membership type: the type and is_active together, which are expected to return one row.

    One live price, and why editing it in place is safe. fee_obligation snapshots fee_schedule_id "so the amount is reproducible even if the schedule is edited", and it copies amount_due beside the reference — so the record of what a person owed was never in the schedule at all. Only the price list was, and a price list is not a record (D-161). What is given up is the price list's own history: nobody can ask this system what a membership cost in 2027. The answer available is an aggregate over the obligations of 2027, which reports what people were actually asked rather than what a list said. No code is named for editing the price row, and the gap is named rather than filled: on the reading this design carries, opening a billing year is reached by whatever grant already lets a person write a fee at that scope, which is this corpus's inference from D-161 and not a ruling inside it.

    fee_obligation — one thing someone owes.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    person_id reference to person, required The person the fee is for. Not the payer — see paid_by_person_id on payment
    membership_id reference to membership, optional One of the three target columns; exactly one is set
    membership_request_id reference to membership request, optional One of the three target columns
    event_registration_id reference to event registration, optional One of the three target columns. The event arm is real, not a hedge: an event with a fee_schedule creates one obligation per registration (D-066), and payment confirms the place.
    fee_schedule_id reference to fee schedule, required Snapshotted so the amount is reproducible even if the schedule is edited
    description text, required
    period_from date, required
    period_to date, required
    amount_due money, required The number this person owes, and it may be written again: editing it in place is how a fee is reduced, corrected, or negotiated, and nothing records that a reduction happened (D-183)
    amount_paid money, required Default 0
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK, as on the schedule it came from
    status text, required Default unpaid. One of: unpaid, partially_paid, paid — and nothing else. The three values say how much of this obligation has arrived, and nothing more: lateness is read from due_date, and a fee nobody is going to pay is a row with a smaller amount_due (D-159)
    due_date date, required The yearly fee expires on a fixed organizational date (D-040); this obligation's due_date is what the lapse sweep compares against (§4). The renewal window that follows is carried as dates, not as a status (§1). This is also the only place lateness lives: an obligation past this date and not paid is overdue, and no status value says so (D-159)
    created_at timestamp, audit time
    created_by reference to person, optional
    updated_at timestamp, audit time
    updated_by reference to person, optional

    Rules

    • period_to is later than period_from.
    • amount_due and amount_paid are each zero or more.
    • Exactly one of membership_id, membership_request_id and event_registration_id is set — one, not zero and not two. Every obligation is for exactly one thing: a membership period, a membership request, or one person's place at one event. The per-person choice for events is deliberate: the fee is flat and per person (D-037), and a per-family price would need the household grouping designed again first (D-057).
    • status is arithmetic, not a decision. It reports how much of amount_due has arrived, so there is no value to set for a fee that will not be collected and no value to set because due_date has passed. A fee nobody is going to pay is an obligation written with a smaller amount_due; being late is a report over a date (D-157, D-159).

    Indexes

    • One person's obligations, by status.
    • Obligations ordered by due_date, restricted to the rows still open — unpaid or partially_paid. Closed rows are not in it. Read down this index past today's date and it is the overdue list: one fact stored once, with no status value to keep in step with the calendar. Lateness is also one of the two money cases round 25 turned into a delivered message, and fee-overdue is the code the messaging module lists for it (10 §2): the people who chase a late fee are reached by a type whose audience an installation edits, not by opening this index (D-210), and which clock fires it is K-14. Whether it fires at all is that installation's edit — this design ships no rows at all beyond the first account (D-150). The index stays exactly as it was: a send does not replace the read it is computed from, and no row of this table records that either one happened.
    • Obligations carrying the event arm, and obligations carrying the membership arm — each restricted to the rows where that column is set.
    • Obligations within a period_from / period_to range.

    The event arm is the third target that is genuinely used. D-037 settled the fee as flat and per person, so an event carrying a fee_schedule has one obligation per registration, created by the code that places the registration and paid to confirm the place (D-066). Nothing creates it if that code is not written: no trigger writes it, and a trigger could not have been forgotten — the trade is stated in 00-conventions §6a. An event with a fee and no obligation is invisible until someone reconciles money against the roster.

    Exactly one, never zero and never two — and unlike most rules in these documents this one can still be stated declaratively, so a migration should state it. Three columns, exactly one set: count how many are present and require that count to be one, which is a condition on the row and nothing else. No subquery, no other table, and therefore emittable by any migration that can put a condition on a row at all — the case where the ORM argument simply does not apply. It is worth naming explicitly, because it is the counterexample to the general worry — most of what D-072 deleted genuinely cannot be restated declaratively (shape 3), and this is not one of them.

    If a fourth target is ever proposed, ask whether it is used, and expect the answer to be no. "At most two of three," with one arm kept against a hypothetical, is not the same domain as exactly one over three real targets, and the difference only shows up when somebody adds the fourth.

    If a family discount is ever wanted, the row is still the right shape — one obligation covering several people — but it now needs the grouping to be re-introduced first, as a group with a period rather than a fake person (04 §3).

    payment — money that moved, as one recorded act.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    public_id external identifier, required Unique. Appears in URLs and exports; id stays internal (00-conventions §3)
    amount money, required Never zero. A negative amount is money going out — a refund, a correction, or a reversal. This is the only way money leaves: there is no refund state anywhere for a row like this to point at, so a refund is written as one of these and the person who recorded it is in the row (D-158)
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK, on money going out exactly as on money coming in
    paid_at timestamp, required When the payment occurred, which may differ from when it was recorded — a bank transfer recorded a week later. Both matter
    recorded_at timestamp, audit time Default: now
    method text, required One of: bank_transfer, cash, voucher, credit_note, other. card and direct_debit removed — there is no card gateway and no mandate system (D-075)
    variable_symbol text, optional The symbol the money arrived under, copied verbatim from the bank statement. Set on every row the import created; the join back to payment_request. Never typed by hand
    reference text, optional Bank reference, transaction id, receipt number
    paid_by_person_id reference to person, optional Who actually paid. May differ from the person the fee is for — a parent paying for a child, a grandparent paying for a grandchild, a sponsor. This is the act-on-behalf record for money
    on_behalf_of reference to person, optional
    status text, required Default confirmed. confirmed or reversed, and nothing else. A bank transfer the bank has reported does not later fail, expire, or get reversed by a card network, so the two values describe whether we still stand behind the row. There is no pending and no failed: a promised transfer is not a state (D-075)
    confirmed_by reference to person, optional
    notes text, optional Free text beside the money. Erasure empties it and the row remains — the emptied shape, not the deleted one, so the skeleton D-088 keeps stays intact and only the remark on it goes (D-215, listed in 06b §4)
    created_at timestamp, audit time
    created_by reference to person, optional

    Rules

    • amount is never zero; zero is meaningless. Negative amounts are reversals.
    • public_id is unique.

    Indexes

    • A payer's payments, newest paid_at first.
    • Payments by date, newest first.
    • Lookup by reference, restricted to rows that have one.

    payment_allocation — which payments satisfy which obligations. It is many-to-many in both directions: one payment may cover several obligations, and one obligation may be paid in instalments.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    payment_id reference to payment, required
    fee_obligation_id reference to fee obligation, required
    amount money, required Never zero. May exceed the obligation's amount_due, and since D-196 it has a routine way to: the automatic attach writes one row carrying the whole transfer, and a 1 100 Kč transfer under a 550 Kč symbol allocates 1 100 to a 550 obligation. That row is not a breach and §4a says so
    allocated_at timestamp, audit time Default: now
    allocated_by reference to person, optional Empty on the row the automatic attach writes (§2a step 6 — the fetch is the actor, and an empty actor column is this system acting on its own, 00-conventions §7a); a person's name on every row a hand moved
    note text, optional

    Rules

    • A given payment is allocated to a given obligation at most once — one row per pair, over all rows.
    • amount is never zero.
    • The sums are not constrained, and one of them is now expected to be over. Nothing here requires an allocation to stop at amount_due, because the automatic half of §2a cannot know whether the payer meant the whole transfer for this child; whether the excess stays is a person's call made with payment.match, whose split outcome already exists for exactly this shape (§2b). The pair rule and the sign rule are row-local, which is why they live here and the sums live in a report (§4a, shape 3).

    Indexes

    • Allocations of one payment, and allocations of one obligation — both directions are asked.

    The allocation table is the part most likely to be right. A payment and an obligation are genuinely many-to-many, and any design that puts payment_id on the obligation (or vice versa) breaks the first time a parent pays for three children in one transfer. That is the common case in this organization, not an edge case. D-196 left the shape alone and changed who fills it: the automatic half now writes one row of its own, carrying everything, and the many-to-many is what a person uses afterwards to move the surplus to the sibling it was meant for. The shape is the reason that correction is a split rather than a rewrite.

    2a. How money actually arrives¶

    D-075. The system requests a payment and then reads one. It never touches the money.

    The flow, in order:

    1. A liability exists — a membership period, a membership request, a place at an event.
    2. The system issues a payment_request carrying a variable symbol and an amount, and writes the invoice over it in the same transaction — the document is compulsory here, so it arrives with the ask rather than later, and nobody is asked whether it should (D-197). Every payable gets a symbol — the yearly fee and a camp fee alike — because a liability with no symbol has money arriving against nothing and no list where that would show (D-181).
    3. The payer is shown the symbol, the amount, and a QR code that encodes all three.
    4. The payer's own bank moves the money. The system is not involved and learns nothing yet.
    5. A bank import reads the account statement and produces a bank_transaction for each incoming transfer, with the variable symbol still attached.
    6. The symbol resolves to a live request, and that is the whole test. The fetch creates a payment, links it, and writes one allocation carrying the whole amount: what arrives under its own symbol is attached in full, and the sum the payer happened to send is never a condition of a match and never becomes one — it is information, and it is shown (D-196). All three rows belong to the fetch's own actor, system:bank-pull, because resolving a symbol is not a human act (D-182), and the allocation's allocated_by is empty for the same reason: an empty actor column is this system acting on its own, and its name comes from the fixed list (00-conventions §7a). The obligation's balance falls by the whole amount, which is exactly what makes partially_paid reachable with nobody allocating anything, and what lets an obligation read over — both are §2b's trade.
    7. If the symbol resolves to nothing live, the transaction stays exactly as it arrived: unmatched, which is not a queue (§2b) (D-180). What "nothing live" covers is now the whole of the unmatched population — no symbol at all, a symbol no request carries, or a symbol whose request has been voided — because the amount came off that list (D-196).

    payment_request — the liability rendered payable.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    variable_symbol text, required Unique across all rows, forever, including cancelled and long-settled ones. Eleven digits from a sequence
    fee_obligation_id reference to fee obligation, required What is owed. The request never invents an amount of its own
    amount money, required Copied from the obligation at issue time, so a later price change cannot rewrite what someone was asked for
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK, copied alongside the amount for the same reason
    recipient_account text, required The account the QR code tells people to pay into
    issued_at timestamp, audit time
    expires_at date, optional When the request stops being payable — the close of a renewal window, or the day before an event
    voided_at timestamp, optional Set by a person, or by cancelling the event the request was for (§2b)
    voided_reason text, optional

    Rules

    • variable_symbol is unique over every row that ever existed, not over live rows. A symbol issued in 2019 must never resolve to a 2026 obligation, because bank statements get re-imported and late payments are the entire reason the renewal window exists.
    • The symbol comes from a sequence. It is never derived from a birth date, a membership id, a member number, or anything else a person can guess or work out. Anyone can type eleven digits into a payment form at their bank; a guessable symbol means a stranger's money lands in a child's record, and the damage is not cosmetic — it reads as a paid fee and an active membership that nobody earned, in the one part of this database that is kept with no end date (D-088). A fee row is the organization's own history from the moment it is written, not a draft somebody confirms later. That is also the ceiling on the damage: since the fee buys membership and nothing else (D-156), no date here can be read as anything more than that.
    • amount is a copy, not a live reference. What a person was asked to pay is a historical fact about the ask, and recomputing it from the current schedule would make every price change rewrite history.
    • One obligation may have several requests over time — a first request expires, or the ask changes, which is a void and a new symbol rather than an edit (D-183) — but at most one live request per obligation. amount_due on the obligation is edited in place; amount on a request never is, because the figure has already been shown to somebody standing at their own bank.
    • A request is not an invoice, and now has one over it every time. The request does not become the document: the two rows keep their own numbers and their own rows, and a request still has no tax identity and no delivery record. What changed is that the invoice is no longer something somebody produces later — it is written by the same write that created this request, in the same transaction, so there is never a moment in which a request exists and is missing its document (D-197). Nothing is still delivered, and nothing here claims to have delivered anything (D-164).

    invoice — the document every ask has. There is no state of awaiting one, because there is nobody who could be asked: the country this organization is in requires the row, so it is written with its request (D-197). Which write, since there is no code for it: fee.create writes the obligation and the request over it, and the invoice joins that same write — one transaction, one actor, one audit payload covering both rows — so this module still has no money row whose write is ungated. invoice.issue is deleted, and what replaced it is that write rather than a hole in the vocabulary: there is no screen offering a choice about producing a document, and a permission nobody can decline is not a permission.

    Where the header comes from, since no table holds it. The issuer's half — the organization's name, address, IČO, DIČ and bank account — is deployment configuration, arriving the way the SMTP relay and the bank's API credentials arrive and named nowhere in the schema. There is no unit row to hold them (D-097) and there is no organization table, and adding one in order to print a header would be D-177's refusal for login methods repeated: a value with one legal value, one installation, and no screen that writes it. Write that down, or an implementer will invent a settings table that this design has refused. The payer's half is already modelled and is rows: domain/02 makes person_identifier a (scheme, value) pair, unique among current identifiers, and a business payer's IČO and DIČ are schemes, not columns — the invoice reads rows that were designed, for a completely different reason, before this question was asked (D-198). The cost is stated rather than discovered: an installation with two legal entities cannot have them both, because the issuer is one set of configuration values and there is no row to choose between. That is D-097 made concrete for the first time — this design supports one organization, and it now says so at the one place where being two would have shown up in the data.

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    number text, required Unique, and never reused. Its own sequence, beside the variable symbol's and independent of it: a symbol resolves a payment to an ask, a number identifies a document to a tax authority, and nothing promises the two agree (D-187). That sequence is legally required now, not merely tidy: unique-and-never-reused was hygiene when a document was a courtesy and is a legal fact now that one is compulsory, which is the one clause of D-187's "no tax identity, no legally-required sequence, no delivery record" that changed meaning — the request still has no tax identity and no delivery record, and no delivery of a document is designed here — sending words became possible in round 25 and a tax document was not given a type (D-197, D-214)
    payment_request_id reference to payment request, required The ask it documents. Never a bare obligation, because the request is what the payer was shown
    amount money, required Copied from the request, for the same reason the request copies it from the obligation: an edit to the liability must not rewrite a document that has already been handed over (D-183)
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK
    issued_at timestamp, required The moment fee.create wrote this row and its request, read off that write. Not a second timestamp a person sets, because there is no second write
    issued_by reference to person, required The actor of the fee.create write that created this request — not a second hand, and not the payer, because there is no row for the asking. This is the loss D-197 names rather than hides: D-187 kept the issuing code apart from payment.match "so that the person who can settle a transfer is not automatically the person who can sign a tax document", and catalog B repeated it. That separation is gone. Whoever holds fee.create produces tax documents as a side effect of recording what somebody owes, and no code, no check and no report distinguishes the two acts. The control left is the one D-188 left standing everywhere else in this vocabulary: fee.create is rare because somebody granted it, once, in the open

    Rules

    • No state. No requested, no sent, no delivered — the sentence survives word for word, now for the stronger reason that there is no moment at which a document could be missing. D-187's argument is why the column was never drawn and is worth keeping: a column meaning somebody owes this person a document, with nobody assigned to it and nothing counting it, is the queue this module deleted wearing a different coat (D-180). Compulsory invoicing does not add that column and does not need one, because it deletes the debt — and what precedes the row is no longer a conversation with the payer but the same write that created the request, which is in the audit trail rather than in a column (D-197).
    • Nothing delivers the invoice to anybody, and the reason is now a missing row rather than a missing mechanism. Round 25 opened the messaging module, so this design can carry words to a person; what it has not been told to carry is a tax document. No message type is configured for an invoice and nobody has configured one (D-214 narrows D-164: a page may no longer write that nothing can be sent, and may still not write a send or a period nobody chose), so the document exists on a screen and is handed over by a person, or printed by one. Everything after this row is outside the system in the same way: getting the document to the payer, filing it, reporting it and reconciling it with an accountant are acts this system does not perform, does not record having performed, and holds no column claiming it performed (D-198). D-164 already forbade writing the first of those as if the system did it; D-198 forbids the schema implying it — and that half is untouched by the round that opened sending, because a type is a row an installation writes (10 §2) and no column of this table may hold a delivery.
    • A voided request keeps its invoice. Both rows are permanent — and this now fires on every reissue instead of almost never, because a changed ask is a void plus a new request (§2) and each reissue carries a document of its own. The reissued ask gets a new invoice with a fresh number from the same sequence and the old number stays retired, because a number a tax authority has seen cannot be handed to a second document (D-197). The ordinary cost of the automation belongs here: a request voided five minutes after it was written leaves a voided invoice behind forever, in a table that — unlike the audit trail — has no end date at all.
    • There is no report over the requests that have no invoice, because that set is empty by construction. D-187's line "Most obligations have no invoice at all" is deleted with the premise under it, and so is the report it left as the alternative to a status: there is nothing left to report. The sequence advances for documents nobody handed to anybody, and that is the price of writing the row automatically rather than a defect in it (D-197).
    • What this row holds is the record that a document exists and what it was for. What the document must contain beyond these columns is decided here and not awaited: the issuer's half of the header is configuration, the payer's address and identifiers are person_identifier rows, and any requirement to print more than this design holds is met outside it (D-198). No column is invented for the rest, and no review is pending behind the decision — D-205 retired the idea that anybody outside this design would check it, so what stands here is a decision of the person designing the system with its risk named.

    bank_transaction — one incoming transfer as the bank reported it.

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    bank_account text, required
    bank_reference text, required Unique per bank account. This is the idempotency key: the same statement imported twice must create nothing the second time
    arrived_at date, required The bank's date, not the import's — this is the date D-040's renewal window is measured against
    amount money, required
    variable_symbol text, optional As reported. Can be absent, wrong, or a stranger's
    payer_description text, optional Whatever the bank gives: a name, sometimes an address
    matched_payment_request_id reference to payment request, optional NULL means unmatched: the absence of a match, not a queue and not a piece of work (D-180)
    imported_at timestamp, audit time When the fetch wrote the row. Which fetch is in that row's own actor column — system:bank-pull — and there is no batch to point at, because the batch table is gone (D-190)

    Rules

    • bank_reference is unique within a bank account. Re-importing a statement is safe and must be.
    • A transaction is immutable once imported. Corrections are new rows or a re-import, never an edit — this is the organization's copy of somebody else's ledger, and the only version of it that will ever agree with the bank.

    The renewal window stops being a promise. D-040 backdates a renewal to the expiry date when payment lands inside the window, and its own note said that rule was only as good as the reliability of payment dates. Until now they arrived when somebody noticed a transfer. They now arrive from the bank, dated by the bank. The sweep in §4 can run unattended because the thing it depends on is no longer a person remembering in February.

    What this deletes from the design. No checkout, no card tokens, no PCI surface, no webhook endpoint to authenticate, no dispute or chargeback state, no reconciliation report against a processor's payout. payment.status loses pending and failed: a bank transfer the bank has reported is confirmed on arrival, and there is no in-between state to model. What remains in status is whether we still stand behind the row — confirmed or reversed.

    2b. Money that cannot be matched¶

    D-076.

    An unmatched transaction is a fact with no subject, so the absence gets stored rather than guessed at — the same discipline as "unanswered is the absence of a row" (D-059). matched_payment_request_id IS NULL is the absence of a match and nothing besides — not a worklist, not a count, not a page somebody is expected to clear. There is no queue here (D-180): most money that lands in this account has nothing to do with a member at all — a grant, a donation, a shop's sponsorship — and an unmatched row is the ordinary shape of the statement rather than an exception to it. D-076's rule survives untouched: a row gets no subject assigned by a match on an amount and a date. What goes is the picture underneath the rule, that an unmatched row means a person is waiting somewhere behind it.

    Why a transaction has no match — and this list is shorter than it was, which is the whole content of D-196: there is no symbol at all — cash-basis donors and people who type their own name in the wrong field; the symbol matches no request; or the symbol matches a request that has been voided. The symbol and nothing else decides it. "The amount is less than asked, or more" was the largest entry on that list and is gone: 500 against a 550 ask and 1 100 against the same ask both match, and both attach in full. So is "the request is already settled," which used to sit beside it — a settled request that has not been voided is still a live request, so money arriving again under its symbol attaches and the obligation reads over. That row is not a failed match and not a breach: it is money that arrived twice for one liability, and a person either moves it or refunds it at the bank. What is left unmatched is therefore money whose symbol is missing, unknown, or dead — a narrower population than the one D-180 was written about, and not a queue.

    What the system does with any of those: it records the money, and since round 25 it can tell somebody. No page opens on it and nothing counts it. What changed is the second half of that sentence: money arriving under no symbol is one of the two money cases the messaging module hands to a type and an audience — "both are now a type with an audience", in 10 §8's own words, the other being the late fee below — where D-210 named this section by its other case, the over-or-under payment. So a notice about such a row is an ordinary message authored by system:messages under a type whose audience an installation edits (D-209), and an automated send is authored under that label rather than gated by a code a job would have to hold (10 §7). This module names no code and no type for it, and does not need one: the vocabulary is rows in message_type, not rows in permission, and which clock fires it is K-14 — a question this page is not allowed to answer by picking a night. What did not change is the absence. There is still no queue and no state: an unmatched row is not held somewhere waiting to be cleared (D-180), a send is an act with its own audit row rather than a condition a row is in, and if such a type's audience resolves to nobody, no row is parked anywhere — 10 §3 calls that case exactly this absence, shaped as words.

    When a person does decide to act, tying the transaction to a request is a write with a code of its own, payment.match, held at the organization's scope by whoever runs the accounting (D-182). There are exactly four outcomes they can choose between:

    Outcome When
    Attach it to a different request The symbol was mistyped; the real obligation is obvious from the payer
    Split it across requests One transfer, three siblings — the ordinary shape of family payment here
    Record as a donation Money with no obligation behind it, which is not the same as money that is missing one
    Refund it, by hand Nothing was meant to be owed. A second payment in the opposite direction (D-075): the transfer goes out at the bank, and what arrives here is a payment naming whoever recorded it (D-158)

    One of those outcomes is now used on a row that has a subject, and that is worth saying where the list is. A surplus D-196 creates is not an unmatched transaction — it is a matched payment sitting partly on an obligation that did not need all of it — and a person moves it with the same Split above, exercised through payment.match on a row that is already attached. Nothing was added for it: the allocation table is many-to-many in both directions exactly so that moving money from one obligation to another is a split and not a correction.

    Why the tempting shortcut is refused, and why the refusal now covers less ground than it used to. Falling back to the payer's identity when the symbol fails looks like it clears most of the unmatched rows. It clears most of their accuracy instead: a parent with two children pays one amount against one symbol is the normal case, not the edge case, and an identity fallback resolves it to whichever child the query reaches first. A fee recorded against the wrong child is a finance error and a membership that appears paid when it is not, both at once — and the sibling it was meant for stays the one who looks unpaid. Both halves sit in rows that are kept with no end date. A fallback is not allowed to be wrong on the common path, and this is the common path.

    The refusal stands and the mistake has moved, which is the trade and not a footnote to it. D-076 was refusing to resolve a failed symbol by guessing at a payer; here nothing is guessed, because the payer's own bank carried the symbol this organization printed. What changed is which error the system makes on its own. It no longer leaves a family's money with no subject — that was the old failure, and a slower one — it now puts all of it somewhere visible and wrong. The module's own normal case makes it concrete: one transfer of 1 100 Kč under one child's 550 Kč symbol, with a second child who also has an ask, lands entirely on the first child, who then reads +550 over while the sibling reads unpaid. No tolerance and no threshold is introduced anywhere to soften this — 4,90 against 5,00 is a shortfall like any other, because a threshold would be a number nobody can defend and everyone would have to remember. What catches it is a type and an audience from round 25, and not somebody happening to open the row: the over-or-under payment notice is one of the two money cases D-210 names as now reaching people instead of waiting for them, and 10 §8 says the same thing from the sending side. Which audience is nobody's to pick from here — it is a row an installation edits (D-209), and the type's name is not this module's to invent. What still holds is the absence: no list holds the row (D-180), and a delivered notice is an act with an audit row, not a worklist. What delivery does not buy is the hand: a message saying a sum arrived twice still leaves the surplus sitting on an obligation until a person holding payment.match moves it, so a person reading an obligation that says +550, or the family being asked, remains the moment anything is decided. Stating it as the decision states it: the design has traded a silence a treasurer notices in February for a number that reads like a success — and round 25 gave that number a voice without giving it a hand.

    Auto-refunding is refused for two reasons: it needs the bank integration to work outbound as well as inbound, which is a second integration with its own authorization; and it returns the grandparent's attempt at a camp contribution. The first of those has changed kind while the refusal stands. Calls coming in are a designed thing now — a registered key, granted like any other caller (05e) — so "outbound is a second integration with its own authorization" is no longer an absence but a decision with a shape to copy. The outbound direction is answered, not designed: an outbound call is either a named person acting or this system's own job under a label from the fixed list, and nothing about it is built yet (D-146, F-27). The second reason was never a mechanism, and stands alone. The one outbound direction this module was asked about has since been refused outright rather than left undesigned: nothing is exported to an accounting system, and an external one is a later version's design with its own document (§5, D-199).

    Cancellation is where most of the volume comes from, and it is decided now rather than later. D-064 made cancellation a recorded fact rather than a state, so the event survives, the registrations survive — and so would an outstanding request against an occasion that is not going to happen. So:

    • Cancelling an event voids its outstanding payment requests, recording why.
    • Money already received for a cancelled event stays where it is, tied to the obligation that has stopped meaning anything, for a person to refund by hand or carry forward to a later event. It is not auto-refunded, for the reasons above.

    This is EV-3's answer, which is why it is closed here rather than left parked in the events module. What has not changed since that answer was written is the seam: this is where the system stops and a person begins, and that person's next act is at the organization's bank, not on a screen in this design (D-158).

    No page counts what nobody is looking at, and that is a cost being paid on purpose. A transfer aimed at a camp whose request nobody ties anything to can sit in this table forever, and nothing anywhere distinguishes it from a grant payment nobody needed to classify — after (D-180) there is no list of rows that need looking at. What round 25 changed is that such a row can now be told about: 10 §8 hands money arriving under no symbol to a type and an audience, and D-214 is why this page may no longer write the flat sentence that nobody is told — a send if an installation configures one, and nothing at all if it does not, which is the same absence wearing a different coat rather than a page promising a notice. What no configuration buys is the worklist. The alternative refused was one whose first entry is always a sponsorship nobody wants to classify, and a worklist nobody clears is a lie about supervision. What makes the no-queue survivable is that the money is not lost, only unattributed, and every row is on the same screen in date order: the payer's belief and the organization's belief are reconciled by a person who was asked, not by a count. D-196 shrank the population this cost is paid over and moved part of it somewhere else. A camp transfer going unattributed forever still happens, but it now takes a symbol that is missing, unknown, or dead to get here at all. A family's part-payment no longer arrives here: it matched, it attached, and it is attributed — to one child, when the parents meant two. The absence this section polices got smaller, and the error it exists to catch moved into the rows that look settled.

    3. Keeping membership.outstanding_balance honest¶

    This heading names a column that no longer exists, and it stays anyway. membership.outstanding_balance is deleted (D-200), and the heading is the slug the invariant register, the register of assumptions (assumption 53) and two earlier rounds already resolve to — a renamed heading is a dead anchor that no build will report, so the column's name survives in a title and nowhere else. There is one definition of what is owed, it is the blockquote below, and this is the only place it is stated. Every screen, every gate and every API response — and a delivered message that quotes one (10 §8) — that shows a balance reads it, over fee_obligation and payment_allocation; 03-membership.md §3 shows it and does not store it. The view this section used to recommend is not created either — the definition is the view, and a second name for it is a second thing to get wrong.

    The recommendation this section has been making since round 6 has been taken, so what follows is a rule and not a menu. The table it used to print offered three options behind a sentence that said "two", and its recommendation — "drop the column and use a view" — named two of the three at once, which is what a table looks like when it has outlived its question. The option taken is the middle one, without the view: the column is gone and nothing replaces its name.

    What the column had going for it, because the honest answer is that it had one thing. Not accuracy. Its defence was that a membership screen wants a number without joining four tables, and that defence is answered the way it should have been answered when the column was drawn: a request memoizes, so the join is not the expensive thing the column was invented to avoid (D-114), at the scale this design states it runs at (D-112).

    What survives the deletion is the argument, and §4a is why it had to survive. A stored balance is a copy, and a copy is maintained by every path that writes an allocation or it drifts — the trigger that used to do it for all of them at once is gone (00-conventions §6a). A wrong number here has a human consequence: either a wrongly-suspended member or a wrongly-chased family. That sentence is why the column is deleted rather than deprecated, and it does not become false once the copy is gone: a definition implemented in several places can disagree across them just as a stored value can, which is precisely what §4a's first check now measures instead of comparing two copies.

    The definition, stated once, and read from here:

    balance = amount_due − the sum of allocations whose payment is confirmed

    The discount term left with the column it was named after (D-183): a reduced fee is a smaller amount_due, and a gap between what was asked and what is owed lives in the ask, not in a term beside it.

    Only confirmed payments count. An obligation is not settled by money that has been promised, intended, described in an email, or reported by a payer who believes they sent it. There is no state for a transfer that is on its way (D-075): either the bank has reported it and it is confirmed, or the system has not heard of it. A member is activated by the first, never by the second.

    "No obligations" is not "nothing owed", and that distinction did not die with the column. Assumption 53 recorded that an imported membership's balance is NULL and not zero, because a zero asserts the organization settled a debt it never saw, and D-093 made imported rows believe things. With no column the distinction does not vanish — it moves into the absence of rows. An imported membership has no fee_obligation underneath it, because the legacy corpus carries no money at all (D-090), so the definition above returns nothing for such a person — and reading that nothing as nothing is owed would be the same lie the zero would have been. It must keep reading as nothing is known in the places the NULL used to be read: in the overdue list §2's index already produces, wherever a balance is rolled up for display (which is what §4a's first check recomputes), and on any screen that would otherwise print 0 Kč — where the honest rendering is no fee record, not a number. The activation gate's answer for such a row is unchanged, and is written down here rather than inferred: it asks whether any attached obligation has a positive balance, an imported membership has none to check, so the test passes vacuously. That is correct for a gate — nobody is being suspended for a debt nobody computed — and it is the same fact that makes an obligation-less imported membership something a person looks at rather than something the model certifies; the load's own rows say which they are by carrying data_confidence = 'low', which names who wrote a row and not whether money is owed, so it is a pointer for a reader and never a rule anything computes. Deleting the column removed a number that could lie; it did not remove the absence underneath it, and this paragraph is the insurance that a later page does not delete the sentence along with the column (D-200).

    4. How this connects to membership¶

    The activation gate. A membership's fee is settled when no obligation attached to it has a positive balance, and that is the whole test with no status excepted from it — a fee nobody is going to collect is an obligation written with a smaller amount_due, so it never presents itself as a balance to work around (D-157, D-159). Two words went out of that sentence with amount_discount: "after discounts" subtracted a term that no longer exists, and a reduced ask is a smaller amount_due, so there is nothing left to subtract (D-183). The balance it reads is §3's definition and nothing else — the gate joins fee_obligation to payment_allocation when asked and stores no answer, because the column beside the membership it could have read is deleted (D-200). D-196 is what makes this test bite in both directions, and the gate did not move to accommodate it: a short payment settles nothing (500 Kč against a 550 Kč ask leaves a 50 Kč balance and the membership stays pending_payment), and a long one settles everything (1 100 Kč against that ask leaves no obligation with a positive balance, so the membership activates and the surplus is simply visible: a negative balance on the sibling obligation, to be moved by payment.match if anybody decides it should be moved, with nobody told it is there and no list counting it (D-180, D-164)). For a membership with no obligations attached at all the test passes vacuously — §3 is where that is stated and where the reason it is right for a gate is kept apart from the reason it is not a settled debt. This test used to exclude obligations carrying waived, written_off or refunded outright, and every one of those values is gone, so the exclusion would now exclude nothing — which is the way you see it was a repair and not a rule. "Settled" is the only thing money says about membership; it does not grant permissions by itself (D-005). The route from a settled fee to anything a person may do goes through a set: the fee makes is_current_member() true, is_current_member() is what MEMBERS evaluates, and only a grant whose expression names MEMBERS cares (D-100) — which is why money cannot become authority by itself.

    The yearly expiry sweep¶

    Run on 1 January (D-047). This is the one automatic membership transition in the system, and the only thing in this document that changes a person's status.

    For every membership that is active, has an obligation with a positive balance, and whose due date has passed:

    1. Mark it lapsed, ending it at the expiry date — not at today. The period ended on that date whether or not anyone noticed.
    2. Open the renewal window as a date, never as a status. The resolver does not know windows exist.

    Nothing has to be told afterwards, and this sweep tells nothing at all. MEMBERS is evaluated live on the checked date (D-100), so the two facts step 1 writes — the period ending and the lapsed status — are visible to the next check, with no job in between and no stored answer to invalidate (D-114, domain/06 §8). The second half of that lead sentence is round 25's. This job expires memberships and writes no message; a renewal notice is a message of its own type authored by system:messages, which makes the notice a separate authored act rather than a side effect of the transition, and which clock fires it is K-14 (D-212, and 03-membership §7's check 4, which says the same thing from the worklist side). What the sweep costs is stated where cost belongs: on 1 January the answer moves for every member at once, and a screen opened that day computes it rather than reading it (D-112).

    Note the personnel consequence, which is easy to miss and fatal to get wrong. This runs for everyone at once, including the person who runs it, and nothing waives the membership test for a named person: D-100 deleted system.gate_exempt, person_holds_gate_exempt and the resolver clause behind them. The answer is the grant text. A grant written MEMBERS * ROLE(it) stops applying to its holder at the sweep; a grant written ROLE(it) keeps applying, because the set test is simply not in it. Assumption: which offices are written that way is not settled by D-100, and getting it wrong fails in the direction that locks a person out with no permission left with which to undo it — the standing report of live grants naming a ROLE(...) operand without MEMBERS is what makes each such choice visible before 1 January rather than on it (03-membership.md §6).

    The expiry is the one automatic transition in this design, and what makes it allowable is that it is not a decision (D-023, D-040): the fee covers a fixed period and the period ends, and the renewal window absorbs the late-transfer case, because a payment inside it backdates the new period to the expiry date and the record shows no gap.

    What the window cannot absorb is a payment that arrived before the expiry but was recorded after it. That is a data-entry lag, not a non-payment, and it is corrected with membership.backdate rather than left as a real gap. Worth a report: members who lapsed and then paid within a few days.

    4a. Integrity checks¶

    Invariants to verify. This section arrived late and by decision (D-135); what it does is not new to this folder, it is what every other page here already had. The rules below are all stated somewhere above this heading — §2, §2a and §3 each state one and then said nothing about what would notice a breach.

    1. The balance the system shows, recomputed from §3's definition. amount_due − the sum of allocations whose payment is confirmed, per obligation, and the same sum rolled up wherever a membership's balance is displayed. Three states put a row on this list: an allocation whose payment has since been reversed still sitting in the total, an obligation whose amount_due was edited in place while a request issued at the old amount is still live — a screen and an ask that disagree is the state D-183 exists to prevent — and a roll-up summing a different set of allocations than the screen does. What it cannot catch is the definition being wrong on both sides — a screen and a report that each recompute amount_due − amount_paid agree perfectly and are both counting money that was only promised. D-200 deleted the column and this check did not retire with it — it changed job. There is no copy left to agree with the sum, so what is verified is that every place a balance appears implements §3's definition: the same query, the same failure list, and D-075's rule that only confirmed payments count is still what it checks. That is a harder check than the one it replaced, because a disagreement can now arise between any two of the readers rather than between one reader and a stored number, and the number of readers is not written down anywhere (D-200).
    2. A payment whose allocations do not account for its amount — and an unmatched payment is not on this list, on purpose. payment_allocation is many-to-many in both directions and §2's rules constrain the pair and the sign of each amount, never the sums, so a payment carrying no allocation and no matched request is money that moved and was never attributed to anything. Since D-196 this check has to say something it never had to say before: an unmatched payment has no allocations because that is what §2b designs. Counting those rows would report a designed absence as a breach, and it would be wrong about most of a real bank statement — grants, donations and sponsorships that were never a member's money (D-180). What the check reads is therefore a payment whose symbol resolved and whose allocations still sum to less than it, which under attach-in-full is a write-path failure and nothing else. And an obligation allocated beyond its amount_due is no longer a finding at all: the automatic attach writes one allocation carrying the whole transfer, so a 1 100 Kč transfer under a 550 Kč obligation allocates 1 100 to it by design (§2, §2a step 6). The check that used to call that "an overpayment that nobody has refunded" would now fire on the module's own happy path, so it is withdrawn here rather than left to be rediscovered as noise. The layer here is the write path, and there is no constraint to sit beneath it: the sums span rows of two tables, which is shape 3, so this report is not a net under a rule — it is the whole of the backstop. §2 called this table "the part most likely to be right", and that is an argument about the shape, not about the data ten years in.
    3. Money nobody ever asked for. Two readings of one failure, cheapest first: an obligation still at unpaid or partially_paid whose due_date has passed with no live payment_request pointing at it, so nothing has ever carried a variable symbol to anybody. The two conditions are read together and neither is stored twice: due_date past plus a status that is not paid is overdue, which is why no status value carries it (§2) (D-159); and upstream of that, an event_registration for an event carrying a fee_schedule with no fee_obligation behind it, which §2 already warned of in one sentence — an event with a fee and no obligation is invisible until someone reconciles money against the roster, because nothing creates the obligation and no trigger could have been forgotten. This is not about money that arrived and did not fit — that used to be a category, and D-196 deleted it: money either resolves to a live request or it has a symbol that is missing, unknown or dead, and nothing fails to fit any more. An unmatched bank_transaction is money with no subject, most of it never meant for a member at all, and the design keeps it that way on purpose (§2b, D-180). This is the other side of the same ledger: a liability with no ask attached. How silent it is is now a configuration question, and the two readings differ. An obligation past its due_date and unpaid is exactly what a lateness type is computed from, so if an installation configures one, the first half of this row reaches a person as a message and not as a check (10 §2). The second half has no date to be late about: an event_registration with no obligation behind it has no due_date, so no type can be computed from it and it stays invisible in both directions — nobody is chased and nobody pays — which is why it is this check's finding and not a message type's. It is the one failure on this list that no unmatched row would ever reveal, because with no queue there is nowhere the two could meet.

    No check here reads a negative balance, and that is a decision rather than a gap in the list. An obligation whose allocations run past its amount_due is a balance below zero — the +550 over §2b names as the thing a person is supposed to notice — and nothing in this section, and nothing in 05d §11, counts those rows. It stays that way for the same reason §2b refuses a worklist: an overpayment is not an inconsistency but a fact about a family's transfer, the surplus is moved by a person holding payment.match rather than by a report, and a count of rows that are legal by design is a list whose first entry is always somebody's correct data (D-180, D-164, narrowed but not repealed by D-214). So the answer to "what catches it" is §2b's and it belongs next to the absence: a delivered notice if an installation configures the type, and then a person reading an obligation that says +550 and deciding where the money goes. A notice is not a count and would not be a check here — it writes nothing in this table, its audience is an installation's row (D-209), and its clock is K-14 — so refusing the count below stands untouched. Writing the count would be a new check, and a new check is a decision this round did not take — it is named as missing here so that a reader looking for it finds the reason rather than the check.

    Checks 1 and 2 are the two that matter, and §3 says why in one line: a wrong number here has a human consequence — either a wrongly-suspended member or a wrongly-chased family. Check 3 is the one that finds a whole class of missing money rather than a wrong row, and it is the only one of the three that can be non-empty because a feature was never written rather than because a row is wrong.

    5. Open issues — the whole of this document, essentially¶

    Nothing is open on this page, and the bullets stay. Three questions were standing when this section last counted itself, and every one of them was answered in round 24; each keeps its place here with its verdict written into it, because a bullet deleted from an open-issues list reads to the next person as "still open, we forgot", which is the failure this round exists to close. Three more were answered while an earlier round was running — who may reduce a fee, whether the organization issues invoices, and what gets a variable symbol — and each is listed at the end of the page beside the decision that closed it. What this module used to leave unnamed at the bottom of this section is no longer a thing a module could own: reporting was never inherited here, it was deleted, and what arrives over this module's money is a type with an audience (D-210, 10 §8) — the paragraph about this module's codes below says what that leaves and what it costs.

    1. ✅ Closed — accounting integration: nothing leaves this system, and nothing is reserved for a system that might arrive (D-199). The bullet asked whether anything needs to be exported, and the answer is that this design owes a future accounting package nothing at all: no export, no feed, no periodic journal, no CSV of the day's bank rows, no external_reference column, no export status, and no actor label for accounting. Everything the money model asserts lives in this module's tables (§2) and is read out of them a person at a time, on the screens this module already has: there is no export path, and there is no code that gates a list either — reporting's codes went with the screen they gated, so neither the read nor the way out exists any more (D-210), and a person who wants this module's data out of it has to read it. The actor list grew by one in round 25 and the newcomer has nothing to do with accounting: system:messages joined it for automated sends (D-212), and 00-conventions §7a is still the whole list, so this bullet's refusal stands as it was written. That is the concrete shape of a deferral that costs nothing: an integration added later will need a label, and needing one someday was not a reason to reserve one now. An external accounting system may appear in a later version and will be designed then, in a document that is not this one — which is why this closes instead of staying filed: the question on the page was never will there ever be one, it was what does this design owe it now. The direction half of the old wording is still the reason it was ever worth asking: who may call this system is designed (05e), this system calling anyone else is answered and undesigned (D-146, F-27), and D-199 is that answer applied to the one outbound direction this module kept asking about.
    2. ✅ Closed — jurisdiction: one half was a fact about the country and the other half was a question about a header, and neither is a rule this module encodes (D-197, D-198). This is the half of an item that used to read "currency and jurisdiction". The currency half was answerable and was answered: koruna, on every table that carries one (§2, D-155) — and the count this bullet printed went stale the day the invoice table arrived, which is what happens to a number beside a sentence. Of the two clauses that were left, one stopped being a question: invoices are compulsory in this country, so §2's document row is written beside every ask and the code that used to gate it is deleted (D-197) — the module did not encode a rulebook, it was told one fact. The other half was refused at its premise: who the document is from is deployment configuration, because there has been no unit row since D-097 and there is no organization table to invent; the payer's IČO and DIČ are already person_identifier schemes; and delivery, filing, reporting and the accountant's reconciliation are acts this system does not perform and holds no column claiming it performed (D-198, D-164). What the module encodes of this country's law is therefore one column that is never reused, and it says so at §2. Whether the organization owes anything about its money to anybody outside itself is answered in the same breath: not by this schema. The sitting this bullet used to point at is gone. It closed by citing "the four data policies in front of a legal review" (00-conventions §8, 06b §6) — D-205 retired the review, so what stands behind every retention and paperwork choice in this module is a decision of the person designing the system with its risk named in the same paragraph, and no page here may say a lawful basis is outstanding. The costs are stated where they bite: two legal entities in one deployment is impossible, and a request voided minutes after it was written leaves a voided tax document behind forever.

    3. ✅ Closed — what a payment that does not fit settles: the condition was deleted rather than adjudicated (D-196). The item this sat inside asked three questions; two were answered in round 23 — every payable gets a symbol, and one live request per liability (D-181) — and the third, the case neither of them reached, was answered by removing the premise under it: the variable symbol is the whole match. A bank row whose symbol resolves to a live request is attached in full, the amount is never a condition of a match and never becomes one, and the difference is shown (§2a step 6). What that settled, in the order it hurts:

      • ~~500 sent against a 550 request stays unmatched~~ — it attaches. The symbol resolves, the whole 500 Kč lands, amount_paid reads 500, the status arithmetic reads partially_paid, and the balance is 50 Kč, with nobody having allocated anything (§2). No tolerance and no threshold was introduced to go with this: 4,90 against 5,00 is a shortfall like any other, because a threshold would be a number nobody can defend and everyone would have to remember. An obligation may equally be paid too much — the status still reads paid, there is no fourth value and no credit concept, and the surplus is an allocation a person moves with payment.match, whose split outcome already existed for exactly this shape. The membership gate did not move to accommodate any of it: a fee is settled when no attached obligation has a positive balance (§4), so a short payment settles nothing and a long one settles everything.
      • And nobody finds out unless an installation has configured a type that says so. Round 25 opened the messaging module, and D-210 names this exact case — the over-or-under payment — as one of the two money sentences that now reach people instead of waiting for them. This module names no type for it and no page of it may pick the audience; whether such a type exists at all, whom it reaches, and which clock fires it are configuration and K-14. What stands untouched is that nothing here holds the row: after D-180 there is no list of unmatched rows for a person to notice it on, and a send is an act with an audit row rather than a state a row waits in. So on the ground finds out still means somebody telephoning the payer because they were asked to, which is a procedure and not a design. What changed is what there is to find out. The rows that used to arrive here as "money that did not fit" now arrive matched and mis-attributed: one 1 100 Kč transfer under one child's 550 Kč symbol, with a sibling who also has an ask, lands entirely on the first child, who reads +550 over while the sibling reads unpaid, on the module's own normal path. §2b states that trade and does not soften it, and what catches it is a delivered notice where somebody configured one, and then a person reading an obligation that says +550 and deciding where the money goes — which is also why no check counts those rows (§4a).

      Why this is not a footnote, and what became of the fear: this was the one place the module could still end up with two money paths — the automatic one, and a hand-corrected one running beside it. It has both, by choice. §2b is the hand half and now it is narrower in one way and wider in another: a person no longer adjudicates money that failed to match, and instead moves money that arrived under the wrong child's symbol. The automatic half stopped being conditional on an amount, so the seam between the two paths is a person deciding a surplus belongs somewhere else — which is a decision somebody holds a code for, not a hole.

    What this section used to ask, and where each answer lives. The list below now runs to nine items. They are kept here in one line each, because for most of them this page is the only place the question was ever written:

    • The fee amount — the question this page asserted twice and never filed, which is why nothing could ever have closed it. Filed now as item 12 of the old numbering and answered in the same act: one live price, edited in place, and the amount a person owes is the number written on their obligation (D-161).
    • Is the fee prorated for mid-year joins — no rule. Someone joining in October gets an amount_due of whatever the person opening it writes, and the renewal twelve months later is written by the same kind of act. Nobody will be able to point at a rule and say this organization prorates, or refuses to (D-160).
    • Who may authorize a refund — nobody, because nothing is initiated here. The transfer goes out at the bank; what arrives here is a payment naming whoever recorded it (D-158).
    • Does the fee cover insurance — it does not. No date in this system is evidence of cover, so the retention question that would have followed from one never arrives, and the softer "still covered during grace" reading D-040 rejected was about a thing that does not exist (D-156).
    • Scholarships and hardship waivers — no mechanism, at any level of granularity. What this item used to be hard about was that a waiver had to be visible to few people and invisible to the rest, with only which codes exist standing between the two; the problem is dissolved rather than solved, because there is nothing to see (D-157).
    • Family and sibling discounts — there is no discount column to grant one with. amount_discount is deleted and a reduced fee is an amount_due written smaller, which is the same act that writes any other amount and leaves no mark that a reduction happened; no report will ever count what that discretion was used for (D-183).
    • Invoicing — closed in round 23 and reopened by the country rather than by this design. What D-197 reversed is precisely the sentence this bullet used to hold — "an invoice is a document a payer asks for" — and it was the right answer to a question nobody had asked yet: invoices are compulsory, so the row is written by fee.create beside its request in the same transaction, invoice.issue is deleted, and the set of requests without a document is empty by construction. What D-187 got right and keeps: its own table, its own sequence — now legally required rather than merely tidy — no state, and a payment_request still is not an invoice and never becomes one. What the law requires of one stopped being item 2's open question and became a fact plus a refusal: the fact is that the document must exist, and the refusal is that this module encodes no jurisdiction's rules (§2, D-197, D-198). What the reversal cost is on the page in §2, not here: D-187 had deliberately kept the issuing code apart from payment.match so the person who may type in a number is not the person who may sign a tax document, and that separation no longer exists.
    • What gets a variable symbol, and one per what — every payable, one live request per liability, never per person and never per person-year. The remainder of that item — issue 3 above — closed with it in the same round (D-181, D-196).
    • Payment methods, and collecting rather than recording — both closed earlier by D-075. bank_transfer arrives by import and cash is recorded by hand; voucher and credit_note stay in the enum until somebody says otherwise; there is no card and no direct debit (§2). What remained in those two items was maintenance, not a decision.

    The count, recounted — and the recount is the lesson, so the old one stays. Nothing is open on this page now: the accounting export closed with D-199, jurisdiction with D-197 and D-198, and what a payment that does not fit settles with D-196; the older items below each still stand beside the decision that closed them. What this section used to print here is kept because a count beside a sentence is a claim: the number printed was nine remaining open, and nine was right, while the list underneath it named seven of those nine and counted the variable-symbol item twice — eight printed, with who may authorize a refund and currency missing, which were the two that round closed. A summary that is right above a list that is wrong is worse than a wrong summary: the summary is what a reader quotes, and the list is what a reader answers from. And the claim at the very top of this document, repeated in §1, said five questions were open while this section said three — the header was still naming discounts and variable symbols that earlier rounds had already answered, and only a reader would ever have caught it. Round 24 deleted that header claim rather than reconciling it with this one, because the honest number at both places was zero. In the tables this page owns, the whole round changed one column and one permission code: membership.outstanding_balance gone (§3) and invoice.issue gone (§2); everything else it did was prose.

    None of what closed here was architectural either, which is why it could stay open this long. How money arrives, how it is requested, and what happens to money that does not match were all decided, so what remained was policy that required somebody in the organization to be asked — and being asked was the only thing that was ever missing. This document did not need a rewrite; it needed answers, and now it has them, which is a different thing from a document that needed neither.

    The module's permission codes are shipped codes, and the paragraph that complained about it is gone. Until round 24 this section closed by pointing at the catalog's deferred namespaces and observing that this module's money already moves while its codes sit there as placeholders (D-182). That was true, and it is closed: D-195 documented them in catalog A's money section, and the deferred table lost the rows that called a working module a placeholder. The six codes this module spends are fee.view, fee.create, payment.view, payment.record, payment.reverse and payment.match. Every one of them sits at applies_to = unit, and no choice is being made: money is not an item. A fee_obligation hangs off a person and a period, a payment hangs off a bank statement, and an invoice hangs off a request — none of them is an occasion an audience could bind to, so a money grant aimed at an item would name a scope the row underneath it has nothing to point at. The consequence worth saying out loud is payment.reverse: it sits at the unit, so whoever may reverse money may reverse any of it — nothing in this shape says this payment and not that one, and the only control over that is who was granted the code, once, in the open. The kind each code is follows the only thing that reads the column: fee.view and payment.view are read, and fee.create, payment.record, payment.reverse and payment.match are write. permission.right_kind is required on every row and has exactly one customer — the guardianship channel, which compares a guardian's right_level against it (05 §2) — so which side of that comparison the two view codes fall on is what decides how far a right of see reaches into a child's money, and these two rows say all the way in. That is a guardian's answer and not a hint, which is the only reason the column is filled in on a money row at all. And there is no seventh code, which round 25 settled rather than deferred. Until last round this paragraph explained why a money report's own code sat deliberately undeclared beside the codes reporting already had, and why a module could not settle a question about a reporting shape by promoting its own row. There is nothing left to wait for, because there is nothing left to gate: D-210 deleted reporting's codes along with the screen they authorized, so a module is not one row short of a namespace that no longer exists, and the deferral is not a deferral of anything. Six codes are the whole of what this module spends, and the shape a listing over money takes is not a permission at all: it is a type and an audience, which is why the two cases this page used to hand to a person who might someday open a list are now sent (§2b, and fee-overdue at the indexes in §2). invoice.issue appears in neither catalog, because D-197 deleted the code one answer after D-195 registered it. The finance question that was in the register — K-10 — closed with the six codes, and the half of it that asked about a money report closed a round later by ceasing to be a question (10 §8).
