    Data Model — Fees and Payments¶

    ⚠ PROVISIONAL ON POLICY, SETTLED ON ARCHITECTURE¶

    How money moves is now decided. Money arrives from a bank API and is requested by a variable symbol and a QR code (D-075); money that cannot be matched waits for a person (D-076). There is no card gateway, no checkout and no dispute handling, and §2a is the consequence.

    What the money is for is answered. The fee is priced in koruna (D-155), it buys membership and nothing else (D-156), there is one live price edited in place, and the number on an obligation is the number a person wrote there (D-161, D-160). Nobody waives a fee and nothing refunds one inside the system (D-157, D-158).

    Five questions are open, listed and numbered in §5: discounts, invoices, the accounting export, jurisdiction, and what gets a variable symbol. Those are policy questions this document cannot answer, and each needs somebody in the organization to be asked.

    This document exists because membership activation depends on payment (../domain/05-membership-lifecycle.md), and because the yearly expiry and renewal window (D-040) need somewhere to read a due date from.

    1. What the domain requires¶

    Settled:

    • There is a yearly fee, flat, per person — it does not vary by age, by household, or by membership type, because there is one membership type (D-037).
    • Becoming a member requires paying it; the membership activates on payment (D-040).
    • The fee expires on a fixed organizational date, typically January. At that date the membership lapses — the person is not a member — and a renewal window follows during which they may renew without re-applying. A payment inside the window opens the new period at the expiry date, so the record shows no gap.
    • There is no grace status. The window is tracked as dates, and the resolver does not know it exists.
    • The fee is charged in koruna. One currency, and a second one is refused rather than deferred; the column stays because the refusal is cheap to reverse with it (D-155).
    • The fee buys membership — no insurance is bought with it, sold with it, or implied by it, so no date in this system is evidence of cover (D-156).
    • There is one live price, edited in place when a billing year is reopened, because what a person owed was never stored in the price list anyway (§2) (D-161).
    • What a person owes is whatever their obligation says. No proration rule and no waiver mechanism decide it: a reduced fee is an obligation written with a smaller amount_due, the same act that writes any other amount (D-160, D-157).
    • Money out is recorded exactly like money in — a refund is a payment in the opposite direction, and nobody initiates one inside this system (D-158).

    Unspecified — family or sibling discounts beyond a per-person amount_discount, whether the organization owes people invoices, whether anything is exported to an accounting system, what the law of this country requires of a fee, and what gets a variable symbol. Each is in §5.

    Settled, and the big one — the organization collects, but not through a card gateway: money is requested by variable symbol + QR code and arrives from the bank API already tagged (§2a, D-075).

    2. Minimal provisional shape¶

    Four tables, deliberately small and easy to replace.

    fee_schedule — a price. One is live at a time, and it is edited in place when a billing year is reopened; there is no price history, and none is needed, because what a person owed was never in here.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    code text, required
    name text, required
    description text, optional
    membership_type_id reference to membership type, optional NULL = applies to any type, e.g. an organization-wide surcharge
    period_months integer, required Default 12
    amount money, required Zero or more
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK, and the only value this organization writes. Required and explicit anyway: a second currency is refused for now, and the column is what makes that refusal cheap to reverse (D-155)
    is_active boolean, required Default true
    created_at timestamp, audit time
    created_by reference to person, optional

    Nothing on a schedule is scoped to a body. A fee_schedule is scoped to a membership type — one row, so nothing in practice — and to nothing else at all: it is the price, current until somebody writes a different one into the same row. A fee is what it has always been charged as: flat, per person, at the unit (D-037). Why nothing narrower: D-095 had already ruled that entry below the membership carries no money — into the sub-units, there is no fee — so a supplementary fee for one body was a possibility no answer ever took. If a group ever needs a charge of its own, that is a policy question for §5 and a new shape of obligation, not a column to hold in reserve.

    Rules

    • code is unique. It covers all rows, not only active ones.
    • amount is zero or more.

    Indexes

    • The one lookup that matters is the live schedule for one membership type: the type and is_active together, which are expected to return one row.

    One live price, and why editing it in place is safe. fee_obligation snapshots fee_schedule_id "so the amount is reproducible even if the schedule is edited", and it copies amount_due beside the reference — so the record of what a person owed was never in the schedule at all. Only the price list was, and a price list is not a record (D-161). What is given up is the price list's own history: nobody can ask this system what a membership cost in 2027. The answer available is an aggregate over the obligations of 2027, which reports what people were actually asked rather than what a list said. No code is named for editing the price row, and the gap is named rather than filled: on the reading this design carries, opening a billing year is reached by whatever grant already lets a person write a fee at that scope, which is this corpus's inference from D-161 and not a ruling inside it.

    fee_obligation — one thing someone owes.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    person_id reference to person, required The person the fee is for. Not the payer — see paid_by_person_id on payment
    membership_id reference to membership, optional One of the three target columns; exactly one is set
    membership_request_id reference to membership request, optional One of the three target columns
    event_registration_id reference to event registration, optional One of the three target columns. The event arm is real, not a hedge: an event with a fee_schedule creates one obligation per registration (D-066), and payment confirms the place.
    fee_schedule_id reference to fee schedule, required Snapshotted so the amount is reproducible even if the schedule is edited
    description text, required
    period_from date, required
    period_to date, required
    amount_due money, required
    amount_discount money, required Default 0
    amount_paid money, required Default 0
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK, as on the schedule it came from
    status text, required Default unpaid. One of: unpaid, partially_paid, paid — and nothing else. The three values say how much of this obligation has arrived, and nothing more: lateness is read from due_date, and a fee nobody is going to pay is a row with a smaller amount_due (D-159)
    due_date date, required The yearly fee expires on a fixed organizational date (D-040); this obligation's due_date is what the lapse sweep compares against (§4). The renewal window that follows is carried as dates, not as a status (§1). This is also the only place lateness lives: an obligation past this date and not paid is overdue, and no status value says so (D-159)
    created_at timestamp, audit time
    created_by reference to person, optional
    updated_at timestamp, audit time
    updated_by reference to person, optional
    import_batch_id reference to import batch, optional Set on imported rows

    Rules

    • period_to is later than period_from.
    • amount_due, amount_discount and amount_paid are each zero or more.
    • Exactly one of membership_id, membership_request_id and event_registration_id is set — one, not zero and not two. Every obligation is for exactly one thing: a membership period, a membership request, or one person's place at one event. The per-person choice for events is deliberate: the fee is flat and per person (D-037), and a per-family price would need the household grouping designed again first (D-057).
    • status is arithmetic, not a decision. It reports how much of amount_due has arrived, so there is no value to set for a fee that will not be collected and no value to set because due_date has passed. A fee nobody is going to pay is an obligation written with a smaller amount_due; being late is a report over a date (D-157, D-159).

    Indexes

    • One person's obligations, by status.
    • Obligations ordered by due_date, restricted to the rows still open — unpaid or partially_paid. Closed rows are not in it. Read down this index past today's date and it is the overdue list: one fact stored once, with no status value to keep in step with the calendar.
    • Obligations carrying the event arm, and obligations carrying the membership arm — each restricted to the rows where that column is set.
    • Obligations within a period_from / period_to range.

    The event arm is the third target that is genuinely used. D-037 settled the fee as flat and per person, so an event carrying a fee_schedule has one obligation per registration, created by the code that places the registration and paid to confirm the place (D-066). Nothing creates it if that code is not written: no trigger writes it, and a trigger could not have been forgotten — the trade is stated in 00-conventions §6a. An event with a fee and no obligation is invisible until someone reconciles money against the roster.

    Exactly one, never zero and never two — and unlike most rules in these documents this one can still be stated declaratively, so a migration should state it. Three columns, exactly one set: count how many are present and require that count to be one, which is a condition on the row and nothing else. No subquery, no other table, and therefore emittable by any migration that can put a condition on a row at all — the case where the ORM argument simply does not apply. It is worth naming explicitly, because it is the counterexample to the general worry — most of what D-072 deleted genuinely cannot be restated declaratively (shape 3), and this is not one of them.

    If a fourth target is ever proposed, ask whether it is used, and expect the answer to be no. "At most two of three," with one arm kept against a hypothetical, is not the same domain as exactly one over three real targets, and the difference only shows up when somebody adds the fourth.

    If a family discount is ever wanted, the row is still the right shape — one obligation covering several people — but it now needs the grouping to be re-introduced first, as a group with a period rather than a fake person (04 §3).

    payment — money that moved, as one recorded act.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    public_id external identifier, required Unique. Appears in URLs and exports; id stays internal (00-conventions §3)
    amount money, required Never zero. A negative amount is money going out — a refund, a correction, or a reversal. This is the only way money leaves: there is no refund state anywhere for a row like this to point at, so a refund is written as one of these and the person who recorded it is in the row (D-158)
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK, on money going out exactly as on money coming in
    paid_at timestamp, required When the payment occurred, which may differ from when it was recorded — a bank transfer recorded a week later. Both matter
    recorded_at timestamp, audit time Default: now
    method text, required One of: bank_transfer, cash, voucher, credit_note, other. card and direct_debit removed — there is no card gateway and no mandate system (D-075)
    variable_symbol text, optional The symbol the money arrived under, copied verbatim from the bank statement. Set on every row the import created; the join back to payment_request. Never typed by hand
    reference text, optional Bank reference, transaction id, receipt number
    paid_by_person_id reference to person, optional Who actually paid. May differ from the person the fee is for — a parent paying for a child, a grandparent paying for a grandchild, a sponsor. This is the act-on-behalf record for money
    on_behalf_of reference to person, optional
    status text, required Default confirmed. confirmed or reversed, and nothing else. A bank transfer the bank has reported does not later fail, expire, or get reversed by a card network, so the two values describe whether we still stand behind the row. There is no pending and no failed: a promised transfer is not a state (D-075)
    confirmed_by reference to person, optional
    notes text, optional
    import_batch_id reference to import batch, optional Set on imported rows
    created_at timestamp, audit time
    created_by reference to person, optional

    Rules

    • amount is never zero; zero is meaningless. Negative amounts are reversals.
    • public_id is unique.

    Indexes

    • A payer's payments, newest paid_at first.
    • Payments by date, newest first.
    • Lookup by reference, restricted to rows that have one.

    payment_allocation — which payments satisfy which obligations. It is many-to-many in both directions: one payment may cover several obligations, and one obligation may be paid in instalments.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    payment_id reference to payment, required
    fee_obligation_id reference to fee obligation, required
    amount money, required Never zero
    allocated_at timestamp, audit time Default: now
    allocated_by reference to person, optional
    note text, optional

    Rules

    • A given payment is allocated to a given obligation at most once — one row per pair, over all rows.
    • amount is never zero.

    Indexes

    • Allocations of one payment, and allocations of one obligation — both directions are asked.

    The allocation table is the part most likely to be right. A payment and an obligation are genuinely many-to-many, and any design that puts payment_id on the obligation (or vice versa) breaks the first time a parent pays for three children in one transfer. That is the common case in this organization, not an edge case.

    2a. How money actually arrives¶

    D-075. The system requests a payment and then reads one. It never touches the money.

    The flow, in order:

    1. A liability exists — a membership period, a membership request, a place at an event.
    2. The system issues a payment_request carrying a variable symbol and an amount.
    3. The payer is shown the symbol, the amount, and a QR code that encodes all three.
    4. The payer's own bank moves the money. The system is not involved and learns nothing yet.
    5. A bank import reads the account statement and produces a bank_transaction for each incoming transfer, with the variable symbol still attached.
    6. The symbol resolves to a live request. The import creates a payment, links it, and the obligation's balance falls.
    7. If the symbol resolves to nothing live, the row goes to the queue (§2b).

    payment_request — the liability rendered payable.

    Fields

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    variable_symbol text, required Unique across all rows, forever, including cancelled and long-settled ones. Eleven digits from a sequence
    fee_obligation_id reference to fee obligation, required What is owed. The request never invents an amount of its own
    amount money, required Copied from the obligation at issue time, so a later price change cannot rewrite what someone was asked for
    currency three-letter currency code, required CZK, copied alongside the amount for the same reason
    recipient_account text, required The account the QR code tells people to pay into
    issued_at timestamp, audit time
    expires_at date, optional When the request stops being payable — the close of a renewal window, or the day before an event
    voided_at timestamp, optional Set by a person, or by cancelling the event the request was for (§2b)
    voided_reason text, optional

    Rules

    • variable_symbol is unique over every row that ever existed, not over live rows. A symbol issued in 2019 must never resolve to a 2026 obligation, because bank statements get re-imported and late payments are the entire reason the renewal window exists.
    • The symbol comes from a sequence. It is never derived from a birth date, a membership id, a member number, or anything else a person can guess or work out. Anyone can type eleven digits into a payment form at their bank; a guessable symbol means a stranger's money lands in a child's record, and the damage is not cosmetic — it reads as a paid fee and an active membership that nobody earned, in the one part of this database that is kept with no end date (D-088). A fee row is the organization's own history from the moment it is written, not a draft somebody confirms later. That is also the ceiling on the damage: since the fee buys membership and nothing else (D-156), no date here can be read as anything more than that.
    • amount is a copy, not a live reference. What a person was asked to pay is a historical fact about the ask, and recomputing it from the current schedule would make every price change rewrite history.
    • One obligation may have several requests over time — a first request expires, or the amount is corrected — but at most one live request per obligation.
    • A request is not an invoice. Nothing is delivered, nothing is numbered for a tax authority, and whether the organization also owes people invoices is still an open question (§5).

    bank_transaction — one incoming transfer as the bank reported it.

    Field Kind Notes
    id surrogate id
    bank_account text, required
    bank_reference text, required Unique per bank account. This is the idempotency key: the same statement imported twice must create nothing the second time
    arrived_at date, required The bank's date, not the import's — this is the date D-040's renewal window is measured against
    amount money, required
    variable_symbol text, optional As reported. Can be absent, wrong, or a stranger's
    payer_description text, optional Whatever the bank gives: a name, sometimes an address
    matched_payment_request_id reference to payment request, optional NULL means unmatched, which is the queue
    imported_at timestamp, audit time
    import_batch_id reference to import batch, optional

    Rules

    • bank_reference is unique within a bank account. Re-importing a statement is safe and must be.
    • A transaction is immutable once imported. Corrections are new rows or a re-import, never an edit — this is the organization's copy of somebody else's ledger, and the only version of it that will ever agree with the bank.

    The renewal window stops being a promise. D-040 backdates a renewal to the expiry date when payment lands inside the window, and its own note said that rule was only as good as the reliability of payment dates. Until now they arrived when somebody noticed a transfer. They now arrive from the bank, dated by the bank. The sweep in §4 can run unattended because the thing it depends on is no longer a person remembering in February.

    What this deletes from the design. No checkout, no card tokens, no PCI surface, no webhook endpoint to authenticate, no dispute or chargeback state, no reconciliation report against a processor's payout. payment.status loses pending and failed: a bank transfer the bank has reported is confirmed on arrival, and there is no in-between state to model. What remains in status is whether we still stand behind the row — confirmed or reversed.

    2b. Money that cannot be matched¶

    D-076.

    An unmatched transaction is a fact with no subject, so the absence gets stored rather than guessed at — the same discipline as "unanswered is the absence of a row" (D-059). matched_payment_request_id IS NULL is the queue; there is no separate worklist table to fall out of sync with the rows it describes.

    How a transaction ends up there: the symbol matches no request. The symbol matches a request that has been voided. The request is already settled. The amount is less than asked, or more. There is no symbol at all — cash-basis donors and people who type their own name in the wrong field.

    What the system does with any of those: nothing. It records the money and waits.

    A person decides, and there are exactly four outcomes:

    Outcome When
    Attach it to a different request The symbol was mistyped; the real obligation is obvious from the payer
    Split it across requests One transfer, three siblings — the ordinary shape of family payment here
    Record as a donation Money with no obligation behind it, which is not the same as money that is missing one
    Refund it, by hand Nothing was meant to be owed. A second payment in the opposite direction (D-075): the transfer goes out at the bank, and what arrives here is a payment naming whoever recorded it (D-158)

    Why the tempting shortcut is refused. Falling back to the payer's identity when the symbol fails looks like it removes most of the queue. It removes most of the queue's accuracy instead: a parent with two children pays one amount against one symbol is the normal case, not the edge case, and an identity fallback resolves it to whichever child the query reaches first. A fee recorded against the wrong child is a finance error and a membership that appears paid when it is not, both at once — and the sibling it was meant for stays the one who looks unpaid. Both halves sit in rows that are kept with no end date. A fallback is not allowed to be wrong on the common path, and this is the common path.

    Auto-refunding is refused for two reasons: it needs the bank integration to work outbound as well as inbound, which is a second integration with its own authorization; and it returns the grandparent's attempt at a camp contribution. The first of those has changed kind while the refusal stands. Calls coming in are a designed thing now — a registered key, granted like any other caller (05e) — so "outbound is a second integration with its own authorization" is no longer an absence but a decision with a shape to copy, and the outbound direction itself is F-27. The second reason was never a mechanism, and stands alone.

    Cancellation is where most of the volume comes from, and it is decided now rather than later. D-064 made cancellation a recorded fact rather than a state, so the event survives, the registrations survive — and so would an outstanding request against an occasion that is not going to happen. So:

    • Cancelling an event voids its outstanding payment requests, recording why.
    • Money already received for a cancelled event becomes a queue item, for a person to refund or to carry forward to a later event. It is not auto-refunded, for the reasons above.

    This is EV-3's answer, which is why it is closed here rather than left parked in the events module. Nothing about the queue's shape changes, and nothing about its end does either: the queue is where the system stops and a person begins, and that person's next act is at the organization's bank, not on a screen in this design (D-158).

    The queue is not allowed to be silently ignored. It is a count on a dashboard, not a page nobody navigates to, and its oldest item is a number someone sees monthly. Unmatched money is the one finance backlog that gets worse by itself: the payer believes they have paid, and the organization believes they have not.

    3. Keeping membership.outstanding_balance honest¶

    03-membership.md has a denormalised outstanding_balance. If it stays, it must be maintained in the same transaction as any allocation change, or it will drift. Two options:

    Option Pros Cons
    Maintain it inside the transaction that writes payment_allocation Always current, no recompute Every path that writes an allocation has to do it — the trigger that used to do it for all of them at once is gone — and the arithmetic is easy to get subtly wrong
    Drop the column, compute on demand Cannot drift Slower worklist queries
    Keep as a materialized view Cannot drift, fast Refresh lag

    Recommendation: drop the column and use a view. The "approved but unpaid" worklist is not a hot path, and a wrong balance produces either a wrongly-suspended member or a wrongly-chased family. A wrong number here has a human consequence.

    The definition, stated once, wherever it is implemented:

    balance = amount_due − amount_discount − the sum of allocations whose payment is confirmed

    Only confirmed payments count. An obligation is not settled by money that has been promised, intended, described in an email, or reported by a payer who believes they sent it. There is no state for a transfer that is on its way (D-075): either the bank has reported it and it is confirmed, or the system has not heard of it. A member is activated by the first, never by the second.

    4. How this connects to membership¶

    The activation gate. A membership's fee is settled when no obligation attached to it has a positive balance after discounts, and that is the whole test with no status excepted from it — a fee nobody is going to collect is an obligation written with a smaller amount_due, so it never presents itself as a balance to work around (D-157, D-159). This test used to exclude obligations carrying waived, written_off or refunded outright, and every one of those values is gone, so the exclusion would now exclude nothing — which is the way you see it was a repair and not a rule. "Settled" is the only thing money says about membership; it does not grant permissions by itself (D-005). The route from a settled fee to anything a person may do goes through a set: the fee makes is_current_member() true, is_current_member() is what MEMBERS evaluates, and only a grant whose expression names MEMBERS cares (D-100) — which is why money cannot become authority by itself.

    The yearly expiry sweep¶

    Run on 1 January (D-047). This is the one automatic membership transition in the system, and the only thing in this document that changes a person's status.

    For every membership that is active, has an obligation with a positive balance, and whose due date has passed:

    1. Mark it lapsed, ending it at the expiry date — not at today. The period ended on that date whether or not anyone noticed.
    2. Open the renewal window as a date, never as a status. The resolver does not know windows exist.

    Nothing has to be told afterwards. MEMBERS is evaluated live on the checked date (D-100), so the two facts step 1 writes — the period ending and the lapsed status — are visible to the next check, with no job in between and no stored answer to invalidate (D-114, domain/06 §8). What the sweep costs is stated where cost belongs: on 1 January the answer moves for every member at once, and a screen opened that day computes it rather than reading it (D-112).

    Note the personnel consequence, which is easy to miss and fatal to get wrong. This runs for everyone at once, including the person who runs it, and nothing waives the membership test for a named person: D-100 deleted system.gate_exempt, person_holds_gate_exempt and the resolver clause behind them. The answer is the grant text. A grant written MEMBERS * ROLE(it) stops applying to its holder at the sweep; a grant written ROLE(it) keeps applying, because the set test is simply not in it. Assumption: which offices are written that way is not settled by D-100, and getting it wrong fails in the direction that locks a person out with no permission left with which to undo it — the standing report of live grants naming a ROLE(...) operand without MEMBERS is what makes each such choice visible before 1 January rather than on it (03-membership.md §6).

    The expiry is the one automatic transition in this design, and what makes it allowable is that it is not a decision (D-023, D-040): the fee covers a fixed period and the period ends, and the renewal window absorbs the late-transfer case, because a payment inside it backdates the new period to the expiry date and the record shows no gap.

    What the window cannot absorb is a payment that arrived before the expiry but was recorded after it. That is a data-entry lag, not a non-payment, and it is corrected with membership.backdate rather than left as a real gap. Worth a report: members who lapsed and then paid within a few days.

    4a. Integrity checks¶

    Invariants to verify. This section arrived late and by decision (D-135); what it does is not new to this folder, it is what every other page here already had. The rules below are all stated somewhere above this heading — §2, §2a and §3 each state one and then said nothing about what would notice a breach.

    1. The balance the system shows, recomputed from §3's definition. amount_due − amount_discount − the sum of allocations whose payment is confirmed, per obligation, and the same sum rolled up wherever a membership's balance is displayed. Two states put a row on this list: an allocation whose payment has since been reversed still sitting in the total, and a discount recorded on the obligation but not carried into the roll-up. What it cannot catch is the definition being wrong on both sides — a screen and a report that each recompute amount_due − amount_paid agree perfectly and are both counting money that was only promised. If §3's recommendation is taken and the column becomes a view, this check does not retire. It moves from "the copy agrees with the sum" to "the view implements the sum", which is the same query and the same failure list, and D-075's rule that only confirmed payments count is what it is checking either way.
    2. A payment whose allocations do not account for its amount, and an obligation allocated past what it owes. payment_allocation is many-to-many in both directions and §2's rules constrain the pair and the sign of each amount, never the sums. So a payment with unallocated remainder is money that moved and was never attributed to anything, and an obligation allocated beyond amount_due − amount_discount is an overpayment that nobody has refunded. The layer here is the write path, and there is no constraint to sit beneath it: the sums span rows of two tables, which is shape 3, so this report is not a net under a rule — it is the whole of the backstop. §2 called this table "the part most likely to be right", and that is an argument about the shape, not about the data ten years in.
    3. Money nobody ever asked for. Two readings of one failure, cheapest first: an obligation still at unpaid or partially_paid whose due_date has passed with no live payment_request pointing at it, so nothing has ever carried a variable symbol to anybody. The two conditions are read together and neither is stored twice: due_date past plus a status that is not paid is overdue, which is why no status value carries it (§2) (D-159); and upstream of that, an event_registration for an event carrying a fee_schedule with no fee_obligation behind it, which §2 already warned of in one sentence — an event with a fee and no obligation is invisible until someone reconciles money against the roster, because nothing creates the obligation and no trigger could have been forgotten. Do not read this list as the queue. An unmatched bank_transaction is money with no subject, and D-076 rules that it waits for a person, which is a correct state with a page of its own (§2b). This is the opposite case: a liability with no ask attached, which is silent in both directions — nobody is chased and nobody pays.

    Checks 1 and 2 are the two that matter, and §3 says why in one line: a wrong number here has a human consequence — either a wrongly-suspended member or a wrongly-chased family. Check 3 is the one that finds a whole class of missing money rather than a wrong row, and it is the only one of the three that can be non-empty because a feature was never written rather than because a row is wrong.

    5. Open issues — the whole of this document, essentially¶

    Five questions are open, and they are the five below. What this section used to ask that it no longer does, and the arithmetic of the list, are at the end of the page rather than inside the list.

    1. Are there family/sibling discounts? amount_discount exists but the rules are unspecified. With a flat per-person fee (D-037), this is the only remaining fee variation, so it is worth a deliberate yes or no. A per-family discount needs a grouping to be designed first — there is none (D-057) — so it is not a constraint change but a shape. A per-person discount is unaffected and is just amount_discount.
    2. Invoicing: does the organization issue invoices (legal requirement in some jurisdictions)? If so, an invoice entity and a numbering sequence are needed. Sharper now: a payment_request looks like an invoice, prints like one, and is not one — it has no tax identity, no sequence required by law, and no delivery record. If invoices are legally required, the honest answer is a second document over the same obligation, not a renaming of this one.
    3. Accounting integration: does anything need to be exported to an accounting system? What is open here is the direction. Who may call this system is designed (05e); this system calling anyone else is F-27.
    4. Jurisdiction. This is the half of an item that used to read "currency and jurisdiction", and only the currency half was answerable: the currency is koruna, on all four tables that carry one (§2) (D-155). What is left is what the law of this country asks of a fee, which this design does not know and has no column for: whether invoices of item 2's kind are compulsory rather than chosen, and whether the organization owes anything about its money to anybody outside itself. This is the finance module's share of the same sitting that already holds the four data policies in front of a legal review (00-conventions §8, 06b §6).

    5. What gets a variable symbol, and one per what? — raised by D-075 rather than left over. That decision fixed how money arrives and named the payment_request that carries it, but not the two choices the module is actually built from.

      • Scope. Do event fees get a symbol, or only membership fees? A camp fee is the same mechanism, and a symbol is what lets a family pay for it at their own bank without staff involvement. Recommendation: every payable, because the alternative is a second, hand-recorded money path sitting beside an automatic one — and hand-recorded is what D-040's renewal window just escaped.
      • Granularity. One live request per liability, matching the fee_obligation row exactly — which is what §2a already assumes. Not per person, because a parent paying for two children in one transfer is the ordinary case and per-person symbols make that impossible to match. Not per person-year either: a camp is a separate liability from a fee and must never share a symbol with one.
      • The amount mismatch, which D-076 defers and this question cannot. 500 sent against a 550 request goes to the queue, which is correct as a mechanism and silent as a policy. Whether partial payment settles anything, whether 4.90 vs 5.00 is a shortfall, and whether the payer finds out at all when their money lands and does not fit — nothing here sends anything to anyone (D-137), so finds out means a person telephoning them or a module that is not designed yet — those are undecided, and the queue will show them at volume the first spring the import runs.

      Why this is not a footnote: it decides whether the module has one money path or two.

    What this section used to ask, and where each answer lives. The list ran to eleven items; four are closed and one is narrowed to what is left of it. They are kept here in one line each, because for most of them this page is the only place the question was ever written:

    • The fee amount — the question this page asserted twice and never filed, which is why nothing could ever have closed it. Filed now as item 12 of the old numbering and answered in the same act: one live price, edited in place, and the amount a person owes is the number written on their obligation (D-161).
    • Is the fee prorated for mid-year joins — no rule. Someone joining in October gets an amount_due of whatever the person opening it writes, and the renewal twelve months later is written by the same kind of act. Nobody will be able to point at a rule and say this organization prorates, or refuses to (D-160).
    • Who may authorize a refund — nobody, because nothing is initiated here. The transfer goes out at the bank; what arrives here is a payment naming whoever recorded it (D-158).
    • Does the fee cover insurance — it does not. No date in this system is evidence of cover, so the retention question that would have followed from one never arrives, and the softer "still covered during grace" reading D-040 rejected was about a thing that does not exist (D-156).
    • Scholarships and hardship waivers — no mechanism, at any level of granularity. What this item used to be hard about was that a waiver had to be visible to few people and invisible to the rest, with only which codes exist standing between the two; the problem is dissolved rather than solved, because there is nothing to see (D-157).
    • Payment methods, and collecting rather than recording — both closed earlier by D-075. bank_transfer arrives by import and cash is recorded by hand; voucher and credit_note stay in the enum until somebody says otherwise; there is no card and no direct debit (§2). What remained in those two items was maintenance, not a decision.

    The count, recounted. Five questions are open: discounts, invoices, the accounting export, jurisdiction, and what gets a variable symbol — the last still three questions in a trenchcoat, because it decides whether the module has one money path or two. The number this section printed was nine remaining open, and nine was right, while the list printed underneath it named seven of those nine and counted the variable-symbol item twice — eight printed, with who may authorize a refund and currency missing, which are the two this round closed. A summary that is right above a list that is wrong is worse than a wrong summary: the summary is what a reader quotes, and the list is what a reader answers from. The claim at the top of this document, repeated in §1, that the fee amount is unanswered named a question that was not on the list either, so it had to be filed before anything could answer it. A count next to a sentence is a claim, and the count was the part nobody had checked.

    None of the five is architectural. The architecture is why they can be left open: how money arrives, how it is requested, and what happens to money that does not match are all decided, so what remains is policy that requires somebody in the organization to be asked — discounts, invoices, the accounting export, jurisdiction, and the choices item 5 names. This document does not need a rewrite; it needs answers.

    None of the five is registered in the open-questions register, and this page is why: the register's finance row reports a number of open questions and names none of them, so a reader who never opens this document finds no finance question at all. The questions settled along the way — activation on payment, the flat per-person fee, the absence of a household grouping, collecting rather than recording — are registered there with the decision that settled each, and nothing else on this page is. The five above are the finance module's own open issues until somebody registers them or says here why not.
