    Open Questions¶

    Everything still unresolved — fourteen questions, each with its schema impact, what changes once you answer, ordered roughly by how much they block. Thirteen of the seventeen questions that were on this page were answered by round 20, every 🟡 among them, and that round also took the last three of § J — what remained of the section round 18 raised by writing down who enforces each invariant. The round also left one behind it: F-34, raised by the answer it gave to AT-2 rather than found on this page: deleting the attribute proposal stage turned out to delete a schedule nobody had written down. It is filed under § F with the questions it resembles, not in a section of its own — a question a ruling raised is an ordinary open question once it exists. § J is therefore gone, emptied by being answered rather than by being reversed, which is the first time this register has closed a section that way.

    Marked: 🔴 blocking something else · 🟡 important · 🟢 can be deferred.

    A question that has been answered is not kept here. Its entry is deleted; what remains is one row in § R. Resolved naming the question, the decision that settled it and what the answer cost, and the reasoning lives in that decision entry. Nothing is silently dropped: a row in § R carries a short anchor, so a link written years ago against F-7 still lands somewhere true.

    What the five survivors are, since that is short enough to say here: A-4's remainder is the organizational calendar, E-3 is how this organisation reads its own civil code, and F-9 and F-11 both turn on what a membership type is for. F-34 is the odd one: nothing in the schema waits on it — it asks who does a job and when, which no ruling can decide alone because the answer is a person's name and a date. Not one of the five is 🟡.

    The section letters are names, not a sequence. Sections B, C and D held only closed questions and are gone; § J joined them in round 20, emptied by answering it, and § G (events) and § Ga (attributes) went the same way when their seven questions were answered. The modules they framed are unaffected — both are designed, and § H still links them. The ids those sections held — B-2, C-5, D-9, EV-2, AT-4 — are still how § R means them, and E-6 is still the question it was when it was filed.

    § I lists every assumption the documentation is standing on; § How to answer says which of these is worth answering next.

    A. Blocking the core design¶

    A-4 (remainder) 🟢 The organizational calendar¶

    Answered. The fee expires on 1 January (D-047) and the renewal window is 60 days (D-048).

    What is left is not a question but a discipline. Both values live in membership_type as columns — expiry_month, expiry_day, renewal_grace_days — so either can be moved by an UPDATE. The one way to lose that property is a scheduled job or a front end that contains the literal 60 or 1. Worth one line in code review: do these values come from the row?

    See: data-model/03 §1, domain/05 §6

    E. Guardianship¶

    E-3 🟢 Guardian acting for an adult child¶

    Question. Can a parent register their adult child for an event, with the child's consent?

    Recommendation. An explicit consent-based delegation, not a guardianship — the legal basis differs and access_grant.is_delegated already supports it.

    Note. Now that grants can target audiences (D-042), the event-wide version of this question — "register everyone in this group" — is answered without touching guardianship at all. What remains is the individual case.

    F. Authorization¶

    F-9 🟢 Should membership_type stay a table with one row?¶

    Question. D-037 kept membership_type as a table with a single seeded member row, on the principle that vocabularies are rows and not enums — at the cost of an FK that always holds one value.

    Impact. A deliberate trade, not an oversight. Worth confirming it is the trade you want, since it is the one place the design pays a structural cost for a hypothetical future.

    See: data-model/03 §1

    F-11 🟢 Does the identity reviewer need to read the person record to settle a claim?¶

    Question. D-089 routes a colliding sign-up to identity_reviewer and resolves it with person.merge. The office an organization writes for this job — the catalog's §11 is an example of writing one — holds person.merge, import.view and import.apply, and not person.view. Nothing ships a reviewer, so what used to be a fact about the seed is now a choice somebody writes: as written, a reviewer decides "is this the same child?" from match signals, names and dates, without opening either record.

    Why this is a question now and was not one before. Merging already needed a human, but the alternative to merging was doing nothing: a wrong call left two rows side by side, visible in the nightly duplicate report, correctable next quarter. The activation gate changed the stakes. A reviewer who rejects a hypothesis lets an account go live; one who confirms it joins two lives, including a login, into one row. Both directions now have a person waiting on the far side of the answer, which is a different job from tidying a queue, and a job the permission set does not obviously cover.

    Positions. - Leave it — the signals are the decision, and a reviewer who needs to read both records is a reviewer who is about to merge on a hunch anyway. Cheapest, and consistent with "identity is what the match says, adjudicated by a person". - Grant person.view to identity_reviewer — settles it, and quietly makes an identity role one of the roles that can read a child's whole record, which is a bigger thing than the role's name suggests and is exactly what the catalog's top tier is reserved for. - A narrower read the catalog does not have yet — the resolver code that would expose only the two candidate rows and their signals, which would be a new code and therefore a decision entry, not an edit to this file. It is not proposed here, and would need arguing.

    Recommendation. Leave it, and watch the queue rather than the design. If reviewers start resolving hypotheses at a suspiciously high confirmation rate without asking for context, that is the sign the signals are not enough — and the honest response is a person.view grant discussed as a top-tier membership, not a new code invented at a desk.

    Needed. Nothing before implementation. Revisit when the first real import has produced a real queue.

    F-34 🟢 Who writes the vocabulary the first import needs, and who holds attribute.define afterwards?¶

    Question. Round 20 deleted the attribute proposal stage: a definition is live at the save of whoever holds attribute.define, and the legacy import refuses at apply time on a mapped column naming a code that is not already defined and live (D-141, data-model/09 §7). So the several hundred definitions the first real batch needs have to be authored before the batch that looks like it produces them. Who writes them, over what period, and who holds the code once the import is done?

    Why this is a question now and was not one before. proposed was the answer while it existed: the import could write codes nobody had read and the queue was where the reading happened. It was a bad answer — a third status no decision had granted, with a check whose whole job was to age it out — but it carried a schedule inside it, and deleting the status deleted the schedule with it. This is the one place in the round where deleting a mechanism removed the answer to a question nobody had written down, and the round noticed only afterward.

    Impact. None on the schema — no column turns on this, and no invariant is stated about it. It is a sequencing risk on the single most fragile operation in the corpus. Day one does not block, and since round 21 that is true in the plainest possible way: day one is that one account, holding system.admin, which satisfies every code including this one, so the first definitions are writable before anything else exists. What is undecided is the work and the handover, and the round narrowed this question to the handover: who reads the column map, whose job it is to write several hundred codes and their value vocabularies before a batch is scheduled, and who holds attribute.define after the person who set the system up stops being the person using it — because one holder is not curation, and the queue that used to make an uncurated vocabulary visible is gone (data-model/09 §8 is what notices rot now, and it can only fire on codes that exist).

    Positions. - The map and the vocabulary are one artefact, written in one sitting. This is what domain/03 §7 already assumes: whoever authors the column→code map authors the codes, and having held the permission while reading the list is the endorsement the old status was invented to express. Cheapest, and it makes the import a two-part job done by one person. - A definitions file imported before the person batch. Turns the vocabulary into a second import format — real design work that does not exist in these pages, and it would need its own refusals. Not proposed here. - Distribute attribute.define first and let the curators write it together. The honest answer to "one holder is not curation", and it is a decision about who may decide what the organization knows about people, which is exactly the kind of thing this catalog refuses to decide in passing.

    Recommendation. Take the first position and write it down as a plan rather than a ruling: the import is scheduled after the vocabulary exists, the map is reviewed top to bottom by the person who wrote it, and attribute.define is named to a person — not to an office, since an office confers nothing (D-100) — before the first batch is attempted. The wrong answer is a new status or a new queue, which is the thing this round just spent an entry deleting.

    Needed. Nothing before implementation. Needed before the first real import is scheduled: a named holder of attribute.define, and the codes the map names committed somewhere a second pair of eyes can read them.

    See: D-141, AT-2 (the question this one replaces), domain/03 §7, permission-catalog §12.

    H. Not yet designed¶

    Each of these is referenced by the schema and has hooks left for it. Two rows carry a ✅ because the area needed no new module: events was designed in round 5, and the medical record was answered out of existence in round 7 — what a reader who says "the medical record" is pointing at is the attribute vocabulary of data-model/09, one class of person data with no privileged subset (D-083).

    Fees and payments data-model/07-finance.md — architecture settled, policy open Five open questions — discounts, invoices, the accounting export, jurisdiction, and the variable symbol — which is the whole of it, and the sentence used to read nine open questions without naming one of them while the module page kept a twelve-item list of its own that nothing here could see. Round 6 had already closed the architectural ones: money arrives by bank API, is requested by variable symbol + QR code, there is no card gateway, and unmatched money queues (D-075, D-076).     Health records ✅ Designed — as domain/08 and data-model/09 There was no medical record to design. Reading is person.view, writing is person.edit, and only the vocabulary needs a code of its own. What is left undesigned is policy around one class rather than a second module: what happens to the history of a value once the audit payloads expire. The other half of that sentence is now answered — whether attribute.define is a sufficient gate for a vocabulary free to invent a "health" category tomorrow, which round 20 settled by deciding there is no second gate: a definition is live at the save of whoever holds the code (D-141), and the widening is a stated property of that code rather than a queue in front of it. The trail side of the same worry is AT-4.
    Communication message.send_direct, message.broadcast (scoped) — shape decided, detail undesigned Shape decided by D-077: internal first, external channels are delivery; everyone may send direct; broadcast is one scoped permission; the recipient picks the channel. Needs: templates, opt-out granularity, retention, moderation. The rule against contacting minors directly was declined as a sender restriction and replaced by mirroring — see MSG-1.
    Reporting report.* permissions sketched The historical queries in domain/05 are the starting set. Query 10 (members per year, as far back as the records reach) is the acceptance test.
    API and UI api_key and its four codes: who may call in is designed (05e, D-116); the resolver's at parameter exists partly for this The caller is designed; the wire is not. What a request looks like, how a secret is presented and carried, and how a session survives a login are API-layer work, and D-072 is the standing division — what is decided is only what may be stored: a hash, and nothing else. The UI side of the same decision is one permissions page answering a second question (§15's tabs). Calls going out were answered in round 20 without being designed: an outbound call is a person's act or this system's job, never a third kind of actor (F-27, D-146). Beside it, three things are settled rather than open — this system's own writers are an empty actor column read as the system acting on its own and named by a fixed label list (D-125), a lockout is a report plus a written procedure rather than a mechanism (D-126), and everything a caller sends over the wire is API work, not schema work.
    Notifications — Nothing in this design notifies anybody today, and that is a ruling rather than a gap (D-164, generalizing D-137): a report is a tab somebody opens and the audit trail is the control. The one person the authority model does point at survives this untouched, because D-126's break-glass actor is a procedure needing somebody, not a message needing a recipient. What this row still describes is an undesigned module, and its one dated shape is worth keeping if it is ever built — the renewal window makes it dated rather than status-driven, a job over in_renewal_window() rather than a watcher on a status column. Nothing waits on it: no rule in the register reads a send, because nothing is sent.
    Payment gateway — Answered, so this row is now narrower than it was. The organization collects via bank API, not a card gateway (D-075): no webhooks, no disputes, no processor reconciliation. What remains undesigned is the statement import itself — file formats, the idempotency key, and what happens to an account the bank revises.
    Multi-language — Answered for the attribute vocabulary by D-149: one language, no translation columns. The same reasoning covers the other words the database holds — nothing else on it was asked about separately — because a row holding two labels has to be asked which of the two the record is. The example this row was written with has itself gone: there is no unit left whose name could be translated (D-097).

    K. Filed by a module page¶

    Nine questions, every one of them raised by round 22, each verified on the page that holds it and deliberately not asked there. They are on this register rather than left in a decision page because they are the defect they came from. A module page had been keeping an open-issue section for sixteen rounds which this register could not see and no reader reached: data-model/07 §5 called itself "the whole of this document, essentially", filed nothing here, and this page's finance row reported "nine open questions" without naming one. A question that is not on a register is not an open question; it is a sentence on a page. These nine are the repair, and the finding that required it is told properly in round 22.

    None of the nine blocks anything, and none was answered this round — they are written here in the words the page that holds them uses, so that answering one starts from the place that has to change rather than from a summary of it. Two of the nine ask whether a column should exist at all (K-4, K-6); the rest ask for a rule, a list, or a name.

    K-1 🟢 Must a member row sit inside its group's own period?¶

    Found in data-model/02 §8, which says the containment is "nowhere ruled", and mirrored in the register, whose row for it gives the save-time check as none — containment is assumed and the backstop as 02 §6 check 2. What changes when it is answered: either a refusal at the save, or the report stays the whole control and the register's row says so on purpose rather than by omission.

    K-2 🟢 Is an ended group hidden from pickers, and may it be reopened?¶

    Also 02 §8. It collides with §1's rule that a returning name is a new group: if an ended group may be reopened, "the same name twice" stops being one rule and becomes two. What changes: nothing in a table; one sentence about what a picker lists, which is the sort of answer this repository has been happiest to receive.

    K-3 🟢 Where is a claimed continuity between two groups recorded?¶

    02 §8 again. D-098 refused group-to-group edges, and the one rule this corpus had about continuity belonged to units: D-096 ruled a reused number a new unit unless someone records the continuity, and D-097 removed the units. What changes: either a self-reference on person_group, which is a decision to reopen that refusal, or the words "the same troop, 1998" stay outside the database, which is survivable and should be written down as chosen.

    K-4 🟢 Is person.gender needed at all, and if so with what value set?¶

    Verbatim from 01b §11. It is on a page about erasure, which is why it matters: a column kept for a form nobody remembers filling is a column the organization has to justify keeping. What changes: a column, its value set, and its place in the redaction rules.

    K-5 🟢 Does the organization have, or will it have, a national SSO identity provider?¶

    Also 01b §11. What changes: how much of account_credential is ever built. It is the difference between this system holding password hashes and holding one external identifier, which is why it is filed even though its answer is a sentence.

    K-6 🟢 Should person.notes exist at all?¶

    Argued on the page that holds it: 01-identity's What is deliberately not on this table. It is the last free text about a person kept outside the attribute vocabulary, it is logged with its contents, and it is deliberately not on the redactor's list — which makes it the one column where "may contain a diagnosis" is a property of the design rather than of careless typing. What changes: a column, or the attribute vocabulary gaining the one job this column was doing.

    K-7 🟢 Which tables does the audit obligation actually cover?¶

    06b §6, which asks for confirmation rather than derivation — §2.1 already obligates every business table, so what is outstanding is somebody reading the list and saying yes. What changes: nothing, if the list is right, which is the cheapest question on this page and the one nobody volunteers for.

    K-8 🟢 Who reads the queue of money that cannot be matched, and how often?¶

    data-model/07 says unmatched money waits for a person, and D-076 decided that waiting is better than guessing. What changes: nothing in the schema — a name and a rhythm, which is the shape of question F-34 is on this page for.

    K-9 🟢 May staff create an account for a child, or only for an adult?¶

    domain/02 §7, and the one question round 22 touched without closing it: D-166 ruled that no consent step and no age gate lives on an account, which settles what the account is not and says nothing about who may open one. What changes: nothing structural — and that is the reason it is worth answering, since a permission with no default is decided by whoever writes the grant.

    R. Resolved — moved to the decision log¶

    Kept here so a reader of this file knows the question was asked and where the answer lives, rather than finding it silently deleted.

    A-3 Activate on payment or on approval? D-040 On payment; the fee expires yearly and a dated renewal window replaces a grace status.
    A-4 Background checks D-043 None are recorded in this system.
    A-4 The expiry date D-047 1 January; the organizational year runs with the calendar.
    A-4 The renewal window length D-048 60 days, stored as a parameter rather than compiled in.
    C-1 May the root read into its sub-units? D-049 No. scope_exposure is the only crossing; no reporting channel is built. Formally closed in round 9, and D-094 gave it a mirror: imported events belong to the root, so a unit's own event list contains history it did not run. Round 10 dissolved the question: there is no sub-unit to read into and scope_exposure is deleted (D-097).
    C-3 Is a reused unit number the same unit? D-096 A new unit, unless someone records continuity as successor_of. A code is a typed label, not an identity. Moot with the table (D-097).
    C-4 Does appointing someone create a role assignment? D-091, restated by D-098, D-100, D-101 No. Round 10 redrew the line the answer drew: the union-of-three-arms became an expression language (+ - *), a group became a hand-stated dated roster, and an audience became the rule. Appointing still creates nothing, and there are no positions left to appoint. Rule-filled roles went to F-12, which round 10 closed no.
    C-5 What creates the units the import's rows point at? D-092, D-093, D-094 Nothing, because the rows name no units. People arrive as bounded memberships, events as root-owned events with registrations. The historical record has no sub-units in it.
    C-6 Should allows_minors refuse a minor's position as it refuses their participation? D-095 Neither: units carry no restrictions and the flag is removed. The control is that nobody joins a unit or takes a position themself — a counselor writes the row. Positions went in round 10 (D-100); the control is now person_group.member.manage.
    D-6 How may the membership gate be waived? D-051 A person-level system.gate_exempt permission, in addition to the role flag. Both are deleted (D-100). What survives is the reason: a sweep must not lock out the one person who can repair it.
    D-7 The root participation row at renewal D-050 Extended across the renewed period; one row, no gap. Retired with the table (D-097).
    C-1 (largely) Organization-wide operational roles without downward inheritance D-035 The problem dissolves: the root owns the registry and the fees. What remains is a reporting question — see C-1. One unit now, so the shape is gone entirely (D-097).
    C-2 May units be reparented retroactively? D-036 Not at all — a unit that moves is a new unit. Moot with the units (D-097).

    How to answer¶

    Nothing here blocks anything. No question on this page is marked 🔴, and none has been since round 4: the model is settled enough that most answers here change data rather than structure, which is what makes them worth a moment rather than a decision meeting. Fourteen questions are left and every one of them is 🟢 — round 20 answered thirteen, and all six 🟡 on the page went with them, and round 22 added § K without closing one. Four of the five are left for a reason, not for a lack of priority: A-4's remainder is the organizational calendar, E-3 is a reading of this country's civil code, and F-9 and F-11 are one question seen twice — what a membership type is for. The fifth is F-34, and it is new: round 20 raised it by answering AT-2, and it is 🟢 for one reason only — the first real import is not next week. None of those five closes on a ruling alone, and F-34 least of all, because its answer is a person's name and a date rather than a rule.

    § K is the opposite kind of debt, and that is why it was worth filing nine questions at once. Six of its nine close on a ruling alone — K-1, K-2, K-3, K-4, K-6 and K-9 each need one sentence from somebody who knows the organization. K-5 and K-7 need a fact rather than a decision — whether an identity provider exists, and whether a list is right — and K-8 needs a name and a rhythm, which is the same shape F-34 has and the reason neither is answerable by a ruling.

    What this repository owes instead is two things, and neither of them is a question.
