    Open Questions¶

    Nothing is open on this page. Round 24 answered the three questions that were here — A-4's remainder, F-11 and K-10 — and each of the three sections that held one is now empty, so § A, § F and § K have joined § B, § C, § D, § E, § G, § Ga and § J among the sections this register has closed by answering them rather than by reversing them. The history of how the list got here is below, because a page that reads as empty is a page a reader will not believe.

    A question that has been answered is not kept here. Its entry is deleted; what remains is one row in § R. Resolved naming the question, the decision that settled it and what the answer cost, and the reasoning lives in that decision entry. Nothing is silently dropped: a row in § R carries a short anchor, so a link written years ago against F-7 still lands somewhere true — which is why the three anchors this page just lost are still here, in their § R rows, and why every page that linked them in the last twenty-four rounds still resolves.

    Zero open questions is not the same claim as nothing is unknown, and the three places the unknown still lives are all on this page or linked from it. § H lists the areas the schema has hooks for and nobody has designed — documents, consents, messaging, reporting, the wire, notifications. § I lists what the documentation is standing on without anyone having verified it. And a module page keeps its own ## Open issues list, which this register has never counted and never should: a module lists what it has not decided; this page lists what nobody has answered, and the two lists have different owners. What has changed is that the second list ran out of items, and the second thing this page used to carry — a legal review it described as owed — has been deleted rather than paid for, because D-205 rules that no such review will happen and that a design should not keep a debt it has decided not to discharge.

    How the list emptied, since "three" was itself the residue of three rounds. Round 20 answered thirteen of the seventeen questions then on this page, every 🟡 among them, and took the last three of § J; it also left one behind it, F-34, raised by an answer it gave rather than found on the page. Round 22 opened § K by letting module pages file questions here. Round 23 emptied § E, answered the nine of § K in the pages that filed them, answered three survivors, and filed one question of its own — K-10 — which is the shape of the residue round 24 inherited: three 🟢 questions, none of them changing a table, and one appendix page nobody had written.

    The section letters are names, not a sequence, and the register now has three of them left: § H, § R and § I. Sections B, C and D held only closed questions and are gone; § J went in round 20, § G (events) and § Ga (attributes) went with their rows carried into § R, § E (guardianship) went in round 23, and § A (blocking), § F (authorization) and § K (filed by a module page) went in round 24 the same way — and § K's id is kept here on purpose, because a dozen pages across the log link against it and a section that closes should not take their destinations with it. The modules they framed are unaffected — every one of those areas is still designed, still linked, and § H still points at each. The ids those sections held — B-2, C-5, D-9, EV-2, AT-4, E-6, and now A-4, F-11, K-10 — are still how § R means them, and E-6 is still the question it was when it was filed.

    § I lists every assumption the documentation is standing on; § How to answer says what this page still owes, now that it owes no answers.

    H. Not yet designed¶

    Each of these is referenced by the schema and has hooks left for it. Three rows carry a ✅ because the area they name is no longer waiting on anybody: events was designed in round 5; the medical record was answered out of existence in round 7 — what a reader who says "the medical record" is pointing at is the attribute vocabulary of data-model/09, one class of person data with no privileged subset (D-083); and fees and payments joined them in round 24, because the three questions that row had been carrying were its last and its architecture had been settled since round 6. The ✅ means nothing is waiting here, not this module is finished: the documents row is unbuilt with it, and so is everything else in the table.

    Area Hooks already in place Notes
    Events ✅ Designed — domain/07, data-model/08 Fifteen questions answered, and the three that were still open — EV-2, EV-4 and EV-6 — were answered in round 20. Nothing about events is open on this page.
    Fees and payments ✅ data-model/07-finance.md — architecture settled, and the policy that was open beside it is answered Nothing is open here now, and the sentence that used to read "three open questions" named the last three: the accounting export, the jurisdiction, and what a payment that does not fit settles. Round 24 closed them — nothing leaves this system to an accounting system and an external one is a later version's design (D-199); the issuer of an invoice is deployment configuration and the payer's identification is already person_identifier rows, so the design encodes no jurisdiction's paperwork rules (D-198); and a payment whose variable symbol resolves is attached in full, so the shortfall that used to be a reason a row could not be matched is now a number on a screen (D-196) — and the same round deleted the column membership.outstanding_balance that §3 had been recommending be deleted since round 6 (D-200). Invoices, meanwhile, turned out to be compulsory, which deleted invoice.issue rather than registering it (D-197). The sentence used to read nine open questions without naming one of them while the module page kept a twelve-item list of its own that nothing here could see. Round 22 closed the rest of that list by answering it: the fee is koruna (D-155), it buys membership and nothing else (D-156), there are no waivers (D-157), a refund happens at the bank (D-158), no proration rule (D-160), one live price (D-161). Round 23 closed three more of that list: a reduced fee is an amount_due written smaller (D-183), an invoice is a document a payer asks for (D-187), and every payable gets a variable symbol (D-181). Round 6 had already closed the architectural ones: money arrives by bank API, is requested by variable symbol + QR code, and there is no card gateway — and its fourth ruling, that unmatched money queues, was reversed this round (D-180) (D-075, D-076). Not one of the three is registered here, which is now a stated choice on the module page rather than an accident of this page's construction — though a finance question is registered here since round 23, and it is not one of the three: K-10, which page writes down the money permission codes this corpus already spends.
    Documents access_grant.scope_type = 'document'; the evidence-reference hook belonged to the removed requirement tables Needs: storage, versions, retention, consent-driven access.
    Consents No dependent.consent.* any more — a guardian at write may give and manage consents (D-074) Needs: template, per-activity consent, expiry, revocation, who may consent. No longer connected to a membership requirement (D-044). is_legal_decision_maker on the guardianship row is the existing hook for "who may sign" — and it is now the only place that distinction lives, since the right level cannot express it.
    Health records ✅ Designed — as domain/08 and data-model/09 There was no medical record to design. D-083 ruled that everything about a person except name, gender and age is one class of data, held as fields in a vocabulary the organization defines at runtime (D-084). Reading is person.view, writing is person.edit, and only the vocabulary needs a code of its own. What is left undesigned is policy around one class rather than a second module: what happens to the history of a value once the audit payloads expire. The other half of that sentence is now answered — whether attribute.define is a sufficient gate for a vocabulary free to invent a "health" category tomorrow, which round 20 settled by deciding there is no second gate: a definition is live at the save of whoever holds the code (D-141), and the widening is a stated property of that code rather than a queue in front of it. The trail side of the same worry is AT-4.
    Communication message.send_direct, message.broadcast (scoped) — shape decided, detail undesigned Shape decided by D-077: internal first, external channels are delivery; everyone may send direct; broadcast is one scoped permission; the recipient picks the channel. Needs: templates, opt-out granularity, retention, moderation. The rule against contacting minors directly was declined as a sender restriction and replaced by mirroring — see MSG-1.
    Reporting report.view and report.export, and nothing else — deliberately The historical queries in domain/05 are the starting set. Query 10 (members per year, as far back as the records reach) is the acceptance test. Reporting is the one place round 24 refused to answer the question in front of it: K-10 named report.finance.view among the money codes to promote, and it stayed deferred, because shipping a per-subject report code beside report.view would put both report shapes in one installation and 05d's rules list says pick one when reporting is designed (D-195). So a money report is gated today by the codes over the rows it reads — fee.view, payment.view — and the shape question is still this row's to answer.
    API and UI api_key and its four codes: who may call in is designed (05e, D-116); the resolver's at parameter exists partly for this The caller is designed; the wire is not. What a request looks like, how a secret is presented and carried, and how a session survives a login are API-layer work, and D-072 is the standing division — what is decided is only what may be stored: a hash, and nothing else. The UI side of the same decision is one permissions page answering a second question (§15's tabs). Calls going out were answered in round 20 without being designed: an outbound call is a person's act or this system's job, never a third kind of actor (F-27, D-146). Beside it, three things are settled rather than open — this system's own writers are an empty actor column read as the system acting on its own and named by a fixed label list (D-125), a lockout is a report plus a written procedure rather than a mechanism (D-126), and everything a caller sends over the wire is API work, not schema work.
    Notifications — Nothing in this design notifies anybody today, and that is a ruling rather than a gap (D-164, generalizing D-137): a report is a tab somebody opens and the audit trail is the control. The one person the authority model does point at survives this untouched, because D-126's break-glass actor is a procedure needing somebody, not a message needing a recipient. What this row still describes is an undesigned module, and its one dated shape is worth keeping if it is ever built — the renewal window makes it dated rather than status-driven, a job over in_renewal_window() rather than a watcher on a status column. Nothing waits on it: no rule in the register reads a send, because nothing is sent.
    Payment gateway — Answered, so this row is now narrower than it was. The organization collects via bank API, not a card gateway (D-075): no webhooks, no disputes, no processor reconciliation. What remains undesigned is the statement import itself — file formats, the idempotency key, and what happens to an account the bank revises.
    Multi-language — Answered for the attribute vocabulary by D-149: one language, no translation columns. The same reasoning covers the other words the database holds — nothing else on it was asked about separately — because a row holding two labels has to be asked which of the two the record is. The example this row was written with has itself gone: there is no unit left whose name could be translated (D-097).

    R. Resolved — moved to the decision log¶

    Kept here so a reader of this file knows the question was asked and where the answer lives, rather than finding it silently deleted.

    Was Question Settled by In one line
    A-1 The organizational structure D-035 One root; all sub-units nest arbitrarily beneath it. Reversed in round 10: there are no sub-units at all, and the unit is a scope rather than a row (D-097).
    K-1 Must a member row sit inside its group's own period? D-171 It must meet it: the resolver reads the intersection of the two periods and every membership is trimmed to the group's lifetime. The containment is therefore neither refused at the save nor assumed — it is read, which is why the register's save-time check stays none on purpose rather than by omission.
    K-2 Is an ended group hidden from pickers, and may it be reopened? D-172 Hidden, never reopened. Its valid_to is never cleared, so "a returning name is a new group" stays one rule instead of becoming two.
    K-3 Where is a claimed continuity between two groups recorded? D-173 Nowhere, chosen: "the same troop, 1998" is said in prose and gets no column. D-098's refusal of group-to-group edges stands, and this is the third place the corpus has put a continuity outside the database on purpose.
    K-4 Is person.gender needed at all, and with what value set? D-174, D-175 Kept and required, with male / female / other and unknown deleted — but not for reporting: it is how the system declines a verb. Erasure overwrites it with the names' grammatical form, which is a fourth value no screen offers.
    K-5 Does the organization have, or will it have, a national SSO identity provider? D-176, D-177 Google from the start, or a password. One external identity is one account, refused at the save — the first save-time check the credential uniqueness invariant ever had. Which methods exist is written in the program, not in a table: the principle is not "anything enumerable is a row" but "anything the organization may decide is a row".
    K-6 Should person.notes exist at all? D-191 Deleted, and erasure got stronger with it: person_attribute rows are deleted at erasure while notes survived a redaction. The boundary is that the person row holds no free text and rows about a person still do — a roster note, a status reason, a field conflict.
    K-7 Which tables does the audit obligation actually cover? D-184 All of them, so the question stops being a list. Only audit_log and access_log sit outside it, and the DELETE carve-out was re-cut to the two moments that survive the pipeline's deletion.
    K-8 Who reads the queue of money that cannot be matched, and how often? D-180 Nobody, because there is no queue — the question's premise was refused rather than answered. Most money that arrives has nothing to do with a member; an unmatched row is an absence like any other. The cost is named in the decision: a camp transfer can sit unattributed forever with no list anywhere that would show it.
    K-9 May staff create an account for a child, or only for an adult? D-179, D-178 Staff may open an account for anybody, and it waits at pending_verification until the person starts it. A child with no login is reached through the guardianship row, never through a shared credential.
    E-3 Guardian acting for an adult child D-185 Both routes the question offered end in the same place, so the machinery stays the one that exists: the guardianship stands until a party ends it, majority is not a party, and nothing happens at 18. A dependent may be an adult.
    F-9 Should membership_type stay a table with one row? D-186 Kept, and renamed in words if not in schema: settings wearing a vocabulary's clothes. The foreign key stays, one row stays, and the page now says what the table is for instead of apologising for it.
    F-34 Who writes the vocabulary the first import needs, and who holds attribute.define afterwards? D-189 One author, before the batch. The column map and the vocabulary are one artefact written in one sitting by one named person; the handover afterwards is an ordinary grant. No second import format, no queue, no status — which is the answer D-141 left without when it deleted the proposal stage.
    A-4 (remainder) Is that calendar the organization's calendar, or a date a database likes? D-193 There are two calendars and the design holds one of them. The membership year is 1 January to 31 December and is stored, in membership_type's expiry_month / expiry_day / renewal_grace_days; the program year — a group's term, a camp season, the collections — is September to August and is stored nowhere: no column, no constant, no rule, so a person_group holds whatever two dates a person types. D-047 and D-169 stand untouched, because both rule the membership half of a pair the register had assumed was one. The cost is a report question with two defensible answers: "how many members did 2026 have" and "who was in a group in 2026" are about overlapping-but-different periods, and no column anywhere says which one a reader meant. The surviving discipline is the half that was never about the date: both numbers are read from the row, and a job or front end containing the literal 60 is how the property is lost.
    F-11 Does the identity reviewer need to read the person record to settle a claim? D-194 The question was answered sideways: it came back as a requirement that permissions depend on each other, and the ruling is that nothing is implied by a permission — a grant allows or denies the one code it names, the resolver reads no dependency, and what is added is a screen that offers to save <resource>.view beside a write, taking which writes two rows. The worked-example reviewer in catalog B §11 now holds person.view, because its author wrote it and no rule put it there. Refused, with the reasons kept: auto-inheritance, because an implied code confers what no row names and D-163's ceiling reads a grant and nothing else; a save-time refusal, which was offered with a strictness question and answered not at save. The hole this leaves is on the page, not in a footnote: an api_key holder, a migration, or the twelve rows an event create writes all bypass the offer, so a reviewer office can hold person.merge with no read and nothing refuses it, nothing lists it, and no report will ever show it.
    K-10 Are the money permission codes still deferred? D-195, D-197 They are codes, and catalog A now has a money section to write them in; catalog B §10 loses its fee, payment and invoice rows, and its own admission that the payment row might not be honest as a deferred namespace goes with it. Two things the answer did not promote, both recorded rather than tidied: report.finance.view stayed deferred on purpose — promoting it would ship two report shapes at once, and 05d's rules list says pick one when reporting is designed, so the finance module spends its six codes and does not get to decide reporting's shape — and invoice.issue, promoted by this answer, was deleted by the next one, because invoices turned out to be compulsory and a permission nobody can decline is not a permission.
    A-1 The unit type vocabulary D-054 Not seeded — org_unit_type is runtime data; only the root type ships. Deleted with the table (D-097).
    B-1 How much legacy data, in what shape? D-056 Large, with legacy member numbers — exact matches, so review is a work queue. Fully answered in round 8: thousands of roster rows, no payment data at all, so every historical row is a participation (D-090). Round 10 moved the noun: the participation table is gone, so an attendance row with no dates imports as a dated person_group_member row or nothing at all (D-097, D-098).
    D-9 Is household load-bearing at all? D-057 No — removed entirely. Addresses are temporal person_contact rows.
    F-1 Which permissions and roles does the organization need? D-055 The catalog stays large and granular; it is a menu, not a checklist.
    — Eligibility for an event — a separate rule engine? D-060 No — eligibility is capability. event.register_self at contribute, via audiences.
    — Is unanswered a stored attendance value? D-059 No — absence of a row. Two stored values: attending, not_attending.
    — Is a weekly meeting an event? D-058 No. Round 10 moved the answer: a Thursday group is a person_group with a dated roster, and unit_participation is deleted (D-097). Still no recurrence, no series table, no occurrence table, and no row that says who attended a given evening.
    — One owning unit per event, or several? D-058 Exactly one. Reversed in round 10b: nothing owns an event — event.org_unit_id is deleted and the people connected to it are named by selectors that bind to it (D-102).
    — Are organizers a grant or a roster? D-062 A roster on the event — labelled and ordered. Grants stay separate.
    — Do unit roles reach the unit's own events? D-063 Yes — an event is an artifact of its unit, not a child. Reversed in round 10b: the artifact rule and can_event() are deleted, and nothing reaches an event from a unit (D-102). D-031's finding survives as the safeguarding property stated in the authorization ERD.
    — Does an event have a status (draft, published)? D-064 No status column. Visibility is the permission model; cancellation is a fact.
    — What happens when a full event gets another registration? D-065 Waitlisted for self-service; admitted for organizers; the overrun reported.
    — Does paying confirm a place? D-066 Yes when the event has a fee; most events are free and confirm immediately.

    — May an event form collect medical detail? D-061 Effectively yes: answers are read by that event's organizers, and no event permission reaches the health record, so the form is the only route for a camp leader to learn an allergy at all. A recorded widening, restated after D-074 removed the namespace the original wording relied on.
    A-2 Permissions conditioned on membership status D-039 Yes, enforced by the resolver, with guardianship and ownership carved out. Round 10 deleted the gate (D-100): membership is now a term a grant's expression may name, not a precondition the resolver checks.
    A-3 Activate on payment or on approval? D-040 On payment; the fee expires yearly and a dated renewal window replaces a grace status.
    A-4 Background checks D-043 None are recorded in this system.
    A-4 The expiry date D-047 1 January; the organizational year runs with the calendar.
    A-4 The renewal window length D-048 60 days, stored as a parameter rather than compiled in.
    C-1 May the root read into its sub-units? D-049 No. scope_exposure is the only crossing; no reporting channel is built. Formally closed in round 9, and D-094 gave it a mirror: imported events belong to the root, so a unit's own event list contains history it did not run. Round 10 dissolved the question: there is no sub-unit to read into and scope_exposure is deleted (D-097).
    C-3 Is a reused unit number the same unit? D-096 A new unit, unless someone records continuity as successor_of. A code is a typed label, not an identity. Moot with the table (D-097).
    C-4 Does appointing someone create a role assignment? D-091, restated by D-098, D-100, D-101 No. Round 10 redrew the line the answer drew: the union-of-three-arms became an expression language (+ - *), a group became a hand-stated dated roster, and an audience became the rule. Appointing still creates nothing, and there are no positions left to appoint. Rule-filled roles went to F-12, which round 10 closed no.
    C-5 What creates the units the import's rows point at? D-092, D-093, D-094 Nothing, because the rows name no units. People arrive as bounded memberships, events as root-owned events with registrations. The historical record has no sub-units in it.
    C-6 Should allows_minors refuse a minor's position as it refuses their participation? D-095 Neither: units carry no restrictions and the flag is removed. The control is that nobody joins a unit or takes a position themself — a counselor writes the row. Positions went in round 10 (D-100); the control is now person_group.member.manage.
    D-6 How may the membership gate be waived? D-051 A person-level system.gate_exempt permission, in addition to the role flag. Both are deleted (D-100). What survives is the reason: a sweep must not lock out the one person who can repair it.
    D-7 The root participation row at renewal D-050 Extended across the renewed period; one row, no gap. Retired with the table (D-097).
    C-1 (largely) Organization-wide operational roles without downward inheritance D-035 The problem dissolves: the root owns the registry and the fees. What remains is a reporting question — see C-1. One unit now, so the shape is gone entirely (D-097).
    C-2 May units be reparented retroactively? D-036 Not at all — a unit that moves is a new unit. Moot with the units (D-097).
    D-1 Fee per person or per household? D-037 Per person, flat, yearly.
    D-2 One open membership, or several of different types? D-037, D-041 One type, so one open membership, enforced person-wide. The participation half of D-041 is moot (D-097).
    D-3 Age-out timing D-038 Retired — there is no age-out.
    D-4 Child → adult transition D-038 Retired — there are no membership categories to transition between.
    D-5 Membership type vocabulary D-037 One type, member; counselor is a position, not a type.
    E-1 Default rights when a guardianship is created D-046 write for a self-registered child's guardian; view for staff-created and imported rows; chosen at creation when recorded manually. D-074 turned the answer from a code bundle into one level, which is why the decision still stands on its original wording.
    F-2 Polymorphic scope_id vs a grantable_item supertable your answer, round 6 Polymorphic — scope_type plus scope_id, as currently designed.
    — The bulk scope-exposure tool D-045 Removed once audiences existed to cover the case it was for.
    — What a membership request must contain D-044 Identity and the fee. The whole requirement mechanism is gone.
    — Whether grants may target groups D-042 Yes — built-in rule-evaluated audiences plus admin-managed named groups.
    E-2 What happens to guardianship at majority? D-069 Nothing — no job, no queue, no computed date. A person ends the row. (Reverses D-024.)
    F-3 Must a high-authority role assignment have an end date? D-071 No. The membership gate plus a human review report; the earlier "yes" recommendation is withdrawn. Both halves moved: the gate is deleted (D-100) and the report became a tab (D-109).
    F-4 Should person.admin subsume merge and redact? D-068 Moot — there are no per-resource admin permissions. The choice moved into reviewable role contents. (Reverses D-007.)
    — Should every resource expose an admin permission? D-068 No. Sixteen codes and the resolver's prefix match are gone; roles are the bundle.
    EV-1 Does an event need an age ceiling? D-070 No. The grant is the rule; a future ceiling would be a built-in audience, not a column.
    EV-5 Should event.manage_participants split? this round Yes — event.presence.mark at contribute, one level below the roster. Round 10 note: the split stands; the level does not (D-099).
    D-8 Should unit_participation reference the membership? D-097 Retired with the table. The refusal is still live reasoning: participation had to survive without a membership, which is why person_group_member is keyed on a person. See data-model/03 §9.
    F-12 May a role be held by a rule, as an audience is? D-100 No. An office is held by a named person and confers nothing; a group is company; a grant is what reaches a set. The office record stays the one dated, human-readable answer.
    — Is there more than one unit, and does anything belong to it? D-097 No. org_unit and friends, unit_participation, scope_exposure and the traversal functions are deleted; the unit is a scope, not a row. Two-way door: no.
    — What is a group? D-098 person_group: named, dated, states its members, has a lifetime, grants nothing by itself. A group is company; a selector picks people; a grant is what reaches them.
    — What does a grant do, and how much? D-099 Allows or denies one permission at one scope, for a period. Allowed iff some live allow applies and no live deny applies. No levels, no rank integer, no locality.
    — Is a role a bundle of permissions, and is membership a precondition? D-100 Neither. role_assignment → role_holder; role_permission, positions and the membership gate are deleted; the test moved into the expression (MEMBERS * ROLE(x)). Posture: default-on → opt-in.
    — How is an audience stored? D-101 As an expression tree over named sets — + - *, brackets, fully-bracketed canonical form, cycles refused at save, the language seeded as selector_type. Semantic equality given up (see F-14).
    — Who owns an event, and whom does a grant about it reach? D-102 Nobody owns it. event.org_unit_id, can_event() and the artifact rule are deleted; four attendance tiers and ORGANIZERS bind to the item, and the answer-view grant is a seeded template instantiated per event.
    — May a rule be reused by name? D-103 Yes: AUDIENCE(code), referenced by code, one namespace with roles and groups, cycles refused across audiences at save, and an audience named by a live grant may not be ended. Its past reach is not reconstructable (see F-17). Amended in the same round: the same refusal now extends to a group named by a live grant — see assumption 63.
    F-19 A permission code is a row, prose names codes, and nothing checks the two D-104 The class is real — four instances in ten rounds, in two different registries.
