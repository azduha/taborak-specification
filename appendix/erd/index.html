    Appendix — Entity Relationship Diagrams¶

    This appendix describes relationships — what points at what, how many, and whether the link is temporal. Field-level definitions live in the data-model documents, each in the one that owns the entity, and are deliberately not repeated here.

    Diagrams of the schema in ../data-model/00-conventions.md. Split by subject area so each stays readable; the areas connect through person, which appears in all of them.

    Notation: → is a foreign key pointing at the referenced table. ⇒ is a polymorphic reference (no database-level foreign key). Temporal tables are marked [T].

    1a. Personal attributes — everything that is not a name, a gender or an age¶

    Key point: these three tables are the whole of what the system knows about a person beyond name, gender and age. There is no health table beside them and no privileged corner inside them: D-083 removed the category and D-084 put an organization-maintained vocabulary in its place. Reading is person.view, writing is person.edit, and only the vocabulary needs a code of its own (attribute.define). Fields and rules: ../data-model/09-attributes.md.

    None of the three is [T], and the no beside them in §7 is a decision, not an omission. A person_attribute row is the current belief, overwritten in place, and audit_log holds the sequence of beliefs, values included — which is why the trail is guarded as person data is (the permission catalog §8). This corpus gives periods to things that are true over an interval — a membership, an address, a position, a guardian's authority — and a descriptive value is not one of those: it is a statement made now about whatever it is about, with a timestamp on the assertion. A valid_from here would ask a volunteer when an allergy began, which is a question the organization is not competent to record and has no rule that could use (D-085). The cost is named rather than left out: the history of a value lives in the one control D-081 calls the corpus's weakest, and §7 lists it as the accepted risk. It is also a deliberate departure from guardianship two sections below, which keeps its current value and its history in business tables — because authority is asked "what was it on that date?" as a routine part of answering whether an act was permitted, and an allergy list is not.

    The two vocabulary tables are non-temporal for a smaller reason of their own: a code leaves service by status, not by a period, so a value recorded against a retired option keeps meaning what it meant on the day it was chosen. Retiring therefore never touches a fact — only redacting a person erases one (../domain/08-person-data.md).

    Three points that matter:

    • membership_request has two person references: person_id (the child, who would become a member) and submitted_by_person_id (the parent, who applied). Collapsing them makes the audit trail false.
    • unit_participation.person_id points at person, not membership. This is the deliberate trade-off that makes the 1990s import possible — an imported roster records who took part, and frequently no membership row exists to point at: ../data-model/03-membership.md.
    • membership_type exists with exactly one row, and category, min_age, max_age and requires_background_check are all gone. Membership is ageless (D-038); age is read from person.birth_date where it matters, which is guardianship and position eligibility, not membership.

    Key points:

    • role_assignment and access_grant are the same idea at two granularities: authority held for a period, in a scope, granted by someone. They share scope_type, the level model, and the resolver.
    • There is no wildcard permission. A check for X.y needs an exact grant of X.y, or system.admin. The old <resource>.admin prefix match was removed with its sixteen codes (D-068, reversing D-007); "can do everything about events" is a role listing all thirteen event.* codes. Note access_level.admin (rank 40) is a grant level and survives — same word, different mechanism.
    • Deny is absolute. An explicit revoked at any scope is checked first and beats everything — including the admin level, ownership, and guardianship. "Most specific scope wins" applies only to allows (step 3).
    • The guardianship channel is entered by shape, not by permission name (D-079): the check is about someone other than the actor, and the channel answers with allows only. A sufficient right_level returns true; no row, none, or too low a level returns nothing at all and the actor's own grants are tried. A prohibition is therefore a revoked grant, never a low level.
    • Authority never inherits downward. A role assigned over a parent unit does not cover its sub-units. unit_ancestors() is not called by the resolver at all. A sub-unit's authority reaches outward only through a scope_exposure row, and the row is written by the sub-unit granting coverage over itself — the crossing is offered from below, never imposed from above. The sole exception is system_admin. But note what the root owns: the person registry, the memberships and the fees live at the root unit, so an organization-scoped role acting on them is working at its own scope, not reaching down (D-035).
    • The membership gate sits on the role tier and nowhere else (D-039). Ownership and guardianship return before it, which is what keeps a lapsed person able to reach their own renewal screen and a lapsed parent able to act for their child. Tiers a–c are not gated: a grant is an explicit act, not an inherited one.
    • A grantee may be an audience. access_grant.person_id and audience_id are mutually exclusive. Built-in audiences (everyone, all_members, the participants of a unit) are rules evaluated at check time; named groups are stored lists and carry the stale-group problem knowingly (D-042).
    • scope_exposure points at a specific role_assignment, not at a role, so coverage is per-person and expires with the assignment. There is no bulk tool and no bulk: marker (D-045). The role tier is the table's only consumer: an exposure extends an assignment, and a direct access_grant carries no assignment for one to attach to, so the unit-grant tier matches exactly and stops (D-078).
    • scope_id is polymorphic (no FK) so a new grantable item type is a config row, not a migration. Accepted cost, with mandatory mitigations.

    See ../data-model/05-authorization.md and ../domain/06-authorization-model.md.

    6. Temporal pattern, illustrated¶

    Every [T] table above uses the same shape. The returning-member case shows why:

    person 100 — Jan Novák
│
├─ membership #1   member   1990-09-01 ──────────────► 2010-06-30   terminated
│    unit_participation  Pack 12  1990-09-01 ──────► 1998-06-30
│    unit_participation  Pack 31  1998-09-01 ──────────────► 2010-06-30
│
│      ← 14-year gap, no rows. Visible as data, not inferred. →
│
├─ membership #2   member   2024-10-01 ──────────────► NULL (open, current)
│    unit_participation  [ROOT]    2024-10-01 ──────► NULL   ← created with the
│    unit_participation  Pack 5    2024-10-01 ──────► NULL     membership, so the
│    position_assignment  scoutmaster, Pack 5  2025-01-01 ──► NULL   roster is uniform
│    role_assignment  unit_leader @ Pack 5     2025-01-01 ──► 2025-12-31
│                      (requires_membership = false → survives a lapse, D-039;
│                       the person-level alternative is system.gate_exempt, D-051)
│
├─ guardianship → child A    2015-03-01 ──────────► NULL
│    guardianship_permission  right_level = write      (source='self' → write)
└─ guardianship → child B    2018-07-01 ──────────► NULL
     guardianship_permission  right_level = view       (court-restricted)

    Note the two membership rows have the same type. They differ only in period and end reason — this person was a member twice, and was a child and then an adult throughout. The old diagram called them child and adult; that was the framing the ageless decision replaced (D-038).

    Questions this shape supports, all of which fail against a design with person.is_member:

    • Was this person a member on 1998-06-01?
    • How many years of membership, excluding the gap?
    • Which unit were they in during 2003?
    • How old were they when they joined the second time? — answered from person.birth_date, which the membership rows never copy.
    • Who are the returning members — more than one membership with a gap between?

    Every one of them is answered as of a date, and an as-of date must be applied to every join in the answer, not only to the root row — a period joined without it leaks the present into the past. That is what the [T] marking on the tables above exists to make visible.

    7. Table inventory¶

    One entry per entity: what it is, what it references, how many may exist, and whether it is temporal. ⇒ marks a polymorphic reference with no foreign key. Entries that exist only to join two other entities say so.

    Read the [T] column as a decision, not a default. A no is normally a lookup table, a definition, or an append-only trail — the kinds of thing that have no period to carry. Exactly one no in this inventory is a choice about a person's data rather than a lack of one: person_attribute, and the reason is written out under its own block below and in §1a.

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]

    That last no is the decision, and it is the reason this row spells it out. Values are overwritten in place and audit_log — carrying the values themselves — is the only history: this is the one place in the schema where "what did we believe in 2019?" is answered from the trail rather than from business data, and D-081 records the trail as discipline rather than guarantee. The trade was made with that in view and is reversible in one entry (D-085, ../data-model/09-attributes.md §3). The two vocabulary tables carry status instead of periods, which is what lets a code be retired without touching a fact; only redacting a person erases values, and a redacted person still holding attribute rows, or still having their values readable in the trail, means the redaction did not happen (../data-model/09-attributes.md §8).

    Events

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]

    Reference vocabularies — reason_code, access_level, grant_scope_type, identifier_scheme, permission, org_unit_type. Each is named by a value held elsewhere rather than pointing at anything, and each exists so that a vocabulary is data the organization can see rather than a list inside the application. attribute_definition is the same kind of thing in spirit and a different shape in fact — it has a curation lifecycle, a child table, and rows pointing back at it — so it is inventoried above rather than here.

    event carries no [T] — it has no valid_from / valid_to, because an event is a dated occasion rather than a period-tracked record. Its lifecycle is starts_at / ends_at / cancelled_at, and it has no status column at all (D-064). event_registration likewise stores no period: a person is attending, or they are not, and "unanswered" is the absence of a row (D-059).

    Removed since the first draft, listed so the inventory stays honest: requirement_code and membership_request_requirement (nothing gates a request — D-044); unit_membership (renamed unit_participation — D-041); and every background-check structure (D-043).

    [T] = temporal, carries valid_from / valid_to. A no says it does not; where the reason for not is a decision about someone's data rather than the nature of the table, the row says so — which in this inventory is person_attribute, and nowhere else.

    8. Foreign key summary¶

    Tables with no FK on a column, and why:

    Column Why no FK
    access_grant.scope_id Polymorphic by design. Validated by grant_scope_type + orphan checks.
    access_log.scope_id Same.
    access_log.authorization_ref_id Points at one of three grant tables depending on authorization_channel.
    access_log.entity_id Polymorphic target of the audited action.
    field_conflict.entity_id Polymorphic; recorded alongside entity_table.
    audit_log.record_id Polymorphic; recorded alongside table_name.

    access_grant.grantee is the one mutual-exclusion rather than missing-FK case: exactly one of person_id / audience_id is set — never both, never neither. The two columns are nullable, so nothing but that rule says which one a grant means; a grant naming neither is meaningless and a grant naming both is ambiguous about whom in the audience it speaks for.

    Every other column ending in _id has a real foreign key with ON DELETE RESTRICT. No table anywhere uses ON DELETE CASCADE — see ../data-model/00-conventions.md.
