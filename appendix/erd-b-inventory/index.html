    Appendix — ERD: the temporal pattern, the table inventory, the foreign keys¶

    How a dated row is written so that an answer survives the thing it describes, then one entry per entity in the whole system, and every reference between them.

    Section numbers are shared across the three parts and never renumbered, because other pages cite them by number and their anchors are built from them: erd.md holds §1–§4, the diagrams of the people — identity and attributes, the unit's groups and offices, membership, guardianship; erd-a-authorization.md holds §5–§5b, the whole authorization answer, its tables and the audience expression tree; erd-b-inventory.md holds §6–§8, the temporal pattern, the table inventory and the foreign key summary.

    6. Temporal pattern, illustrated¶

    Every [T] table above uses the same shape. The returning-member case shows why:

    flowchart TB
    P["person 100 — Jan Novák"]
    M1["membership #1 · member<br/>1990-09-01 → 2010-06-30 · terminated"]
    GAP["14-year gap<br/>no rows; visible as data, not inferred"]
    M2["membership #2 · member<br/>2024-10-01 → open"]
    G1["person_group_member · oddílová rada<br/>1996-09-01 → 2004-06-30"]
    G2["person_group_member · registre 2025<br/>2024-10-01 → 2025-12-31"]
    RH["role_holder · treasurers<br/>2025-01-01 → open"]
    AU["audience · MEMBERS * ROLE(treasurers)<br/>an expression, evaluated live"]
    AG["access_grant · payment.record<br/>allow, at the unit<br/>2025-01-01 → open"]
    GA["guardianship → child A<br/>2015-03-01 → open<br/>right_level: write"]
    GB["guardianship → child B<br/>2018-07-01 → open<br/>right_level: view"]
    P --> M1
    M1 --> GAP --> M2
    P --> G1
    P --> G2
    P --> RH
    P --> GA
    P --> GB
    RH -.->|"named by code inside"| AU
    AU -.->|"aimed at by"| AG
    RH ==>|"the office reaches them only<br/>through that grant"| AG

    Note the two membership rows have the same type. They differ only in period and end reason: this person was a member twice, and a membership carries no age — the type is a fee and renewal parameter, not a kind of person (D-038).

    Where the first row came from is not drawn. A 1990s roster row states a period of membership, and since D-093 one reviewed batch may turn that stated period into membership #1 itself: both ends dated, data_confidence low, the batch id on it, no fee_obligation and a balance that stays absent rather than zero, because the corpus carries no money in it (D-090). Two shapes remain forbidden, and they are the two the ban was ever really about: an open-ended imported membership, and one inferred from an attendance row. A closed period gates nothing in the present; an open one hands authority to whoever signs up under that name tomorrow. A people batch writes the membership and nothing else — a membership has no twin row beside it (D-097) — and the group rows are written by somebody who knows the group exists (../domain/03-identity-and-legacy.md §7).

    Questions this shape supports, none of which a flag on the person row could answer:

    • Was this person a member on 1998-06-01?
    • How many years of membership, excluding the gap?
    • Which groups were they in during 2003? — person_group_member answers it, because those rows are dated rather than derived from a rule that could change its mind.
    • Who held the office of treasurers in 2023? — the same shape of answer, one role_holder row, which is why F-12 is answered no: an office is held by a named person.
    • Would that grant have reached them on that date? — holder row, expression and grant are all evaluated as of the same at, and none of the three may be stored as a materialized list (D-101).
    • How old were they when they joined the second time? — answered from person.birth_date, which the membership rows never copy.
    • Who are the returning members — more than one membership with a gap between?

    Every one of them is answered as of a date, and an as-of date must be applied to every join in the answer, not only to the first one — a period joined without it leaks the present into the past. That is what the [T] marking on the tables above exists to make visible.

    7. Table inventory¶

    One entry per entity: what it is, what it references, how many may exist, and whether it is temporal. ⇒ marks a polymorphic reference with no foreign key. Entries that exist only to join two other entities say so. A table that no longer exists is not here: this is the inventory of what the schema is now, and every gone name has its row in removed-concepts.md.

    Read the [T] column as a decision, not a default. A no is normally a lookup table, a definition, or an append-only trail — the kinds of thing that have no period to carry. Exactly one no in this inventory is a choice about a person's data rather than a lack of one: person_attribute, and the reason is written out under its own block below and in §1a.

    Identity

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    person The human being. Holds identity only — no status, no role, no membership Nothing, except itself: merged_into_person_id ⇒ self One per human, effectively; a merged row survives as a tombstone and is never deleted. The pointer is never rewritten forward when the survivor is itself absorbed, so chains persist and resolve_person() walks them (D-087) no
    person_name One name a person has had, and when person Many per person; one current row per name_type yes
    account The ability to log in. Optional, and independent of membership person Zero or many per person over a lifetime; at most one live row no
    account_credential One authentication method's secret or external handle account — deliberately not person Many per account; one current row per external issuer and reference pair yes
    person_contact An email, phone or postal address, as a dated row rather than a column person Many per person; one current primary per type and purpose yes
    person_identifier An identifier issued outside the system person; identifier_scheme for its scheme Many per person; (scheme, value) unique among current rows yes
    identifier_scheme The vocabulary of identifier schemes Nothing Lookup table, one row per scheme no
    identity_hypothesis A proposal that two person rows are the same human person twice; the reviewer is a person At most one live hypothesis per pair; a rejected pair may be re-proposed no
    person_merge The record that one row was absorbed into another, and how to undo it person twice; identity_hypothesis, optional One per merge performed; a reversal is recorded on the same row no
    field_conflict One value a merge could not decide between two candidates — raised for either half of the record, an identity field or a descriptive value (§1a) person_merge; ⇒ (entity_table, entity_id) — polymorphic by nature Many per merge no

    Personal attributes — everything known about a person other than name, gender and age (§1a)

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    attribute_definition One thing the organization collects about people: the code, its label, its type, and whether several values may be current at once Nothing One code per normalized spelling — Allergy and allergy are one row; statuses live and retired only — a definition is live at its save (D-141) — and retiring keeps the row and its values no
    attribute_option One allowed value of a one_of definition attribute_definition One row per code per definition; a retired option is never re-meaning, because values stored against it are historical facts no
    person_attribute The value one person currently has for one code person, attribute_definition, and attribute_option when the type is one_of One current row per person and code; more only where the definition sets allow_multiple, which no constraint can check (it reads another table) no — deliberately

    That last no is the decision, and it is the reason this row spells it out. Values are overwritten in place and audit_log — carrying the values themselves, for 24 months — is the only history. This is the one place in the schema where "what did we believe in 2019?" is answered from the trail rather than from business data, and the answer reaches two years back and no further (D-088): the honest description of this trade is no long history, not merely no temporal columns, and the trail carrying it is discipline rather than guarantee (D-081). Why this is written out on one row of an inventory: if a case ever needs the long version, the fix is temporal values on this table, not a longer trail — a decision worth one entry, and the argument for it is in D-085 and ../data-model/09-attributes.md §3. The two vocabulary tables carry status instead of periods, which is what lets a code be retired without touching a fact; only redacting a person erases values, and a redacted person still holding attribute rows, or still having their values readable in the trail, means the redaction did not happen (../data-model/09-attributes.md §8).

    Groups and offices — what the organization module holds now (§2), plus the two office tables it shares with authorization, drawn in §5a and specified in ../data-model/05-authorization.md §3 and §4

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    person_group A named set of people with a lifetime — a crew, a registration team, everyone who ever ran the camp. Confers nothing: authority arrives only through a grant aimed at the group Nothing. Its name is unique together with role.code and a set audience's code, which no constraint can express, so the write path carries the rule and an integrity check asserts the write path still does One row per name. Ended by valid_to, never deleted while member rows exist, and ending it is refused while a live grant — or a live audience reachable from one — names it, because EVERYONE - GROUP(x) with an emptied group denies everybody yes
    person_group_member Join entity: this person was in this group for that period. Written by a person, never derived from a rule person_group; person — person, not membership, so a 1998 roster row does not have to invent a membership in order to be true; and person as the one who added them Many per group at once; no overlapping periods for one person in one group, over all rows including ended ones, which a hand-written exclusion constraint enforces and the write path only explains (../data-model/02-organization.md §2) yes
    role An office — treasurers, registrar, counselors. A definition, valid always; the period lives on the holder row. It carries no permissions and confers nothing: the codes an office holds are the live grants whose audience names ROLE(x) Nothing. Its code joins the same one namespace as group names, and every reserved selector_type.code is refused against it One row per code; deleting one is refused while holder rows exist, so an office cannot be emptied in one save no
    role_holder Who holds an office for what period — one named person, never a rule: an office record that cannot answer "who held this in 2023" is not one (F-12) role; person twice — the holder, and the person who appointed them At most one open row per person and role; no overlap over all rows; nothing forces a row to end, and the standing report is the control (D-071) yes

    Membership

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    membership_type The fee and renewal parameters. One row, member, kept as a table so the vocabulary can grow Nothing Exactly one row today (D-037) yes
    membership One period of membership, with its own ending person, membership_type; itself via renewed_from_membership_id Many per person over a lifetime; one open at a time, ever yes
    membership_request An application. Two person references on purpose: the subject and the submitter person twice, membership_type, and membership twice (what it produced, what it renews). It names no group and no unit beyond the unit itself: which set a new member lands in is a later write on that set, not a term of the application (D-098) At most one open request per person no

    Relationships

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    guardianship One adult's responsibility for one dependent, carrying one right_level person twice — never the same person twice One current row per pair; unlimited past rows; a dependent may have any number of guardians at once yes
    guardianship_permission The temporal history of that row's right level — one value with three choices, changed over time (D-074) guardianship Exactly one open row per guardianship; its value is what guardianship.right_level copies yes

    Authorization — the tables are drawn in §5a and the expression in §5b

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    permission One coded capability — the only thing a check asks for, and a grant names exactly one Nothing One row per code; the code never changes. applies_to says whether the code may be granted at the unit or also on an item, checked when the grant is saved; right_kind exists for exactly one customer, the guardianship channel (D-099) no
    grant_scope_type The registry of things a grant may be written on, and the table its id lives in. unit is its only non-item row — which is where the unit now lives Nothing One row per scope kind: unit, event, person_group, role, and document deferred. Adding a grantable thing is a row here rather than a migration. api_key is deliberately absent: a key is a grantee and never a thing a permission is granted on, so "rotate the accounting key and no other" is not a writable grant at this scale, and the debt is recorded rather than hidden (D-122) no
    access_grant The only table that grants. A grantee, a scope, one permission, an effect, a period Exactly one grantee of three kinds: person, audience or api_key — never two, never none (D-116); grant_scope_type and ⇒ scope_id, absent exactly at unit; permission; person as grantor, delegator and revoker — always a person, including on a grant whose grantee is a key At most one live allow per grantee, scope and permission, counted once per grantee kind so the one rule is three partial unique indexes — denies sit outside all three on purpose, because allow-beside-deny is the only way to say "the unit's events, except this one"; a delegation must carry an end date, and a grant naming a key is never a delegation and so carries no mandatory expiry (D-116, D-120) yes
    audience A set of people a grant may name — an expression over named sets, stored as a tree (D-101) Its own operand rows; code is optional and is the only thing another audience may name (D-103); an operand is one named row of the registry, never a stored roster (D-105) One row per audience; the tree is stored exactly as the writer wrote it and its height is bounded by nothing — the save-time cycle walks are the only structural guard, and nothing bounds what resolving one costs, which D-112 accepts at the stated size rather than leaves unaddressed (D-081); an audience a live grant names is never deleted — it is flagged deprecated, hidden or disabled, the first two changing no answer and the third resolving to the empty set on purpose, as an audited write that prints the rules it touches (D-109) — a flag is a state, not part of a compilation, so resolution reads it live, once per request, and disabled means now rather than next time the rules are compiled (D-114); an ended audience reaches nobody, so ending one is refused while a live grant names it yes
    audience_operand Join entity: one row of one audience's expression tree — a node (operator, left_id, right_id) or a leaf (selector_kind, ⇒ selector_arg_id), never both audience, denormalized onto every row so one audience's tree is one indexed fetch; itself, parent_id on the child; selector_type; and ⇒ a role, person_group, audience, person or event with no foreign key — D-011's accepted trade-off, extended by D-101 One row per node or leaf; a node never belongs to another audience than its parent's; the forest is acyclic, checked on save no
    selector_type The registry of operands the language has: the reserved word, whether it takes an argument, whether its argument-less form binds to the item being checked, and what type the argument names Nothing Thirteen seeded rows — the thirteen operands of the language, including PERSON, which is what lets an expression name a single human (D-105), and including OLDER(n) and YOUNGER(n), the two whose argument_type is a whole number of years rather than a table and whose set moves with no write at all, because they read person.birth_date on the date being checked (D-107). Six argument_type values, five of them tables; the operators are + - *. Every row is_system and organization-read-only, and every row both seeded and shipped — a code with no evaluator arm is a deployment bug, not a held feature (D-104) no
    api_key A caller that is nobody: one registered integration, installed by a named person, holding one live secret that is shown once and kept only as a hash. Not an account (a way for a person to log in), not an office (held by a person, confers nothing), not a selector (no expression can name one) — and so a grantee in exactly the sense a person is (D-116) person as owner_person_id — a contact, not a channel, never read to decide an answer; person as created_by, required, because attribution for an act is not a dependency of the authority; person as revoker. Grants name it from access_grant.api_key_id, a real foreign key and not a third reference-less key (§8) One row per integration; code and secret_hash each unique among live rows — the second a lookup before it is a constraint; valid_to optional, absent meaning current, and nothing forces a key to end: the report is the control (D-120). Rotating replaces the hash in place, so there is no window in which both secrets work and no history of past ones (D-119). Nothing at all is derived from created_by, and no key is ever inside an expression — the registry keeps its thirteen rows and none of them names a key (D-117) yes
    access_log The audit trail — who did what, by what authority, with what result person as actor and as subject, or api_key as actor instead of a person — the two are never both set; ⇒ authorization_ref_id naming access_grant or guardianship_permission — the enum holds exactly those two channels, so an older row reading role or system names a channel this model cannot read, which is deliberate (D-100); ⇒ entity_id Append-only to the application; rows are deleted at 24 months by a scheduled job, which is the third exception to "nothing is hard-deleted" and the opposite shape to audit_log, whose rows live forever with emptied payloads (D-145, against D-088) no

    role and role_holder are inventoried under Groups and offices above, where the module keeps them; both are drawn in the authorization diagram, because that is where a reader meets what they do — which is nothing at all, on their own.

    Audit and import

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    audit_log The row-level trail of changes to business tables, before and after values included until the payload window passes ⇒ (table_name, record_id) — polymorphic; person as actor, and on whose behalf, or api_key as actor instead of a person — the two actor columns are mutually exclusive and on_behalf_of is never set on a row whose actor is a key (D-121); a row naming neither is this system acting on its own, a complete answer to who did this, and the trail's own actor_label then says which writer it was, from a fixed list rather than from free text (../data-model/00-conventions.md §7a, D-125) One row per change; the row is never deleted, but a scheduled payload reduction empties the values 24 months after writing (D-088) no
    redaction_log That a redaction happened to a person, by whom, and to what person; the person who requested and the person who performed it One per redaction performed no
    import_batch One arrival of historical data, where it stands, which half of the corpus it carries (people or events), and the raw-column→code map a person read before it applied person as creator, applier and reverting party One per batch; the map lives here as one object rather than per staged row, because it is one artefact a human reads top to bottom and the codes it names arrive live (D-090, D-092); staging rows may be truncated once applied no
    staging_import A raw source row, verbatim, with its file and line import_batch One per source row per batch no
    staging_import_row That row parsed, plus what the parse could not decide, and what it eventually created staging_import; person as resolver; and what it created — person and, on a people batch, the membership whose both ends the source stated (D-093: a stated period may be imported, an open-ended one may not and one inferred from attendance may not), and on an events batch the event and the event_registration One per staged row no

    Finance (provisional)

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    fee_schedule What may be charged, for what kind of period membership_type, optional — absent means it applies to any type. A schedule is organization-wide by construction: there is no unit row to attach one to (D-097) As the organization defines yes
    fee_obligation What one person owes for exactly one thing person (who it is for), fee_schedule, and exactly one of membership, membership_request, event_registration Many per person; the target is never two and never none. An imported membership creates none, and its outstanding_balance is NULL rather than zero, because zero would be a claim the export does not support (D-093) no
    payment Money that arrived person as payer — deliberately not the person the fee is for One per recorded receipt no
    payment_allocation Join entity: this payment counted towards this obligation, by how much payment, fee_obligation A given payment is allocated to a given obligation at most once no
    payment_request The ask, carrying the variable symbol that ties a bank transfer back to it fee_obligation — the amount is copied, never recomputed Several per obligation over time; at most one live; the symbol is unique over every symbol ever issued no
    bank_transaction One incoming transfer as the bank reported it payment_request when matched — absent means it is in the queue; import_batch Unique per bank reference within an account; immutable once imported no

    Events

    Entity What it is Points at How many [T]
    event A dated occasion people register for. Owned by nobody fee_schedule, optional, when it costs money. It belongs to no unit and nobody: the people connected to an event are named by operands that bind to it (ORGANIZERS, PLACED, CONFIRMED, ATTENDED, ON_WAITLIST), so there is no owner for a check to ask (D-102). created_by is attribution, not ownership, as D-005 says One row per occasion; no recurrence, no series no
    event_organizer Who is running an event, with a label and an order — an appointment, not a grant (D-062) event, person Many per event; the list is the domain answer to "who runs this", and it is the one cascade delete in this design (§8) no
    event_registration One person's answer about one event, plus whether they turned up event, person — person, never membership, so a 1998 camp roster does not smuggle its people into MEMBERS today One per person per event; unanswered is the absence of a row (D-059); was_present is three-valued and is deliberately the basis of no selector no
    event_question One thing this event asks, of attendees, organizers or both event; and optionally attribute_definition by code, which is what lets a question prefill from a stored fact and write a fresh value back — the binding never names a value row (D-139) As many as the event asks; defined per event, never shared yes
    event_answer What one person said to one question, stored as a row and not as JSON event_question; person — who answered, for a child the child, not the registration row and not whoever submitted on their behalf One row per question per answerer; no expiry — it outlives its event, and erasure empties the value and keeps the row (D-140) no

    Reference vocabularies — grant_scope_type, selector_type, identifier_scheme, permission. Four, and one fewer than this page used to list: reason_code is deleted by D-128, because a reason is what a person writes, not a row to maintain. Each is named by a value held elsewhere rather than pointing at anything, and each exists so that a vocabulary is data the organization can see rather than a list inside the application. attribute_definition is the same kind of thing in spirit and a different shape in fact — it has a curation lifecycle, a child table, and rows pointing back at it — so it is inventoried above rather than here.

    event carries no [T] — it has no valid_from / valid_to, because an event is a dated occasion rather than a period-tracked record. Its lifecycle is starts_at / ends_at / cancelled_at, and it has no status column at all (D-064). event_registration likewise stores no period: a person is attending, or they are not, and "unanswered" is the absence of a row (D-059).

    [T] = temporal, carries valid_from / valid_to. A no says it does not; where the reason for not is a decision about someone's data rather than the nature of the table, the row says so — which in this inventory is person_attribute, and nowhere else.

    8. Foreign key summary¶

    Columns with no FK, and why:

    Column Why no FK
    access_grant.scope_id Polymorphic by design. Validated by grant_scope_type + orphan checks. It is absent exactly when the scope type is unit, and that absence is the schema-level form of "the unit is a scope, not a row" (D-097).
    audience_operand.selector_arg_id The second one, and newer: one integer pointing at a role, a person_group, an audience, a person or an event depending on selector_kind, resolved by selector_type.argument_type and caught by the same nightly orphan scan. D-101 carried D-011's trade-off across deliberately, mitigations included — the registry is the only thing that says which table an id belongs to, so an unmapped kind is the empty set, not an error. It also sometimes holds no reference at all: OLDER(n) and YOUNGER(n) store a whole number of years here (D-107), and that does not make a third key — 18 points at nothing, so the orphan scan has nothing to walk, a merge has nothing to repoint, and this row is still describing one column that sometimes carries a reference and sometimes carries a scalar.
    access_log.scope_id Same shape as the grant's; absent for a check at the unit. Followed by 05d §11 check 23, with exactly one legal absence available to it: of the kinds this column can name, only an event is ever removed, and only by the import's revert.
    access_log.authorization_ref_id Points at one of two tables, access_grant or guardianship_permission, and nothing else; a historical row whose channel reads role keeps the word and loses the reference, which is the honest state of a value whose table is gone (D-100). Followed by the same check and excepted by nothing, because neither target is ever deleted: a set id that does not resolve is the only unacceptable answer. A row whose channel is outside the enum names no table at all, so the scan reports it beside the findings rather than as one.
    access_log.entity_id Polymorphic target of the audited action. Followed by the same check, with its exception written there: an absence is legal only where the table named is one this design removes and the trail shows the removal — a person_attribute row for an erasure, a person, membership or event row for a revert. Whether this column is ever written naming a person_attribute row is stated nowhere, so the erasure arm may have nothing to fire on.
    field_conflict.entity_id Polymorphic; recorded alongside entity_table. Followed by the same check, and its finding is the dangerous one of the four: a conflict row naming nothing is an import that recorded an undecided field against a row which is not there, and the decision a person later takes inherits that. Revert accounts for the ones whose entity_table is person or membership and left an entry naming the row; the rest are broken.
    audit_log.record_id Polymorphic; recorded alongside table_name. Not followed, deliberately (D-134): an audit row outliving the row it described is what an erasure and a revert look like afterwards, and 06b §5's check 6 locates the payloads it exists to test through exactly this reference. A count here would report the two paths this design trusts as integrity failures.

    There are seven reference-less keys, and the class matters more than the count. access_grant.scope_id and audience_operand.selector_arg_id are ids with no constraint behind them, and each carries the same four things that make that survivable: a registry saying which table an id belongs to, a save-path check against that registry, an orphan scan the scheduler owns, and an explicit rule that a failure resolves to the empty set (../data-model/00-conventions.md §9). The other five are the same class of column and they do not all get the same answer, which is what F-30 asked and D-134 settled: four are followed by 05d §11 check 23, and audit_log.record_id is followed by nothing on purpose, because an id in a trail is written in order to outlive the row it names and the redaction check reads that reference in order to run at all. What the six followed columns share is the registry and the scan; what none of the seven gets is a constraint, because no foreign key can name one of several tables. A scalar is not one of the seven: OLDER(n) and YOUNGER(n) write a whole number of years into audience_operand.selector_arg_id, and there is no table that number should have named, so there is no registry lookup to miss, no orphan for the scan to find and nothing for a merge to repoint. The count is about references, and 18 is not one (D-107). A candidate arrived with api_key and is not one either: access_grant.api_key_id names exactly one table, always, so it carries an ordinary foreign key and needs none of the four things above — no registry saying which table it means, no save-path check against that registry, no orphan for the scan, no rule about what an unreadable kind resolves to. Becoming the third grantee cost a column with a constraint, not a column without one (D-116).

    access_grant has no grantee column — that is the one mutual-exclusion case rather than a missing-FK case: exactly one of person_id / audience_id / api_key_id is set, never two, never none. All three columns are nullable, so nothing but the rule says whom a grant means; a grant naming none is meaningless and one naming two is ambiguous about whom in the audience it speaks for. The three are not three spellings of one thing: a person and a key are read exactly as written, and only an audience is an expression evaluated as of the date being checked (D-116, D-117). audience_operand carries the same shape of rule one table down — operator with left_id/right_id, or selector_kind with selector_arg_id where the registry says the code takes an argument — and for the same reason: a row satisfying neither is a node in no tree, and an expression containing one resolves to nobody.

    Every other column ending in _id has a real foreign key with ON DELETE RESTRICT — the newest of them api_key's owner_person_id, created_by and revoked_by, and the api_key_id carried by access_grant, access_log and audit_log — with one exception: event_organizer.event_id is the single ON DELETE CASCADE in the design — an appointment is nothing once the occasion it appointed somebody to is gone, and the guards in ../data-model/08a-event-guards-and-checks.md §8 are what make that delete safe to allow at all. Two operands whose argument is an integer add no key and no cascade, because there is no row for either to be a child of — still one cascading column, still event_organizer.event_id. Everything else that merely looks like a child restricts: a registration, an answer, a member row, a holder row, a grant. The general rule, and this one exception, are written down once in ../data-model/00-conventions.md §9 — nothing cascades in front of a 40-year trail.
