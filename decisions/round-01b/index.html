    Decision Log — The founding decisions (D-016 … D-030)¶

    Renewal and requests, guardianship, the two audit trails, the legacy import, and finance as a provisional placeholder.

    The founding decisions (D-001 … D-015) ← | All decisions | Refinement rounds 2 and 3 (D-031 … D-046) →

    D-016 — Renewal opens a new membership row, not an extended valid_to¶

    Status: decided Date: initial design

    Decision. Shape A. A renewal closes the prior membership and opens a new one, linked by renewed_from_membership_id.

    Why. Extending valid_to destroys the ability to see that someone skipped a year and re-joined — the most commonly asked question about renewals — and makes retroactive corrections ambiguous. You asked for history preserved across decades.

    Cost. More rows; queries must handle a chain. The self-reference makes the chain explicit rather than inferred.

    D-017 — Requests are separate from memberships¶

    Status: decided Date: initial design

    Decision. membership_request is its own table. A rejected request produces no membership row.

    Why. A rejected request must not pollute member listings; a person may apply several times; review data has no meaning on a membership; submitting is self-service while reviewing is a staff capability.

    D-018 — membership_request records the subject and the submitter separately¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: initial design

    Decision. person_id is the person who would become a member (the child). submitted_by_person_id is who submitted it (the parent).

    Why. Collapsing them — the common shortcut — makes it impossible to answer "who applied for this child?" and falsely attributes the parent's action to the child. You confirmed caretakers act on behalf, which requires both parties to be recorded.

    Consequence. The same pattern applies to payment.paid_by_person_id / payment.on_behalf_of and to access_log.on_behalf.

    D-019 — Household membership grants no access¶

    Status: ⚠ PARTLY SUPERSEDED — the household tables are gone (D-057), so there is no household membership left to grant anything. The principle remains true and is the reason to keep this entry: shared address never implied access, the access never came from these tables, and it still does not.

    Date: initial design

    Decision. household exists for billing and address grouping only. Being in a household grants no permission over any other member of it.

    Why. A stepparent with no guardianship row has no access to the children's records. All access comes from guardianship + guardianship_permission. This is a safeguarding property, not an oversight.

    D-020 — Guardian permissions are scoped to the guardianship row¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: initial design

    Decision. guardianship_permission.guardianship_id, not the (guardian, dependent) person pair.

    Why. You answered that both read and write access must be explicit. Scoping to the pair would let a guardianship ending and a new one beginning (a renewed court order) silently retain all prior permissions. Scoping to the row forces a deliberate re-grant.

    Cost. A re-granting step in a rare case. Accepted.

    D-021 — Health/medical access is separate from consent authority¶

    Status: decided Date: initial design

    Decision. dependent.medical.view and dependent.consent.give are distinct permissions.

    Why. A parent who may consent to a camp is not necessarily a parent who may read a medical file, and vice versa. Court orders frequently restrict one without the other. Conflating them makes the common restriction inexpressible.

    D-022 — Lapsing and termination are different permissions¶

    Status: decided Date: initial design

    Decision. membership.lapse (administrative, non-payment) and membership.terminate (end for cause) are separate permissions, the latter held by very few.

    Why. Conflating them means every volunteer who chases unpaid fees can expel a member. Termination in a youth organization is frequently a safeguarding decision.

    D-023 — Nothing is auto-lapsed or auto-terminated¶

    Status: ⚠ partly superseded by D-040 and D-038

    Date: initial design Superseded in part by: D-038 (age-out), D-040 (expiry), D-043 (background checks)

    Decision (as originally written). The system produces worklists; humans act. Grace periods absorb late payments. Age-out is driven by membership_type.max_age but the transition is reviewed.

    Why. Automatically removing a child's membership because a bank transfer was recorded late is a real harm. Automation should stop short of the consequence.

    What survives, and what does not¶

    The principle survives and is still the design's default: for anything that is a judgement, the system produces a worklist and a human decides. Terminations for cause, safeguarding removals, and guardianship expiry at majority (D-024) all still work this way.

    Two halves of the original text do not survive:

    Original claim Now
    "Grace periods absorb late payments" The grace status is gone. There is a renewal window — dates, not a state (D-040).
    "Age-out is driven by membership_type.max_age" There is no max_age and no age-out. Membership is ageless (D-038).

    The expiry is the one automatic transition. D-040 resolves the tension the original "why" names, and resolves it by removing the decision rather than the automation: the fee covers a fixed period, the period ends on a date everyone already knows, and nobody chooses anything. The late-bank-transfer case the original wording was protecting is absorbed by the window's backdating rule instead. The reversal is recorded in full at ../data-model/07-finance.md §4, including the one case the window cannot absorb.

    D-024 — Guardianship does not auto-expire at majority ⚠ REVERSED¶

    Status: ~~assumption~~ reversed by D-069 Date: initial design · reversed refinement round 5

    Reversed — but not in the direction you might expect. The alternative was not auto-expiry. D-024's sin was the review queue: still an automatic behaviour, still computing a majority date the system has no authority to assert. D-069 removes the queue and the date. The conclusion D-024 reached — do not silently strip access — is kept; the mechanism is withdrawn.

    Decision. The system computes the candidate date and puts it on a review queue. Staff close the row.

    Why. In many jurisdictions a parent's authority over health and school records does not end at 18, and thresholds vary. An automatic cutoff will silently strip legally valid access.

    D-025 — Audit log redacts sensitive values before insert¶

    Status: decided Date: initial design

    Decision. audit_trigger calls redact_audit_values() before writing. Free-text fields that may contain personal data are redacted.

    Why. An audit log that stores the data it audits is a second copy of sensitive data with weaker access controls and longer retention. Redaction in a cleanup job is not sufficient — the job might not run.

    D-026 — Two audit trails¶

    Status: decided Date: initial design

    Decision. access_log (who accessed what, by what authority) and audit_log (field values before/after) are separate tables.

    Why. Different retention needs, query patterns, and access permissions. Merging them produces a table too large to query and too sensitive to expose wholesale.

    Consequence. access_log.authorization_channel + authorization_ref_id are what make it an audit trail rather than a log — they answer "by what authority?", which is the question asked in a dispute.

    D-027 — Legacy import is staged, reviewed, idempotent, and per-batch reversible¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: initial design

    Decision. Six stages (staging → parse → match → review → apply → log). Dry-run mode. Deterministic source_row_hash for idempotency. Per-batch revert while no live data references the imported rows, refusing rather than partially applying.

    Why. You confirmed historical records must be imported. Import errors into a four-decade dataset are expensive and often silent.

    Consequence. Revert past the safe window is refused, not attempted — a "revert" that orphans live references is worse than an honest refusal.

    D-028 — Imported memberships never get a NULL valid_to¶

    Status: decided Date: initial design

    Decision. Imported rosters end at the end of the organizational year of the last roster the person appears in, marked data_confidence = 'low'.

    Why. A NULL valid_to makes a 1998 member a current member in 2026 — inflating the member count and granting member privileges to people who left decades ago. This is the most dangerous possible default.

    D-029 — Finance design is provisional and will be rewritten¶

    Status: assumption / open Date: initial design

    Decision. ../data-model/07-finance.md exists only so that membership activation has a referent. Expect to rewrite it.

    What is likely right regardless: payment_allocation as a many-to-many between payments and obligations (a parent paying for three children in one transfer is the common case, not an edge case), and fee_schedule with validity periods so historical amounts are reproducible.

    See: the twelve open questions in that document.

    D-030 — Finer permissions will be added incrementally¶

    Status: decided Date: initial design

    Decision. The permission catalog in ../appendix/permission-catalog.md is a starting point. Adding a permission is a data change (one row plus permission_level rows), not a schema change.

    Why. You asked for granularity and an admin fallback at every resource; the prefix-match rule (D-007) means new permissions are automatically covered by existing .admin grants, so granularity can be increased later without touching existing roles.
