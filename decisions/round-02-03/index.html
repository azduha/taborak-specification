    Decision Log — Refinement rounds 2 and 3 (D-031 … D-046)¶

    The first answers to the open questions: no downward inheritance, deny absolute, one root unit, one membership type, the fee and its renewal window, audiences as grantees.

    The founding decisions (D-016 … D-030) ← | All decisions | Refinement round 4 (D-047 … D-057) →

    D-031 — No downward inheritance of authority; scope_exposure is the only crossing¶

    Status: decided Date: refinement round 2 Reverses: D-015

    Decision. Authority is settled at the scope where it is granted and never inherited downward. A role assigned over a district does not cover the troops in it. The permission resolver performs no ancestor walk — unit_ancestors() must not appear in can(), grant_satisfies(), or can_act_for_dependent(), and integrity check 13 is a CI assertion that this stays true.

    Authority crosses downward through exactly one mechanism: a scope_exposure row, written by the unit granting coverage over itself, naming a specific role_assignment — not a role — with its own validity period and withdrawal trail. The crossing is offered from below, never imposed from above.

    Why. Your rule, stated twice: "The organization itself should not be able to itself decide permissions over the nested unit, it must come from the unit itself up. The only exception is the system admin." Downward inheritance violates it structurally, because a single row written anywhere above a unit would silently decide authority inside it.

    The exception. The system_admin flag is checked before the resolver runs and is therefore outside this rule. It is scoped to the whole system, not to a unit, so it is not a counterexample to "authority originates at the unit" — but it is all-or-nothing and should be rare.

    See: ../domain/06-authorization-model.md, ../data-model/05-authorization.md.

    D-032 — Deny is absolute; locality orders allows only¶

    Status: decided Date: refinement round 2 Amends: D-013

    Decision. Two separate rules that were previously conflated into "local overrides global":

    1. Deny is absolute and scope-independent. An explicit revoked at any scope wins over everything, including a more specific allow, the admin level, ownership, and guardianship. The deny check does not walk ancestors — a deny is matched at the organization scope or at the exact same scope, and that is sufficient to be absolute.
    2. Locality orders allows. Among allows, the most specific scope determines the level: item grant → unit grant (exact or exposed) → organization grant → role. First sufficient tier wins.

    Why. "The local permission overrides the global one" is true for allows and dangerous for denies. Court-ordered restrictions and safeguarding suspensions must not be overridable by the accident of a grant being written closer to the item. Separating the two rules keeps the local-override intuition where it is correct and removes the one case where it is a safety failure.

    See: ../domain/06-authorization-model.md, ../data-model/05-authorization.md.

    D-033 — Multiple people may occupy the same position¶

    Status: decided Date: refinement round 2

    Superseded by D-100, along with the position module. That more than one person may hold an office at once survives as role_holder being a dated holder row rather than a column — the same answer, given to a different table.

    Decision. Position occupancy is many-to-one by default. position.max_holders is NULL (unlimited, the default) or N ≥ 1 for a genuinely single-holder position (a chair). Overlap for the same person in the same position in the same unit is still prevented by an exclusion constraint; the holder limit is enforced by the check_position_holder_limit() trigger.

    Why. Two co-leaders, three quartermasters, a shared secretary — the common case is multi-occupancy, so the schema must not make it a workaround. Keeping max_holders optional and defaulted to unlimited means the single-holder chair is the special case, not the rule.

    Note. Position is not authority (D-005). Holding a position grants nothing by itself; is_leadership only suggests a role_assignment.

    See: ../data-model/02-organization.md, ../domain/04-organization-structure.md.

    D-034 — The person record holds no deceased flag¶

    Status: decided Date: refinement round 2

    Decision. There is no deceased_at, no is_deceased, and no person-level death constraint anywhere in the model. Personhood ends when it ends; the organization does not record it.

    Why. Your instruction: "Person does not need to hold the 'deceased' parameter, we do not log that." A death flag is also a data-protection liability (special-category data in some jurisdictions) and a merge hazard — it would need to survive person_merge conflicts that have no good resolution.

    Consequence. "Deceased" now has exactly one encoding, and it is indirect: a membership that ended, a position_assignment that ended, a guardianship that ended. Historical records stay correct without it, because every participation is already a period (D-002).

    D-035 — One root unit; all sub-units nest arbitrarily beneath it¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3

    Superseded by D-097: there is one unit, and nothing nests, because there is no unit row to nest underneath.

    Decision. There is exactly one top-level unit: the organization itself. Every other unit is a sub-unit of it, nested to arbitrary depth and in arbitrary combination — weekly meeting groups, smaller packs, sections, teams. The nesting is not a fixed ladder of national → region → district → group → troop.

    Membership lives at the root. People register and pay at the organization, and the membership is theirs as a member of the organization. The sub-units do not hold memberships; they hold participation (D-041) and they are self-governing — their own events, their own local leadership, their own roster.

    Why. Your answer: "The people are members of the main top-level unit. The smaller ones are totally arbitrary and can be arbitrarily nested… they all answer to the main unit, the people are mainly registered (and paid) under the top unit, but they can be also part of the smaller packs, that can have their own events, local leadership and so on."

    Consequence. The single root was enforced structurally, not by convention — the statement of the moment, kept as it was decided:

    -- Exactly one unit may exist at the root.
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_org_unit_single_root ON org_unit ((true))
    WHERE parent_unit_id IS NULL;

    The requirement now: at most one unit may have no parent, whatever emits the schema cannot emit it, and the migration owes it — 02-organization §3, via data-model/00 §6a.

    This largely dissolves the organization-wide-role problem (C-1). That problem existed because "the organization" and "the units holding the data" were assumed to be different things. In this model they are the same: the root unit owns the person registry, the memberships and the fees, so a treasurer or registrar working at the root is not reaching into anything — that data is its own. What remains is a reporting question (may the root see what happens in the packs?), not a permission-inheritance one.

    The no-downward-inheritance rule (D-031) still does real work in the other direction: the root cannot decide permissions inside a pack, and a pack that wants the root's help must expose its scope.

    See: ../domain/04-organization-structure.md, ../data-model/02-organization.md.

    D-036 — Units cannot be reparented; a unit that moves is a new unit¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3

    Superseded by D-097 — there is no parent column left whose change could be refused. This was the design's only instance of an immutable column, so F-10 shape 4 lost its subject here, which is what that entry now records.

    Resolves: C-2

    Decision. org_unit.parent_unit_id is immutable. A unit never changes parent. If a troop genuinely transfers from one group to another, the old unit closes and a new unit is created under the new parent, the people are moved across, and the lineage is recorded in org_unit_relationship with a transferred_to relationship.

    Why. Your answer, to the question of whether a unit may be reparented: "Not allowed at all — a unit that moves is a new unit."

    Consequence — a large simplification. Because the parent never changes:

    • The materialized path/depth never needs a subtree cascade rewrite. The existing "reparenting is rare and dangerous; make it a single named procedure" warning disappears along with the danger.
    • Historical reports cannot silently change. "Who was in the 4th Troop in 2001?" keeps answering the same thing forever, because no unit's ancestry is ever edited.
    • The denormalized path stays trustworthy without versioning, which was the main reason retroactive reparenting was expensive.

    Enforcement. A trigger rejects any change to parent_unit_id on an existing row. org_unit.transferred_to_unit_id (or the relationship row) records where the people went.

    Amended after D-072. There is no trigger, and this is the one enforcement in this log that cannot be moved down a layer. parent_unit_id immutability is not expressible as a constraint — not because PostgreSQL lacks the feature an ORM fails to emit, but because no declarative constraint can talk about what a row used to be. So the rule now lives as a guard in the write path, and the record here has to be honest about what was lost on the way: This entry's original claim was that a trigger made reparenting unreachable from any client, including the legacy import. A write-path guard is reachable from exactly the clients the trigger was chosen to exclude. The compensations available are narrow and named in open question F-10, shape 4: a single named repository operation for hierarchy writes, and a test that attempts the update.

    See: ../data-model/02-organization.md.

    D-037 — Exactly one membership type; the fee is flat and per person¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3 Supersedes: the child/adult/counselor/honorary/lifetime seed set

    Decision. There is one membership: everyone who is a member is a member. The yearly fee is flat and per person — it does not vary by age, by household, or by whether the person is a child or an adult. Being a counselor or leader is a position (D-033), never a membership type.

    Why. Your answers: "Only one for all, counselor is a position", and between one type with an age-varying fee, two types, or one flat fee, "One type, and the fee does not vary by age either."

    The table is kept, with one seeded row. membership_type survives as a table rather than collapsing into a constant, because the document's own principle is that vocabularies are rows and not enums, and because a second type (an honorary or supporting membership) is a plausible future that would otherwise require a migration. The cost is an FK that currently always holds one value — an honest, small cost, recorded here rather than hidden.

    Consequence. membership.membership_type_id becomes effectively constant, and the overlap exclusion constraint is no longer scoped by type — see D-041 and ../data-model/03-membership.md.

    D-038 — Membership is ageless¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3 Removes: membership_type.category, min_age, max_age, and the age-out workflow

    Decision. Age has no bearing on membership. There is no age ceiling, no age floor, no automatic age-out, and no child/adult category on a membership. A person may be a member for life.

    Why. Your answer: "No age boundary at all — anyone can be a member forever."

    What survives. Age is still a fact about a person (person.birth_date) and it still matters where the law makes it matter:

    • Guardianship — a minor has a guardian, and consent is a guardian's act. This is a legal relationship, not a membership rule.
    • Safeguarding — a unit may declare whether minors take part in it.
    • Position eligibility — a scoutmaster may be required to be an adult.

    What disappears is the machinery that made belonging conditional on age: the age-out worklist, max_age-driven completed transitions, the child→adult transition workflow, and the "is this person a child?" query.

    Retired questions. D-3 (age-out timing), D-4 (child→adult transition) and D-5 (membership type vocabulary) are resolved by this decision and by D-037.

    D-039 — Member permissions require a current paid membership¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3 Resolves: A-2

    Decision. The permission resolver does evaluate membership state. Authority held through a role is honoured only while the holder has a current membership, with exactly two carve-outs:

    Channel / case Membership required?
    Roles with role.requires_membership = true Yes — no current membership, no access
    Roles with requires_membership = false (appointed counselors, local leaders) No — an adult volunteer keeps their leadership access with an unpaid fee
    Guardianship (dependent.*) No — guardianship comes from a legal relationship, not a membership
    Ownership (your own record) No — otherwise a lapse would lock a person out of the screen they renew on

    Why. Your answer: "Yes — permissions conditioned on membership", and on how far the rule reaches: "Everything except guardianship and adult volunteers." The third option — locking an unpaid person out of even their own profile — was explicitly not chosen, which is why ownership joins guardianship as exempt.

    The cost, stated plainly. This is a real coupling and it is worth naming rather than discovering later:

    • ~~The cache must be invalidated on every membership transition, including the yearly expiry sweep, which is a bulk event touching everyone at once. ~~ ~~The cache design must be built for this from the start, not retrofitted.~~ The origin of a warning that was copied twice and read zero times. This bullet is where the requirement was first written; data-model/03-membership.md §6 restated it in the round that moved the gate to MEMBERS, and the two lists that D-111 drew three rounds later named every table except membership — so the sentence existed in two files, in the module that had the coupling, while the boundary was drawn in the module that owned the cache. D-114 deletes the obligation along with the cache and records both facts, because the useful lesson is the copying, not the requirement: a dependency stated in prose at the far end of a coupling is not a constraint on the page that has to satisfy it. membership is still read live by MEMBERS, so the coupling this bullet was afraid of is now the design rather than a hazard in it.
    • A lapse silently removes access. That is the intent, but "why can't I see this?" becomes a question whose answer is a date, not a decision someone made.
    • role.requires_membership changes meaning — from "reported, drives a worklist" to "enforced by the resolver". The "leaver with access" report remains useful, since it now detects roles that should have been closed rather than the mechanism doing the blocking.

    See: ../domain/06-authorization-model.md, ../data-model/05c-access-log-and-resolver.md.

    D-040 — The fee expires yearly; a date-tracked renewal window replaces a grace status¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3 Resolves: A-3, D-3 (grace modelling)

    Decision. Membership activates on payment, and the fee is yearly, expiring at a fixed organizational date (typically January). When it expires:

    1. The membership lapses — the person is not a member and holds no member permissions from that date. There is no third "grace" status in the resolver.
    2. A renewal window — a fixed period such as three months, tracked as dates, not a status — follows the expiry. During it the person may renew without re-applying.
    3. A payment inside the window opens the new membership period at the expiry date, so the membership history shows continuous coverage with no gap and the person is not misclassified as a returning member.
    4. A payment after the window opens a new membership at the payment date, and the gap is real. It still does not require a new application.

    The tension, named. You described the grace period as existing "for them to pay to stay members", and separately confirmed that during it they are "expired now, but they can rejoin without re-applying." Those pull in different directions on one point: whether the person is a member during the window.

    This decision resolves it as: not a member during the window, but the record shows no gap once they pay. The alternative reading — genuinely continuing as a member through the window — would mean a lapse is not detectable until the window closes, and would put a third state into every permission check. If the organization would rather have the softer version, it is a change to membership.renewal_grace_days and the renewal rule, not a rewrite.

    See: ../domain/05-membership-lifecycle.md.

    D-041 — Belonging to a sub-unit is a participation record, not a membership¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3

    Reversed as to the sub-unit by D-097; kept as to the distinction by D-098. Being a member and being on a group's roster are still two rows, with two lifetimes and two different permissions guarding them. Only the right-hand side changed: it is a person_group now.

    Decision. unit_membership is renamed unit_participation and reframed. A person holds exactly one membership (at the root unit, paid yearly — D-035) and zero or more participation rows, one per sub-unit they take part in.

    Why. Your answer, to how the schema should record being "part of a smaller pack": "A participation record, separate from membership." The rename matters because the old name asserted something untrue — that belonging to a pack was a species of membership — and "three memberships, three statuses, three fee questions" is exactly the ambiguity the answer avoids.

    Consequence — the overlap constraint simplifies. With one membership type (D-037), the exclusion constraint drops membership_type_id and becomes a person-level invariant — quoted as decided, in the form the constraint had at the time:

    ALTER TABLE membership ADD CONSTRAINT ex_membership_no_overlap
    EXCLUDE USING gist (
        person_id WITH =,
        daterange(valid_from, valid_to, '[)') WITH &&
    )
    WHERE (status IN ('pending_payment','active','suspended'));

    The requirement now: one person, one open membership, ever, where "open" means those three statuses — a no-overlap rule over a period, which is exactly the shape an ORM will not generate (open question F-10 shape 1); the temporal pattern is in 00-conventions §6 and the check that catches a bypass is 1b in 03-membership §7.

    "Am I a paid-up member?" is now one row, one status, one date. That question was the reason the type was in the constraint.

    A root participation row is created with the membership, so "everyone in unit X" is one uniform query whether X is the organization or a Thursday-evening pack.

    See: ../data-model/03-membership.md.

    D-042 — Grants may target audiences, not only individuals¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3

    Decision. A grant can name an audience instead of a person. Two kinds:

    Kind Evaluated Examples
    Built-in audiences By a rule the resolver knows, not by a stored list everyone, all_members, the guardians of a given person, the participants of a given unit, the leaders of a given unit
    Named groups From a stored membership list someone administers "the summer camp kitchen crew"

    access_grant therefore carries person_id or audience_id, never both.

    Why. This came out of your answer about scope_exposure: "If the unit wants to expose something (a single event eg), they set the permissions to that event for 'everyone' or a group of people to expose it to them." The design had no way to express that — every grant named a person — which is a genuine gap your answer surfaced rather than a detail.

    Built-in audiences are rules, not lists, and that is the important property: a new pack member automatically receives what the pack was granted, with no rows to maintain and no stale-group problem. Named groups are the escape hatch, and they carry that problem knowingly — nobody ever removes anyone from a group.

    See: ../data-model/05a-audience-and-grammar.md.

    D-043 — No background checks are recorded in this system¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3 Resolves: A-4

    Decision. The system records no background checks: no person_identifier scheme, no requires_background_check flag on membership types, positions or roles, no expiry report, no daily alert, and no person.background_check.* permissions.

    Why. Your answer: "We do not have any background checks in place, at least not ones handled by the system."

    What this removes. The whole person.background_check.* permission pair, the background_check requirement code, the background_check_officer seed role, the "leader with expired clearance" report, and one of the two reasons position.requires_adult_membership existed.

    If that changes, the cleanest route is a requirement_code row plus an expires_at-bearing record — the machinery this decision removes was general enough to carry it. Recorded here so re-adding it is a deliberate act with a visible history rather than a surprise.

    D-044 — Nothing gates a membership request except identity and payment¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3

    Decision. A membership request is accepted on identity and payment alone. The requirement_code and membership_request_requirement tables are removed — there is no blocking checklist, no evidence collection, and no waiver workflow.

    Why. Your answer, asked what a request must contain to be accepted: "Just identity and the fee — nothing to verify." The option to keep the mechanism as empty infrastructure was offered and not taken.

    What survives. Guardian consent for a minor's account is unaffected — that is a guardianship-layer fact (D-005), not a membership requirement, and its removal here does not weaken it.

    D-045 — The bulk scope-exposure tool is removed¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3

    Decision. The bulk exposure tool is removed, along with the bulk:<batch> reason marker, the bulk branch in check_exposure_direction(), and integrity check 12 that listed bulk rows for review. Every scope_exposure row is written by the unit it covers.

    Why. Two answers, in sequence. Asked whether the tool should exist, you said you did not know what it meant and described instead how you actually think about exposure — "they set the permissions to that event for 'everyone' or a group of people". Once audiences (D-042) were added to cover that case, the question was put again and your answer was: "Not needed if audiences are added."

    Consequence — the rule is now absolute. There is no sanctioned bypass, no marker prefix, no exception to explain. scope.expose held at the covered unit is the only way a row is created, and it is enforced by the same predicate at three layers (domain/06 §2).

    This is a genuine simplification, and it removes the design's most uncomfortable paragraph — the one arguing that the rule had to bend for scale.

    D-046 — Guardianship defaults depend on how the guardianship was created¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) Date: refinement round 3 Resolves: E-1

    Decision. The default permission set granted when a guardianship is created depends on its source:

    How it arose Default
    A parent self-registers their own child Full authority — dependent.admin
    Recorded manually by staff, or from a court order Set at creation — the person creating it chooses the set

    Why. Your answer: "A parent self registering their own child gets full access, if it is created manually later, it can be set on creation."

    Consequence. guardianship.source and guardianship_permission.grant_reason are what make the difference auditable — the same word ("guardian") now means two different default scopes depending on paperwork, and the record says which was chosen and why.
