    Decision Log¶

    Decisions taken, with the reasoning that produced them. Recorded so they are not re-litigated from scratch — and so that when a decision is reversed, the reason for the original choice is visible.

    How this log is filed. The entries live in the round that took them; this page indexes all 149 of them and lists the reversals. Read the round you are curious about — each one says what was asked and what the answers did to the design — or follow a D- number from any page straight to its entry. Nothing is summarised here that is not stated in full there.

    Status values: decided — settled, the data model reflects it (since D-072: the documents, not a schema file). proposed — recommended, awaiting your confirmation. assumption — taken by default, stated in italics in the docs, easily reversed. reversed — no longer in force; the entry is kept, marked, and the replacement is linked. Reversals are also listed together in Reversed decisions below, where they were first collected; the collection has not been reordered, because a section with a stable anchor is worth more than a section in a tidier place.

    Five SQL fences survive in this archive, and only in these entries. D-072 moved the schema out of the documentation, but it did not rewrite the entries that had been written in the schema — at D-035, D-041, D-060, D-066 and D-068. Each is a quotation of the shape a decision was made in, and each says below it where the requirement actually lives now. No later entry adds a fence: the rule for writing this log from here is D-081 — state the requirement, name the layer that owes it, and let an identifier be a name rather than an actor.

    Reversed decisions¶

    • D-015 — Authority inherits downward through the unit hierarchy. Reversed by D-031 (refinement round 2). The organization must not be able to decide permissions over a nested unit; authority originates at the unit. The original entry is kept in its round, marked reversed, rather than edited away.
    • "Local permissions override global ones" (a phrasing used in the domain docs, never a numbered decision). Amended by D-032: still true for allows, and deliberately not true for denies. The statement in isolation was the source of the ambiguity, so it no longer appears unqualified anywhere in the docs.
    • Person deceased field (never assigned a decision number; it appeared in the person table and the permission catalog). Removed by D-034.
    • position.is_unique_per_unit (a boolean). Replaced by position.max_holders (D-033).
    • The single-rooted hierarchy as an assumption (§2 of the organization docs described a national → region → district → group → troop ladder, with groups permitted directly under national). Replaced by D-035: one root, arbitrary nesting beneath it.
    • Retroactive reparenting as an open question (C-2). Resolved by D-036: not permitted at all.
    • membership_type.category, min_age, max_age, and the age-out workflow. Removed by D-038.
    • unit_membership (the name, and the claim that sub-unit belonging is a species of membership). Renamed and reframed by D-041.
    • The bulk scope-exposure tool, its bulk: reason marker, and integrity check 12. Removed by D-045; their replacements are audiences (D-042).
    • Background checks in every form (requires_background_check, the permission pair, the requirement code, the background_check_officer role, the expiry report). Removed by D-043.
    • requirement_code and membership_request_requirement. Removed by D-044.
    • The "condition only a narrow set of permissions on membership" recommendation (a recommendation, never a numbered decision). Reversed by D-039: the resolver does evaluate membership state, with guardianship, ownership and exempt role classes carved out.
    • role.valid_from / valid_to, the role.retire permission, and the resolver's check on a role's own period — all introduced by D-052 and removed the same round by D-053. A role is a definition and is valid always; the period lives on role_assignment. The superseded reasoning is kept in D-052's entry, because why it looked right is worth knowing.
    • role.requires_membership as the only way to waive the gate. Widened by D-051: the flag survives and still answers "is this role membership-dependent?", but a person-level system.gate_exempt permission now waives the gate for a named individual regardless of which roles they hold. The original framing — in which D-6 was a question about which roles to exempt — is what this supersedes.
    • The seeded sub-unit vocabulary (pack, group, team, and before them the national/region/district/group/troop ladder). Removed by D-054: the unit-type seed is the root and nothing else, and the organization defines its own types as runtime data. The words still appear across these docs as illustration — they are not types the system ships with.
    • household and household_member, plus fee_obligation.household_id, its CHECK arm, ix_obligation_household, and integrity check 7. Removed by D-057. The address is a temporal person_contact row of type postal. Note this is not in the list as a reversal of D-019: D-019's principle — shared address grants no access — is untouched and still binding, since the access never came from these tables. Only its subject was deleted.

    • The trimmed-permission-catalog direction implied by F-1 (a review that would cut the catalog down to what the organization needs now). Resolved the other way by D-055: the catalog is a deliberately large, granular menu, and further permissions arrive with the modules not yet specified.

    • <resource>.admin, and the resolver's prefix match. All sixteen per-resource wildcard permission codes (person.admin, event.admin, …) and the X.admin branch of can() were removed by D-068, reversing D-007. Two things sharing the name survive and are not reversals: the admin grant level (rank 40), which is a different mechanism, and dependent.admin, which is a shorthand a person deliberately stores on one guardianship row rather than an implied capability. The failure mode D-007 was written to prevent — a new permission nobody bundled into a role — is accepted, and integrity check 18 is the net that replaces the wildcard.

    • The guardianship review queue at majority, together with the computed candidate date it relied on. Removed by D-069, reversing D-024. Note the direction: the replacement is not auto-expiry. Both automatic behaviours went. D-024's conclusion — never silently strip a legally valid arrangement — is kept; its mechanism was a deferral that still asserted a date the system cannot justify.
    • The "require valid_to on high-authority role assignments" recommendation (F-3, a recommendation, never a numbered decision). Withdrawn by D-071: the membership gate already expires what an expiry date would have expired, for the same population, without inventing a date. Read together with D-053, which placed all the weight on role_assignment.valid_to — the human review report is now the entire control.
    • The proposal to give event a min_age/max_age pair (raised as EV-1 within this round, never built). Declined by D-070: a registration-time condition the capability model does not know about is the first rule-engine rule. No reversal, since nothing was shipped — recorded here because the idea will return.
    • Merge chains as an assumption. Decided by D-087, in the same direction the assumption pointed — chains persist and resolve_person() walks them. It is in this list because of how it was argued: the docs justified chains by saying flattening would make unmerge impossible, which is a reason resting on a capability nobody had asked for. Reversing a human-approved merge is now a requirement, so the conclusion is unchanged and the argument behind it is not — and flattening, previously "faster", is now a trade against a named feature. The integrity check that read a tombstoned survivor as a breach was corrected the same round: under decided chains it is normal.
    • The audit trail as the permanent keeper of values. Narrowed by D-088 to a payload window of 24 months (rows survive; old_values/new_values are emptied). D-085 is not reversed and its overwrite-in-place stands, but its stated justification — "the trail is the history" — is true for two years and untrue afterwards, which changes what that decision costs. Read the two entries together; neither is complete alone.

    • "A historical roster may create a membership if there is payment evidence" (the safe default kept open in 06-audit-and-import.md §6). Retired rather than answered by D-090: the corpus carries no payment data at all, so the exception arm has nothing to fire on and the default is simply the rule. Historical rows are unit_participation; membership comes only from the current register. Recorded because the reason for the rule — an inferred open-ended membership would make a 2003 roster a current member with authority today (D-039) — is the part worth keeping if a corpus with money in it ever arrives. Retired outright one round later, which is the more instructive half: the corpus turned out to state membership periods directly, so the rule was replaced by D-093 and the frame it rested on by D-092. The reason survives; the rule does not. A decision recorded from a description of the data is only as durable as the description.

    • "An audience does not nest", and audience.kind's rule-xor-list split. Both withdrawn by D-091: a group is now a rule arm, a manual arm and an inclusion arm whose membership is their union, and a group may include another. Neither was ever a numbered decision — both were prose in data-model/05 §8, which is how they survived three rounds of review unexamined while doing real work (they are the reason "the crew, plus everyone who leads this pack" needed two grants and why a composition the organization asked for in round 9 had been silently impossible). The cycle the nesting ban existed to prevent is refused at the write instead, which is the same answer D-087 gave for merge chains one round earlier — worth reading as the pattern rather than the reversal: when a rule was written to prevent a hard case, the first question is whether the hard case can be refused at the write rather than defended against at the read.

    • org_unit_type.allows_minors, and the write-path check it drove (integrity check 2 in 03-membership.md §7). Removed by D-095: units carry no restrictions, and the control is that nobody joins a unit or takes a position in one themself. This is in this list because of what replaced it, not because a column went — a flag on a type became a permission held by named adults, which is D-081's "say who enforces it now" answered with a change of kind rather than a rename. The consequence is symmetrical: the system can no longer tell anyone which units are for children.
    • The correction made to 03-membership.md §5.1 in round 8 — that "an ordinary import touches neither arm of this pair", because a roster writes participations and no memberships. Reversed by D-093 within one round: the membership export writes memberships by the thousand, so the root-participation obligation points at the import again, which is what that section said before round 8 for entirely different reasons. Recorded because a correction that gets reversed is the kind a reader will not trust, and the reason to trust it here is that both rounds were wrong about the same unknown thing — the shape of a file nobody had read.

    Round 10 removed a dimension of the model, and the reversals below are the largest single set in this log. They are grouped rather than listed one per line, because they are not twelve independent reversals: they are one diagnosis ("I have over-specified the system and this complicates things") drawn through the unit hierarchy, the authority ladder, the membership gate and event ownership at once.

    • The unit hierarchy itself — org_unit, its types, names, relationships, participations and the upward scope_exposure crossing. Deleted by D-097: there is one unit, it is the organization, and it is a scope rather than a row. Reverses D-008, D-035, D-036, D-041, D-049, D-050, D-054, D-078, D-096 and deprives D-031 of its subject. The property this bought — access to a body's children had to be granted by that body — is recorded in D-097 as lost, not as replaced.
    • The level ladder (access_level, permission_level, the rank integer, and everything that compared levels). Deleted by D-099: a grant is one permission code with an effect of allow or deny. This reverses D-012 including the reason it was written (a level string compared alphabetically inverts the model — now there is no level to compare), makes D-013's deny an effect rather than a level, and deletes half of D-032: locality only ever ordered allows against each other, and a boolean answer has nothing to order. D-074 survives on a new permission.right_kind column, which D-099 names as a column with exactly one customer.
    • The membership gate as a resolver clause (role.requires_membership, system.gate_exempt, person_holds_gate_exempt). Deleted by D-100, which moves the test into the selector: MEMBERS * ROLE(x) gates on current membership because MEMBERS is evaluated live, and leaving MEMBERS out is how a role is exempt. Reverses the enforcement half of D-039 and all of D-051, including the lockout hazard D-051 had to document. The posture reverses from default-on to opt-in, and the new failure mode — a grant written without MEMBERS authorizing a lapsed member, silently — is answered with a report and an import count, not by the resolver.
    • Positions (position, position_assignment, is_leadership, max_holders) and role_assignment (becoming role_holder, with no scope), by D-100, which also deletes role_permission: a role confers nothing by itself, and the codes an office carries are the grants whose audience names ROLE(x). The elected-versus-appointed distinction survives only as a role the organization invents. F-12 is closed, and the answer is no.
    • The audience's three arms, and the inclusion edge. audience.resolver_key, scope_parameter, audience_include and include_depth_limit are deleted by D-101, which replaces them with an expression over named sets stored as a tree of binary rows. This amends D-091 rather than reversing it: the outcome D-091 wanted — composing "the kitchen crew, plus whoever leads this pack" in one grant — survives, and the intersection and difference it could not express now can be written. What dies with the edge table is the transitive walk, the graph-shaped cycle check and its CI assertion, because a tree cannot cycle. The one thing genuinely lost is one-edit widening ("add the crew to everywhere this group is used"), answered by naming the composite.
    • Event ownership (event.org_unit_id, can_event(), the unit-authority rule). Deleted by D-102, which reverses D-058's ownership, D-063 and D-094, and answers "who is connected to this event?" with selectors that bind to the item being checked (ORGANIZERS, PLACED, CONFIRMED, …) instead. D-062 stands untouched: an operand is a set, not a grant.
