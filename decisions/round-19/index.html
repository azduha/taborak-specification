    Decision Log — Refinement round 19 (D-133 … D-136)¶

    Two trail rules are unchecked and now say so; four of seven polymorphic columns get an orphan scan and the audit row deliberately does not; the two modules with no check list get one; and an election is not recorded, because a role holds only the people.

    Refinement round 18 (D-127 … D-132) ← | All decisions

    What was asked. Round 18 had just emptied the top of the "answer next" list and filled it again with six questions of its own making, all of them found the same way: write down who enforces each invariant and count the blanks. This round was asked to put questions, not to change the model, and it did: four were put, four were answered, and none of the four changed a table. That is the shape this corpus is in — the remaining questions are about what a report is able to see and what a piece of prose is worth when nothing runs it.

    What the round found while answering, which is the part worth reading. Three of the four questions arrived with a premise that did not survive being checked against the page that owns the rule.

    • F-29 blamed the payload window for a gap the window does not cause. The check it was debating would compare a key's valid_to, its revoked_at and an audit row's occurred_at — three columns of the accounting skeleton, all permanent. The report could be written and would work in thirty years. So the answer "no report" is not a limit the design is under, it is a guarantee the design declines, and the documents now say that instead of implying the opposite.
    • The register row minted beside that question described a rule no page states. 00a carried "which audit views a holder may open follows the payload window, not the table" against a citation to 06 §2. No page in the corpus says any such thing; 06 §2.3 says close to the opposite — a per-entry check inside the history view was considered and left unimplemented — and that question is AT-4, already open. Under D-129 a register row is a claim about a page and is verified against it, never asserted, so the row is withdrawn. It got into the register in round 18 by the route D-129 warns about: the inventory was written from the pages, and this row was written beside the inventory.
    • F-30's own positions could not count. They offered to "scan the two a person can cause" and then named four columns. The seven columns were re-counted from the field tables before anything was decided, which is also what turned up a second, quieter defect: erd-b still said "there are exactly two reference-less keys" after round 18 had corrected that exact sentence in 00 §9. The register and the appendix had drifted apart by one round.
    • Round 18 had fixed one page of that count and left four. Checking the drift meant grepping the sentence, and the grep found the same claim in 05b twice, 05a and erd-a twice — "exactly two polymorphic keys in this design go without a foreign key", where the design has seven, every one of them with a sibling column naming what it points at (grant_scope_type, argument_type, scope_type, authorization_channel, entity_type, entity_table, table_name). Three of the five cite §11 for the assertion, and §11 never makes it — which is D-129's defect again, a claim about a page that the page does not make, stated five times and verified zero times. All five now say two of seven and point at the page that keeps the count. A count that was taken by looking is a count of what somebody looked at, and one round is long enough for the corpus to believe it.

    One decision from round 18 is still not in the model. D-132 was ruled last round and is applied by a sweep this round owes and has not yet run: the seeded roles, the seeded audiences, the seeded event.answer.view grant, the worked-example offices presented as seed, role.is_system, audience.is_system. F-18 below is the second decision this round closed that had been arguing, in the present tense, from is_system.

    D-133 — Two trail rules stay unchecked, and say so out loud¶

    Status: applied.

    Question. F-29 asked what a report may be asked to see about an audit row whose payload has been emptied on schedule. Two rules had no report beneath them: that no write is ever audited against an api_key that had already ended, and — as the register had recorded it — that which audit views a holder may open follows the payload window. Both were listed as blocked by D-088's 24-month window, which empties the payload and is the part a check would have to read.

    Answer. No check is written for either rule, and the page that states the first one now says the guarantee is not offered. Of the three positions on the page — check the envelope rather than the payload, accept the blindness and say so, or widen the window for the columns these checks need — the second was chosen, and chosen with its reason corrected first.

    Why not the cheap-sounding one. "Check the envelope" is genuinely cheap: the columns involved are a key's valid_to, its revoked_at, and the audit row's occurred_at, none of which is a payload. Writing it was declined, not impeded, and the decision records what that costs rather than dressing it up: nobody will ever be able to demonstrate that no write was audited against a key that had already ended. A report nobody reads is not a control either, and this corpus has already decided — nine times over, in checks that exist only to re-ask whether a guard is still there — that the number of reports is not what makes a rule true. Widening the window was refused for the reason the question itself named: a privacy answer dressed as an integrity one.

    What was actually wrong in the question, and what was wrong in the register. The window was never the obstacle — see the lede. And the register's second row for this question described a rule that exists nowhere. Both halves are repaired: 06b §5 now carries the ruling beside the sentence that admits the gap, and the phantom row is deleted from 00a rather than re-worded, because there is nothing to re-word it into. The nearest real rule — a history screen over the trail is a person-data surface, and a per-entry "could this reader see this field" check was considered and left unimplemented — is stated where it lives, in 06 §2.3, and its open question is AT-4. This decision does not answer AT-4 and does not close it.

    Deletes: one row of 00a, and with it the eleventh none. Changes no table, no column, no code.

    See: 06b §5, 00a, D-088, D-129, and F-29 in § R.

    D-134 — Four more polymorphic columns get an orphan scan, and the audit row never¶

    Status: applied.

    Question. F-30. Seven columns hold an id with no foreign key behind it and a sibling column saying what kind of thing it points at. Two were followed by an orphan scan (05d §11 check 2); five were not scanned anywhere: access_log.scope_id, access_log.authorization_ref_id, access_log.entity_id, field_conflict.entity_id and audit_log.record_id. The question said explicitly that these are not one decision, and it was right, because the answer for the trail is the opposite of the answer for a conflict row.

    Answer. Four get a scan; audit_log.record_id never does, and the pages say why in those words.

    • field_conflict.entity_id, scanned outright. A conflict recorded against a row that is not there is a broken import. There was no sentence anywhere saying so, which is the reason this one was the cheapest of the four.
    • access_log's three, scanned with the legal-absence rule written beside them. A reference that no longer resolves because a person was erased at their own request is not an integrity failure, and a scan that cannot tell that apart reports the erasure path's own correct output as a breach.
    • audit_log.record_id, deliberately not scanned. An audit row that outlives the row it described is what a redaction looks like afterwards. Counting those as orphans would train the reader to ignore the report — the first false alarm is the expensive one — and the alternative, a legality rule over the whole trail, is a rule about absences so broad it would be read once and never again. The column is not an oversight any more; it is the one place this design states that the trail is allowed to point at nothing.

    What this corrected as it went. 00 §9 and erd-b both counted reference-less keys, and after round 18 fixed the sentence in one of them the two disagreed by a round; both are now correct and say which columns are scanned, which are scanned-with-an-exception, and which is exempt. 00a's row for this rule cited only the appendix, which is the inventory rather than the rule's home, and under D-129 it now cites 00 §9.

    Adds: one numbered check in 05d §11, beside check 2 and cross-referenced with it, and no TIME-BOMB label — this one is a report over data, which is what the label is for keeping apart. Changes no table and no column. Whether an orphan in access_log after an erasure is legal is a rule about which rows survive erasure, and the check inherits whatever 00 §4 and 06b already say.

    See: 00 §9, 05d §11, erd-b, and F-30 in § R.

    D-135 — The two modules with no check list get one each¶

    Status: applied.

    Question. F-31. Every data-model page states its rules and then lists what would notice a breach — except 07-finance and 06a-import, which state rules and stop. That is why most of the register's blanks sit in those two modules: their rules were unassigned by construction, not by oversight, because there was no section for an assignment to live in. It was the only yes-or-no on the page.

    Answer. Yes: three checks on each page, in a new 07 §4a and a new 06a §4.

    The check What a row means
    Finance 1 — the balance, recomputed from §3's definition A member wrongly suspended, or a family wrongly chased. Survives §3's own recommendation to drop the column, because then it checks the view against the definition instead of the copy against the sum
    Finance 2 — a payment whose allocations do not account for its amount Money that moved and was never attributed, or an overpayment nobody refunded. No constraint can span the two tables, so this report is the backstop
    Finance 3 — money nobody ever asked for An open obligation with no live request, or a paid-for event with no obligation behind the registration. Silent in both directions: nobody is chased, nobody pays
    Import 1 — an imported membership that should not exist An unbounded period, or a membership written from an attendance list. The one row in the register that reaches the permission gate: an open membership satisfies is_current_member() (D-039)
    Import 2 — one event per (title, date), one registration per attendee row The application-layer rule uq_staging_event_match has been lost, and ten thousand attendees have become ten thousand events
    Import 3 — a batch whose own accounting does not close Rows neither applied nor discarded, a row that created nothing, ids from the wrong half of the corpus, or a reverted batch that left rows behind — read against the audit entries the revert wrote

    What the round noticed while writing them. The import page had been citing, for two rounds, "check 1 in §5" — a section that page does not have. The citation was correct in intent and wrong in fact, and §3.1 now points at §4, where check 1 is the one it meant. The strongest of the six import checks was therefore already in the corpus as a promise about a section that did not exist, which is the same failure as a register row with no report beneath it, one level up.

    Adds: two sections, six checks, and three rows in 00a for rules both pages had stated and never registered — the bounded imported membership, a batch's own accounting, and a payment's allocations. The register is 71 rows where it was 69, and its none column is seven where it was eleven. It also costs the register its excuse: What the register cannot say had been explaining the concentration of blanks by pointing at these two pages, and can no longer.

    See: 07 §4a, 06a §4, 00a, and F-31 in § R.

    D-136 — An election is not recorded; a role holds only the people¶

    Status: applied.

    Question. F-18, open since D-100 deleted position with its is_leadership, min_age and max_holders: does a role say elected or appointed, and if so what does an election write? The page's own framing was fair to both sides — role_holder has valid_from, valid_to, granted_by and note, which is nearly enough to describe an election, and granted_by names the secretary rather than a mandate.

    Answer. Neither. The organization manages elections outside this system, and a role holds only the people who hold it. No column for term kind, no table of elections, no record of how a person came to fill an office. What arrives is the outcome: one role_holder row, a period, and note for whoever wants the reason written beside the fact rather than buried in the trail. Stated where a reader will meet it, in domain/04 §6, with a pointer from 05 §4.

    The refusals, each with the reason it was refused.

    • A boolean on role — elected or appointed — would make the distinction queryable and would guard nothing. A field that looks like a control in a place where no control is wanted is worse than the absence of one, because someone starts relying on it, and the first exception teaches them not to. The question itself noticed this: it observed that a flag "invites a check which D-100's posture would refuse to enforce".
    • A table of elections would describe a process this system does not run. There is no ballot, no candidate list, no quorum rule anywhere in these documents, and D-043 already settled that this is not the registry of everything true about a volunteer.
    • The distinction has nowhere to bite. An office confers nothing by being held (D-100), so there is no dangerous thing an election could confer and no answer anywhere in the resolver that would move if a row knew how its holder arrived. This is the fact that makes the answer cheap, and it is worth naming before anyone offers to make the answer expensive again.

    The cost, stated because the page says it out loud rather than leaving it to be discovered: nothing in this system will ever refuse to appoint someone to an office the bylaws reserve for a vote. That rule lives in the bylaws and in the people reading them.

    Two of the question's load-bearing facts had already gone while it sat open: role.max_term_months is deleted (05 §3 lists it among the columns deleted with their mechanisms), and role.is_system — which the question cited as what keeps seeded offices distinct — is deleted by D-132, whose sweep is still owed. A question that has been open long enough to lose its premises should be re-read before it is answered, and this one was.

    Changes no table and no column. Three pages that had been calling this an open question now say it is closed: glossary, catalog-a §5, and domain/06 §5.

    See: domain/04 §6, 05 §4, D-100, D-043, and F-18 in § R.

    What the round did not do¶

    • It did not run D-132's sweep. The seed still ships in the pages: 05d §12, catalog-b §11, and role.is_system in 05-authorization.md. That is a cleanup with a decision already written, and it is the next thing this repository owes itself.
    • It did not answer AT-4, which D-133's repair brushed against and explicitly left where it was.
    • It added no check that it could not point at a page. That rule held even where it was inconvenient: the register still says none seven times, and three of those seven are open questions rather than chores.

    The gate ran. scripts/check-registries.py reports no unbaselined finding at the end of this round, and mkdocs build --strict is clean. D-127 says a round does not close on an unbaselined finding; nothing here needed a new baseline line, which is the quiet way for a round to go.

    What the link pass swept up on the way. Five links still pointed at F-8 by its heading slug, which stopped resolving the round an explicit <a id="f-8"> was put inside that heading; the target was fine and the address was not, which is the one kind of breakage a reader never sees coming because the question is still there, one click away from nowhere. A slug is derived, and a derived thing has to be recomputed, not remembered — which is why every anchor touched this round was recomputed rather than typed.
