    Decision Log — Refinement round 25 (D-206 … D-216)¶

    Round 24 emptied the register. This round fills it again, on purpose, and that is the honest shape of it. It opened as four questions about loose ends — three free-text columns, one spelling clash, and two standing questions about what reporting even is — and one of those answers came back as a designed messaging module, which is the feature README had kept in its deferred list since round 1 and which this corpus has referred to in the conditional mood for twenty-four rounds. A design that gains a feature gains questions, so the register leaves zero and goes to seven (K-11 … K-17); an empty register was never a claim that the design was finished, and round 24 said so in its own words.

    Two of these answers reverse recent decisions, and both reversals are named rather than absorbed. D-210 deletes four permission codes, two of them shipped, on the ground that a report is a delivery and not a screen — which undoes the reporting posture round 21 fixed and the deferral D-195 chose one round ago. D-214 narrows D-164, the eight-round-old rule that no page may say the system sends anything — the rule twenty-nine lines across nineteen pages were written to obey, two of them added by round 24 itself on 2026-11-01, the same day as this round. Nothing here pretends those sentences were always going to be wrong; they were correct under the ruling that stood, and the ruling moved.

    One answer arrived twice and differently. Asked who a notice reaches, the first answer was "the admin selects an audience for each event type" and the follow-up was "each message type". The audience hangs on the message type (D-209); what an event type wants as a default is filed as a question instead of being inferred from the first wording.

    Nothing in this round was asked that a previous round had answered — the discipline round 23 and round 24 both promised. Where this round touches an old decision it narrows it in the open: D-164 by D-214, D-199's actor list by D-212, and D-082 by D-213, which turns a sentence about mirroring into a rule about rows.

    Where the questions came from. None of them was in the register, because the register had just been emptied.     D-206 — A message is one table, this system is one of its authors, and a notification is a type of message¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) — opens the module README has listed as deferred since round 1, and starts data-model/10 Messaging.

    The answer, as given. "Let's call it messages, not notifications (notification is a type of message), but yes, they are the same machinery and a message can be sent to a single person or an audience."

    The rule. There is one thing, and its word is message. A message row has exactly one author, and the author is either a person (author_person_id) or this system acting on its own (actor_label, from the fixed list — D-125). A notification is a message whose message_type_id is set, and nothing more: the word names a kind of row, not a table, not a module, and not a second mechanism beside the one a person uses to write to another person.

    Why one table, when an automated notice has nobody to ask. The temptation is a notification table next to message, on the argument that a safeguarding record should tell a system notice and a person's letter apart without reading a column. It is refused twice over. Once by shape: this corpus has refused two tables of the same shape before, in D-100, and the difference between these two tables would be exactly which table a row sits in. And once by substance: what makes a notice attributable is already there — the actor label, and one audit row per write (D-184) — while a second table would need its own expiry rule, its own audit shape, and its own reader, and those three would drift apart within two rounds the way four pages used to drift apart over one sentence about NULL (00-conventions §7a).

    What this changes. Three tables, in a new data-model/10-messaging.md: message_type, message, message_recipient. README loses "notifications" from its deferred list and gains the module's shape in the paragraph that already draws communication's line. The ERD inventory gains three entities. And the sentence in that same README paragraph — "nothing in this design sends anything to anybody today" — is false from this round on and is rewritten by D-214.

    D-207 — A message is addressed to one person or to a saved audience, and the recipient rows record an act and not a set¶

    Status: decided (from the same answer) — gives the send its shape, and answers the half of D-110 that a reader could have carried into messaging by mistake.

    The rule. A message names exactly one of two targets: target_person_id or audience_id. Exactly one, never both, never neither — the same discipline a fee_obligation keeps over its three possible targets. At the save, an audience is resolved once by the resolver that already answers grants, and one message_recipient row is written per person reached.

    What the rows are, and what they are not. They are the record of the act: whom this message reached, on which door, and whether it was opened. Nothing resolves against them. No audience expression may name a message's recipients as a set, which is the whole reason this paragraph exists: an operand must be a named registry row and never a stored set, and a table that starts as a log ends as a vocabulary the moment somebody writes PERSON(...) over it. A grant's record of whom it reached stays the expression it holds (D-110); a message's record is the rows, because an audience can be edited tomorrow and the fact that thirty people were addressed in March must not change with it.

    A message is never edited. A body written is a body kept; a wrong send is followed by a second message that says what the first got wrong. This is D-183's rule about an ask, reused without ceremony, and it is what makes "nothing ages out" (D-211) survivable at all.

    The absence worth naming. An audience that resolves to nobody writes no recipient rows, and the message row still exists. That is not an error and there is no queue: it is the same shape as money that arrived under no symbol (D-180) — the design records the act and does not invent a waiting room for a result nobody claimed.

    D-208 — message.send is the only messaging code, and the audience a send names is not checked¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) — registers messaging in catalog A, and closes catalog B §12's open half.

    The answer, as given. "One permission to write messages to anyone, call it message.send (it will be held by the leadership or anyone the granter decides)."

    The rule. message.send is registered: one code, applies_to = unit, marked twice for sensitivity, and it authorizes writing a message to one person or to an audience. What the send names is not checked. There is no per-person test over an audience, no requirement that the writer could read the rows of everyone addressed, and no rule that an event's organizers may address only their event. D-163's ceiling still governs who may hand the code out, and one audit row records every send; that is the whole control, chosen over the alternatives rather than left as an oversight.

    message.send_direct and message.broadcast were never codes. They appear in catalog B §1 as names and in its §12 as an open question about which scope broadcast should carry — and they appear nowhere in catalog A's register, which is what a register is for. They retire as names. They are deliberately not entered in removed-concepts.md: the first column of that page is this corpus's dead-name register, and a row there is a death certificate for something that lived. These two never did.

    Catalog B §12's open half closes with them. Its question was whether message.broadcast should hold one scope or two; there is no broadcast code to scope, and an audience narrows a send instead of a grant scope doing it. D-099 is untouched: one grant, one permission, one scope.

    This narrows D-077 in the place its title is not about. D-077's other half was that messaging is direct by default and no permission exists for it — everyone could write one message to one person, and only reaching a crowd needed a grant. From this round one code gates every write, a sentence to one person included, so "direct by default" now describes the shape of a send — one target instead of an audience — and no longer a right. The corpus's own rule runs in the new direction: D-197 ruled that a permission nobody can decline is not a permission, and this code can be declined, which is what makes it the control the answer asked for ("held by the leadership or anyone the granter decides").

    The cost, named in full, because the answer did not buy the route as well. A person who holds no code has no way to write to the organization through this module — not to a leader, not to a group, not about their own child's membership. This entry does not design a route for that person and does not claim one exists, and it does not soften the ruling by inventing an ungated exception beside the code the answer asked to be the only one. What the account keeps is everything else in this design: a person's own rows are readable to their guardians through the guardianship channel, and an erasure request is a person's act that never needed a messaging code, because there is no messaging code in front of it.

    A job does not hold this code. An automated message is written under an actor label (D-212), because a permission gates a person's act and a job has none to gate.

    D-209 — A message type is a row, and the audience it reaches is configuration an admin edits¶

    Status: decided (from two answers, the second correcting the first) — puts the reach of an automated message where an installation can change it.

    The answers, as given. "Ok, not permissions, but in the system configuration, the admin selects an audience for each event type" — and, asked which word "type" belonged to: "Each message type."

    The rule. message_type is a table: code, name, description, audience_id, is_automated, valid_from, valid_to, created_at, created_by. A type reaches the audience on its own row, chosen from the saved audiences 05a §8 already documents. is_automated is true exactly when a job may write the type, and audience_id is required exactly then — a human send carries its own target on the message instead (D-207). A type is retired with valid_to, never deleted, which is D-109 saying what it says about anything a row may point at.

    This is membership_type again, and saying so is the point. D-186 found a table that looked like a vocabulary and was really a settings panel with a vocabulary's clothes. A message type is the same animal: the code is the vocabulary (renewal-window-opened, fee-overdue, registration-confirmed), the audience is a setting, and what an organization changes on a Tuesday afternoon is the second and not the first.

    What the two wordings disagreed about, and what was not inferred from them. An event type is not what holds an audience here. The first answer named one; the follow-up named the message; the message wins because it is the row that fires. Whether an event type should propose a default audience that a per-event send may narrow is a real question and is filed as K-13 rather than drawn in from the first wording — D-204 is the reason this round refuses to do that: an entry that widens an answer while writing it down becomes a false claim about a table.

    Who may edit a type is not settled here. Every write in this design is gated by a code, and no code for this table is proposed, because a vocabulary table's editing has been refused a code before (D-152). The working default is whoever holds system.admin at the unit, and the question of whether that is right is K-12.

    D-210 — A report is a delivered message, so all five report codes are deleted and reporting stops being a name¶

    Status: decided (from your answers) — deletes two shipped codes and three deferred ones, and rewrites every sentence in this corpus that ends with somebody opening a list.

    The answers, as given. "Report is a system message delivered through a notification." Asked whether a report still has a screen a person opens: "Only a delivery message, depending on a type delivered to people with specific permission." Asked what happens to report.view: "Both die."

    The rule. There is no report screen in this design. What an organization calls a report is a message of a type, addressed to an audience, delivered by the machinery in D-206. Five codes go, not four. report.view and report.export lose their rows in catalog A, and report.finance.view, report.membership.view and report.safeguarding.view lose their rows among the deferred namespaces — the deferral of a finance report was D-195's deliberate choice one round ago, and a code that would gate a screen is not worth deferring once there is no such screen. This entry's first draft named four and missed report.membership.view, which catalog B §10 had carried beside the other two and which the leaders office named as its one report row; a namespace dies by its whole list, so the miss changed nothing about the ruling and everything about this page's accuracy, and the code now has its own row in the dead-name register. The report.* namespace stops existing. Reporting is not a permission, not a module, and not a deferred item: it is an audience and a type.

    What the deleted codes' absence is filled with. Twenty-five lines across seven pages named one of these codes at the commit this round starts from, forty occurrences because several lines name two of them: seven in catalog A, nine in catalog B, three in 07-finance, two in domain/06, and one each in 05d, 05e and the open register. Counted by program, and the count is this entry's own correction: the first draft said seventeen lines across ten pages and listed 03-membership, 09-attributes and domain/05 among them, and none of those three pages ever named one of the five codes — their report sentences are sense 3 below, which survives. Most of the twenty-five sentences were written to be honest about a hole — they are the corpus admitting that a rule needs a human to notice something. None of them becomes "nobody notices". Each becomes one of two things, and which one is decided sentence by sentence in the sweep, not by a rule here:

    • a type and an audience. The over/under payment notice (07-finance §2b) and the overdue notice (07-finance §3) are exactly the two cases this round was asked about, and both now reach people instead of waiting for them.
    • an honest absence. Where a sentence said a person with report.view opens the list and no type is configured, the sentence now says the thing plainly: nobody computes it, and the design is not waiting for somebody who might.

    Three things this corpus calls a report, and only one of them changes meaning.

    1. A report as a permission — deleted. report.view and report.export authorized a screen, and there is no screen. This is what the answer was about.
    2. A report as something delivered — the new sense. "Report is a system message delivered through a notification" is the answer's own sentence, and in that sense a report is a message of a type addressed to an audience. D-126's break-glass report fits this sense better than it ever fitted the old one: the decision always wanted whoever can act to be reached, and had no machinery to reach them.
    3. A report as a listing nobody receives — this keeps its wording, and the reason is that three decision titles are built on it. "The gate and the report are the control" is D-071's title, "a key outlives whoever installed it and the report is the control" is D-120's, and D-126's title is built the same way. Twenty-nine live lines echo them, and the two halves do not overlap: seventeen carry one of "no report will ever show it", "the report of long-lived open grants", "a report over a date", and twelve say the report is the control — and each means a check exists, somebody runs it, nothing delivers it. Rewriting that idiom would misquote a title that has an anchor, and an anchor moved is a citation lost, so the idiom stands where it is. What is forbidden from this round is the other two senses being confused: no sentence may promise that a listing reaches anybody, and no page may name a report.* code, because there is no code and no screen.

    Where a page had to be precise, the register was the place, so the register changed its word. The invariant register headed its right-hand column Report and its prose said "a report over the state they would breach"; that column lists numbered checks, so it is now headed Backstop — the word the page already used for it in its own reading instructions — and the page states that nothing in the column changed but its name. A canonical page a resolver of citations reads is worth one rename; twenty-nine echoes of a quoted title are not.

    What this costs, said where it bites. There is now no screen anywhere in this design that shows an organization-wide list. 05d §12 used to state that exactly two reporting codes exist, and that sentence is gone. And report.export was the one moment this design had data leaving it by intention; with it gone, D-199's "nothing leaves" gets stronger, not weaker — a person who wants data out now has to read it.

    D-211 — Nothing about a message ages out, and erasure does not reach messages¶

    Status: decided (from two answers) — the third kept-forever posture in this design, and stated with the argument against it rather than without it.

    The answers, as given. Asked which of the body, the address, and the read moment expires: "Nothing ages out." Asked what erasure reaches: "Erasure does not reach messages."

    The rule. A message row, its body, its message_recipient rows, and their read_at are never scheduled away and never emptied by an erasure. This sits beside the accounting skeleton, which D-088 keeps indefinitely, and against two clocks that do run: audit payloads empty at 24 months (D-088) and access_log rows are deleted at 24 months (D-145). A message is the first body of text in this design that is written to be kept and is then kept.

    The argument against it, recorded because it was available and was declined. D-191 deleted person.notes on the ground that free text about a person, sitting outside the vocabulary and outside erasure, made erasure a lie. The same argument is standing in this room: a message body is free text about a person, it is written by the organization rather than curated, and it now survives the one act a person can demand. That argument was put and refused. The answer's own weight is that a message is correspondence — an act that happened on a date — and an act this organization performed is not something it deletes on request. 06b §4 names what erasure empties and says that messages are outside that list by decision, with this paragraph as the reason, and 00-conventions §8 carries the same sentence in the section that lists the erasure's reach.

    The consequence, not softened. An erasure removes the person and leaves every message addressed to them: the recipient row points at a tombstone, and its body keeps saying what the organization told that person, and when, and on which door. 01b is where a person's row survives as a tombstone, and that is what makes this survivable rather than broken — nothing dangles; it points somewhere quiet.

    D-212 — system:messages is the sixth actor label, and the renewal sweep stops being a sender¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) — 00-conventions §7a goes from five rows to six, for the first time since round 17 fixed the list.

    The answer, as given. "One new label: system:messages."

    The rule. system:messages joins the fixed list, and it is the author of every automated message. system:renewal-sweep stops being a sender: it expires memberships, which is what it has always done, and the renewal notice is a message whose author is the messaging label. One label per kind of act, so that adding a type to configuration never adds a label to a vocabulary. The run writes one audit row for itself (D-184), as every table the application writes owes.

    The count moves, and a count that moves has to be swept. Five labels were the settled number: D-199 wrote it in as a refusal of a sixth for accounting, and 05e says it twice in prose. D-199's refusal stands untouched — the rejected label was an accounting one, and there is still no accounting label and no export — but its sentence about the size of the list is narrowed here, and its row in the index says so. History pages keep their wording; that is what makes them history, and it is why D-145's label count in round 20 does not get edited either.

    What still has no answer. Which clock fires which automated type — nightly, hourly, or at the moment a row crosses a date — is not ruled here, and the renewal window's own arithmetic (D-048) was deliberately date-driven rather than job-driven. It is K-14.

    D-213 — The account chooses its own doors, a child's message mirrors as its own rows, and only e-mail lands today¶

    Status: decided (from your answers) — completes D-077's "the recipient chooses the channel", and turns D-082 into a rule about rows.

    The answers, as given. "They can decide to read it only within the system or enable external delivery via email, push notifications or (possibly) SMS." And, on who decides for a child: "The account opts in; guardians get their own."

    The rule. The choice belongs to the account: account.opt_in_email, boolean, required, default false. The inbox is not a door and cannot be turned off — a message_recipient row with channel = inbox is the record that a message exists for you, and a design where that can be declined is a design where "you were addressed" has no evidence. A door arrives with its column on the day its transport exists. E-mail has one, because person_contact already carries a verified email value. Push has nowhere to live: a device token is not a contact point — a person holds many, they expire silently, and contact_type's values (email, phone, postal, website, messaging) all describe something that survives its device. SMS would land on the phone row and has no transport. No column is written for a thing this design cannot do, which is the same lesson D-192 drew from an enum value that claimed a kind of actor existed. Where push lives is K-15.

    Mirroring, as rows. A message addressed to a minor is mirrored to that minor's guardians as their own recipient rows, on their own chosen doors. Guardianship is already the channel that says who that is — it carries a right_level and needs no selector (D-113) — so D-082 gains no table and no age branch in the resolver. It gains a physical form: the mirror is rows, and a row is why "the sender is told it will be" is now an ordinary in-app statement at the write rather than a message about a message.

    The question mirroring opens, filed rather than answered. A message addressed to an audience may contain minors by the hundred, and every one of them mirrors to their guardians on this rule. Whether an audience-addressed message mirrors at all — or only a message addressed to one named child does — decides whether a class-wide announcement generates a thousand rows and whether a guardian can see that their child was on a list they were not told about. It is K-11.

    D-214 — The system sends, and D-164 is narrowed to the one thing it was protecting¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) — narrows D-164, which is rewritten nowhere and re-read everywhere.

    The answer, as given. Asked how much of D-164 survives: "Narrow it, keep the deadline rule."

    The rule. The system sends, and a send is a named act. A person's send is gated by message.send; a job's send is authored by system:messages; the rows themselves record who was addressed, on which door, and whether it was opened; and one audit row answers for the write. What stays forbidden is a page inventing a period nobody chose. "Told within 14 days" may be written only if a row holds the 14 and a named decision chose it — that was D-164's real work, disguised as a rule about verbs, and it is untouched.

    What moves. Twenty-nine lines across nineteen pages, outside this folder, assert that nothing is sent or that nobody is told — counted with the vocabulary of D-164 itself, and listed in the sweep's brief rather than here, because a count I cannot re-run is a count that goes stale silently. Each is now either true in a narrower sense or false. The false ones are rewritten where they are, in the tense they are in: README's "nothing in this design sends anything to anybody today", 04-relationships's "Nothing notifies anybody", 05d's "sends nothing", 07-finance's two, domain/02's "no message is sent", and the register's own. Two of them were written today by round 24 itself, in 07-finance, in the commit that obeyed this rule across the whole corpus — and the follow-up sweep committed hours later read that page again and left both sentences standing, correctly, because under the rule that still stood they were correct. That is recorded here rather than quieted: it is what ruling in the present tense costs when the ruling moves, and it is why every page this round touches cites the decision rather than its own memory of the rule.

    What survives intact. D-137 stands: a guardian appointing a guardian tells nobody, because no type reaches anybody for that event and nobody has configured one — which is now the reason rather than the rule. D-126's named break-glass person stands, for the reason round 22 already gave: a procedure needs a name, and a notification does not. And D-146 is still unanswered about everything except messages — an outbound call to another system is not a message and is still undesigned.

    D-215 — The three notes columns stay, and erasure empties them¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) — closes the loose end that D-191 left one table away from itself.

    The answer, as given. "Keep, add to erasure."

    The rule. membership.notes, membership_request.notes and payment.notes stay exactly where they are — free text beside the row, where the work happens. Erasure empties all three, the way D-140 empties an event answer rather than the way a redaction overwrites a name: the row survives, the text goes. They join the list in 06b §4 and the reach sentence in 00-conventions §8.

    What this does not widen, and why that is stated. Free text about a person exists in more places than these three columns, and 06b says so about the others by name: a roster row's note, an account's status_reason and status_note, and the two values a field_conflict holds when a merge could not choose between them. The answer given was about three columns and this entry rules on three columns. Whether the others join them is K-16 — a question, not a footnote, because round 24 learned the hard way that an entry which quietly generalizes an answer becomes a false claim about a table two sections away.

    D-216 — Every worked example names the office treasurer, and what the operand reads is still unstated¶

    Status: decided (from your answer) — repairs fourteen sites, and files the thing the answer did not settle.

    The answer, as given. "Rename every example to treasurer."

    The rule. Fourteen places write ROLE(treasurers) — including the worked audience that opens 05a, which is the sentence a newcomer reads first. Every one of them now names treasurer, the office catalog B §11 writes out and the glossary defines as held for the unit. An example that names a different office from the worked one is not a second example; it reads as a typo in a language that has fourteen occurrences and no compiler.

    What the answer did not rule, kept as a question rather than a guess. Renaming the examples says nothing about what ROLE(x) resolves against — role.code or role.name. Nothing in this corpus states it, and both readings are consistent with everything written down: the operand's argument type names the role table, not a column of it. It is K-17, and it is not cosmetic — under one reading a rename silently changes whom an old expression picks out, which is the cost D-201 already made payable for a group's name.

    What this round adds to the register, and why an empty register was not a finished design¶
