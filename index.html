    Information System — Design Documentation¶

    Design documentation for the organization's information system: people, accounts, membership over time, organizational structure, and permissions.

    Status: draft, actively being specified. Nothing here is frozen. See decisions/decision-log.md for what is settled and open-questions.md for what is not.

    The one idea this whole design rests on¶

    A person is not a type. A person is a person.

    Nothing about being a child, an adult, a counselor, a parent, or a member is a property of a human being. Each of those is either

    • an event or period — membership, guardianship, holding a position; or
    • a derived fact — "child" means "has an open membership of type child", "parent" means "is the subject of a guardianship relation".

    This matters because of a case you named explicitly: someone who was a member from September 1990 to June 2010, and again from October 2024 to the present. They were a child member, then nothing, then an adult counselor. That is one person row and two membership rows. A model that puts is_member or member_type on the person cannot express it, and cannot answer "who was in Pack 12 in 1998?" six years from now. (The 1990–2010 membership is the optimistic half of that sentence: the legacy import creates participation only, since the corpus it reads carries no payment evidence at all, so that membership exists only where somebody recorded it on evidence — D-090. "Who was in Pack 12 in 1998?" is answered by the participation row either way.)

    So there are five rules the data model obeys throughout:

    # Rule Consequence
    1 Personhood is separate from participation person holds identity only. No member flags.
    2 Participation is a period, not a boolean membership, unit_participation, guardianship, position_assignment all carry valid_from / valid_to.
    3 Access is granted, never implied by identity Being a parent does not by itself grant access to the child's data — a guardianship_permission row does.
    4 Belonging to a sub-unit is not membership There is one membership, at the organization. Sub-units hold participation records.
    5 Authority does not flow down the hierarchy A unit is covered only if it exposes its scope — including the root, and including for reading.

    Rule 4 is load-bearing: D-041. "Am I a paid-up member?" is one row, one status, one date — regardless of how many Thursday-evening groups the person turns up to.

    Rule 5 is the organization's own constitutional principle, and it has no exceptions short of the alarmed system_admin escape hatch: D-031. It applies to reading as well as acting — the root does not see into a pack's events and rosters unless the pack exposes them (D-049). Most of what the root needs is not a crossing at all, because the membership registry and the fees belong to the root in the first place.

    Scope boundaries¶

    In scope now: identity, everything else the organization records about a person, accounts, membership over time, organizational units, guardianship, events, and the authorization system.

    Deliberately deferred (referenced so the data model does not block them, not designed): fees and payment processing beyond the provisional shape in data-model/07-finance.md, communication/messaging, documents, reporting, and the API/UI layer. Each is listed in open-questions.md with the hooks already left for it.

    Off this list, because the module it named does not exist: health records. There is no health module waiting to be designed. D-083 decided that everything the organization knows about a person beyond a name, a gender and an age is one class of data, held in fields the organization defines while it runs — so the health file this list used to promise is ordinary descriptive data, specified in domain/08-person-data.md and data-model/09-attributes.md. Events also came off this list, for the opposite reason — they were designed: domain/07-events.md and data-model/08-events.md.

    How to change this documentation¶

    You are expected to iterate. When refining:

    1. Update the affected document(s) — data model files carry the rules and the fields they bind, domain files carry the reasoning. No data model file carries SQL: since D-072 a rule is written as a requirement, and data-model/00-conventions.md §6a names the layer that must carry it.
    2. Record anything non-obvious that you overrule in decisions/decision-log.md.
    3. Move resolved items out of open-questions.md rather than deleting them, so the reasoning survives.

    Where this documentation states an assumption you did not make, it is written as Assumption: in italics so you can find and kill it with a search.
