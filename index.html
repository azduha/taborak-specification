    Information System — Design Documentation¶

    Design documentation for the organization's information system: people, accounts, membership over time, the groups and offices people belong to, and permissions.

    Status: draft, actively being specified. Nothing here is frozen. Decisions holds what is settled and why; open questions holds what is not.

    These documents are the specification. There is no schema file and no code to disagree with, so a rule that is not written down here does not exist. Every page states the design as it is now; what a page used to say, and the argument that changed it, lives in the decision log — nowhere else.

    The one idea this whole design rests on¶

    A person is not a type. A person is a person.

    Nothing about being a child, an adult, a counselor, a parent, or a member is a property of a human being. Each of those is either

    • an event or period — membership, guardianship, holding an office, being in a group; or
    • a derived fact — a person whose recorded birth date makes them a minor on the date being asked about, or one side of an active guardianship row.

    There is one membership type, and it is ageless: no floor, no ceiling, no age-out, no child→adult transition. Age is a fact about the person, recorded once as person.birth_date, and it matters where the law says it matters — guardianship, and whatever rule an organizer writes for one event. It never matters in a membership row. No column anywhere says which bodies are for children; the control that does that job is that a named adult holds the write, and nobody joins anything by their own act.

    The case that forced this: a person who was a member from September 1990 to June 2010, and again from October 2024 to the present. They were a child member, then nothing, then an adult counselor. That is one person row and two membership rows. A model with is_member or member_type on the person cannot express it.

    The legacy corpus arrives as two exports — people, with the period each was a member of the organization, and events, with who attended. Because the people export states membership periods, the import writes bounded membership rows: both ends dated, data_confidence = 'low', one row per stated period, never open-ended and never inferred from attendance — an open membership satisfies is_current_member(), so an inferred one would make every 1998 roster a current member today. The import writes no sub-unit history, because the corpus names no sub-units and there are no sub-units to name; a group's roster before go-live is a thing a person types in. "Who was in Pack 12 in 1998?" has no answer, and that is a property of the design rather than a gap in the data.

    Five rules the data model obeys throughout:

    # Rule Consequence
    1 Personhood is separate from participation person holds identity only. No member flags.
    2 Participation is a period, not a boolean membership, person_group_member, guardianship, role_holder all carry valid_from / valid_to.
    3 Access is granted, never implied by identity Being a parent does not by itself reach the child's data — the guardianship row's right_level does, and none is a value it is allowed to hold.
    4 A group is not membership, and neither is an office There is one membership, at the unit. A person_group holds a dated roster and a role holds dated holders, and neither grants anything by being what it is.
    5 Authority is one permission, allowed or denied A grant names one permission code, one scope (the unit, or exactly one item) and an effect. Allowed when some live allow applies and no live deny applies. No levels, no "nearer wins", deny absolute.

    Rule 4 has two subjects and they are kept apart on purpose. A person_group is a set of people — a lifetime, a dated roster, no authority, joinable only by somebody else's act. A role is an office: a named thing like chair or treasurer, with dated holders and no permissions inside it. An office confers nothing at all; what a chair may do is a grant written somewhere that names ROLE(chair). "Am I a paid-up member?" is one row, one status, one date, however many Thursday-evening groups the person turns up to and however many of them they run.

    Why the two are never merged: a group's roster is materialized and dated, while an office is held by a rule that is re-evaluated on the date being asked about. One of them has a reconstructable past and the other does not, and a grant's reach must be readable from the grant.

    Rule 5 replaced an older principle rather than inheriting it. Where this page states a boolean answer, an earlier design stated "authority does not flow down the hierarchy: a unit is covered only if it exposes its scope". That died with the hierarchy — the unit is a scope, not a row, and there is exactly one of it, so there is nothing above or below to flow between.

    A safeguarding property went with the hierarchy and nothing here reproduces it. Access to a body's children used to have to be granted by that body, by the people who know them, who recruited the leader, and who can be asked. With one unit, a person.view grant at unit scope reaches every member's entire record — the widest read permission in the system. What stands between a person and that read is a named adult holding the write, an audit trail whose payloads expire after two years, and a report of who holds the write. What did not go with it is the half of the rule that still has a subject: nothing is implied by who somebody is, and a deny cannot be outvoted.

    Scope boundaries¶

    In scope now: identity, everything else the organization records about a person, accounts, membership over time, groups and offices, guardianship, events, and the authorization system.

    Deliberately deferred — referenced so the data model does not block them, not designed: fee and payment processing beyond the provisional shape in data-model/07-finance.md, documents, reporting, notifications, and the API and UI layers. Each is listed in open-questions.md with the hooks already left for it.

    Communication sits between the two lists, and the line is worth drawing rather than guessing. Its shape is decided: internal first, external channels are delivery rather than a product, anyone may send a direct message, broadcast is one ordinary scoped permission, the recipient chooses the channel, and a direct message to a minor is mirrored to that minor's guardians. Its codes are in the catalog. What is undesigned is the module — templates, opt-out granularity, retention, moderation. Notifications stay genuinely undesigned: the renewal window made them dated rather than status-driven, which is a design note, not a design.

    There is no health module, and none is waiting to be designed. Everything the organization knows about a person beyond a name, a gender and an age is one class of data, held in fields the organization defines while it runs — domain/08-person-data.md and data-model/09-attributes.md. Sensitivity is graded by what a grant exposes, never by which table a permission code names.

    How to change this documentation¶

    You are expected to iterate. When refining:

    1. State what is. A page carries the current design and no history. When you overrule something, delete the old statement from the page and let the decision entry own the exchange. Where a reader would plausibly reach for the thing you removed, leave one line naming it, saying it is not there, and saying what to use instead — that is a guardrail, not a memory.
    2. Update the affected documents. Domain files carry the model in plain language; data-model files carry the rules and the fields they bind. No file carries SQL: a rule is written as a numbered requirement, and data-model/00-conventions.md §6a names the layer that must carry it. Where no layer can enforce a rule, the text says a convention, not a guard rather than letting a reader assume a guarantee.
    3. Record anything non-obvious that you overrule in the decision log, in the round that took it, and add it to that page's index.
    4. Move a resolved item out of open-questions.md rather than deleting it, so the reasoning survives.
    5. A name that leaves the model gets one row in the removed-concepts appendix and an entry in the log. The organization may still be using the word, so it is not deleted from its vocabulary — only from the model.

    Where this documentation states an assumption you did not make, it is written as Assumption: in italics, so you can find and kill it with a search.
