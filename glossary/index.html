    Glossary — Ubiquitous Language¶

    Terms used consistently across every document in this folder. Where everyday language is ambiguous, the definition here wins and the schema follows it.

    The three-way split that everything else depends on¶

    These three words are frequently conflated in real organizations. This design keeps them strictly apart, because conflating them is the single most common source of unfixable data models in this domain.

    Term Definition Table
    Person A human being the organization knows about, now or ever. Exists independent of whether they have ever logged in, paid, or applied. person
    Account A set of login credentials attached to a person. Optional — a person imported from a 1994 paper roster has no account. account
    Member Not a noun in this system. A person is "a member" at a moment in time if they hold a qualifying membership. It is always a question with a date attached. membership

    A fourth, related distinction:

    Term Definition Table
    User Avoid entirely. When you mean the account, say account; when you mean the human, say person. Most systems use "user" for the wrong one and it never gets fixed. —

    Identity¶

    Person — A human. Stores given name, family name, birth date, and gender. Nothing else. In particular: no member status, no child/adult flag, no troop, no role, and no deceased flag — the organization does not record death. What the organization records about the human is not on this table at all: it is descriptive data, reached by the same permission as the rest of the person record, because none of it is a different kind of object.

    Account — Credentials and login state for a person. A person may have zero or more accounts over their lifetime (e.g. a childhood account abandoned in 2010, a new one created in 2024). Accounts are never shared between people, not even between a parent and child.

    Contact point — A single email address, phone number, or postal address belonging to a person. Stored as many rows per person, not as columns, so that a person can have five email addresses, or a new kind invented later, without a migration. Each has a type, a purpose, and a verification timestamp.

    Identity hypothesis — A proposed but unconfirmed link between two person rows that may actually be the same human. Created during legacy import or when someone registers claiming a historical identity. Confirming one triggers a merge.

    Merge — The act of declaring two person rows to be one human, and collapsing them. Never a delete: the absorbed row survives as a tombstone pointing at the survivor, so that historical foreign keys keep resolving.

    Personal data¶

    Everything the system knows about a person once you subtract what they are called and counted as. The module is domain/08-person-data.md, the tables are data-model/09-attributes.md, and the reasoning is D-083, D-084 and D-085.

    Descriptive data — Everything the system holds about a person other than their name, their gender and their age: one class of data, with no privileged corner inside it. Allergies, medication, insurance numbers, languages, measurements, clothing sizes, dietary habits, a grandparent's phone manner — the same kind of fact, held in fields rather than files, none of it a different kind of object from any other, and all of it reached by the permission that already reads the person. The term exists to rule a category out: there is no sensitive sub-record with its own codes, which is what D-083 declined on the merits rather than failed to build. Its cost is stated there and in domain/08 §4, and it is the reason person.view is graded in the catalog's top tier.

    Attribute definition — One thing the organization has decided to collect: a code, a label, a description of what belongs in the field, a value type, and whether several values may be current at once. The type draws the form and raises the warning; it never refuses a save a human insists on — 2019 recorded as the year of a checkup is a truer record than an empty field behind a validation error. Creating, renaming, merging or retiring a definition is attribute.define; recording a value against one needs nothing beyond person.edit.

    Person attribute — The value one person currently has for one code. Overwritten in place: the row is the organization's present belief, and the sequence of earlier beliefs lives in the audit trail, values included — logged on the same terms as a contact point or a note, because a list of person fields whose values must not be logged would be exactly the privileged corner D-083 deleted, and would leave nothing behind for an overwritten value. What guards the trail is who may read it, not what it omits. No valid_from / valid_to, deliberately (data-model/09 §3).

    Proposed code — A definition that came into existence because somebody recorded a value against a code that did not exist. It waits in the curation queue: kept out of data entry forms and reports, yet readable by anyone already entitled to read the person, because the organization's data quality problem is not the family's confidentiality problem. It is where an improvised need at 23:00 goes instead of into a notes box that nobody will read again.

    Retired code — A definition taken out of data entry and left alone everywhere else: readable, reportable, revivable, its values untouched. Deleting a label must never delete the facts filed under it, or the badly-named label is never retired and the vocabulary accretes instead of being curated. Redaction is the opposite operation and the only eraser: retiring keeps the values, erasing a person deletes them.

    Curation — Keeping the vocabulary meaning one thing: merging near-duplicates, promoting or discarding proposals, retiring codes nobody uses, and writing the description that stops one code meaning four things to four people. It is attribute.define plus somebody who reads what they are given, and the documents treat it as a job rather than an outcome — an open vocabulary left unwatched decays into allergy, Allergy, alergie and allergies (2), after which the layer still stores everything and answers nothing.

    Not to be confused with identity, which stays structured in its own tables: a fact keeps a table of its own when there is a rule about it that an open layer physically cannot hold — one primary postal contact, one current name, a birth number that may not repeat. The boundary is that test, not sensitivity (domain/08 §1).

    Organization¶

    Org unit — Any node in the organization's structural hierarchy. Two kinds, and the difference is load-bearing:

    • The organization itself — the single root unit. There is exactly one, and it is where people are members.
    • Sub-units — weekly meeting groups, packs, sections, teams. Created by the organization or by other sub-units, nested to arbitrary depth. They run themselves: their own events, their own local leadership, their own roster.

    There is no fixed ladder of levels. Types are configurable rows, not a hardcoded enum, because the vocabulary will change over decades while the data must not.

    Unit type — A category of org unit (organization, pack, group, section, …). Defines whether units of that type may nest, and inside which other types. Most types carry no nesting rules at all, which leaves them unconstrained — the organization can invent a kind of group and nest it wherever it likes without asking the schema's permission.

    Position — A defined role within a unit that people can occupy: scoutmaster, assistant_leader, unit_treasurer, quartermaster. A position is a slot; it exists whether or not anyone fills it.

    Multiple people may occupy the same position at the same time. The schema makes no assumption of single occupancy. Some positions are organizationally limited to one holder at a time (position.max_holders), but that is a per-position limit, not a structural rule — and it is a limit the position declares about itself, never one imposed from outside the unit.

    Position assignment — The period during which a specific person occupies a specific position in a specific unit. Carries a start, an optional end, and a primary flag. Several assignments may be open against the same position simultaneously.

    Membership¶

    Membership — The period during which a person is a member of the organization. Holds valid_from, valid_to (null = open/current), and a status in an approval workflow. A person accumulates multiple memberships over time; they never overwrite one another.

    There is exactly one membership, and it is ageless. Membership does not distinguish children from adults, has no age ceiling, and does not end because a person got older. Being a counselor or a leader is a position, never a membership type.

    Membership type — A table kept for the future, currently holding one row: member. It survives as a table rather than collapsing into a constant so that a second kind of membership (a supporting or honorary one) would be a data change rather than a migration. The fee is flat and per person, so the type does not affect pricing either.

    Participation — The period during which a person takes part in one sub-unit. This is what a pack's roster is. Distinct from membership in every way: a person holds exactly one membership and any number of participations, participation is free, and it ends when the person stops attending rather than when a fee is unpaid. A participation row for the root unit is created alongside each membership, so "everyone in unit X" is one uniform query.

    Membership request — A person's application to become a member. Separate from the membership itself, because a request has its own lifecycle (submitted, under review, rejected, withdrawn) and can be rejected without ever producing a membership. Nothing gates a request except identity and payment — there is no checklist to satisfy.

    Lapse — A membership ending because the yearly fee was not paid or the person withdrew. Distinct from termination for cause, which requires a reason code. A lapsed person is not a member and holds no member permissions, though a renewal window lets them rejoin without re-applying.

    Renewal window — The fixed period after a membership expires during which payment restores it without re-applying, and — importantly — opens the new period at the expiry date, so the record shows continuous coverage and the person is not misclassified as a returning member. Tracked as dates, not a status: during the window the person is simply expired.

    Returning member — A person whose new membership follows a gap. The system detects this rather than treating them as new. This is the case you described: 1990–2010, gap, 2024–present.

    Relationships¶

    Guardianship — A period during which one person is responsible for another. Directional: guardian → dependent. Carries relationship kind (parent, legal_guardian, foster_parent, step_parent, grandparent, sibling, other) and an end date that a person sets. Not a birthday: the system takes no position on majority, and no job ends one (D-069).

    Party to a guardianship — The guardian and the subject, both. They, and not staff, run the arrangement: either may end it, a guardian may add another guardian, and no request has to go through the organization (D-073). Staff hold the same powers and are a route, never the route. This is scope authority applied to a household: authority over a nested thing originates at that thing.

    Guardian's right — One of exactly three values on the guardianship row: none, view, or write. Nothing more granular exists (D-074). They rank onto the same ladder as every other grant — 0, 10, 20 — so a guardian is not a second authorization model.

    Being a guardian still grants nothing by itself; none is a real value and the common starting point for anything the guardian did not assert themselves. What D-074 gives up in exchange for the short vocabulary is recorded in the catalog: it is not possible to say "read everything except the medical record."

    That sentence is not waiting on a mechanism, and the right level has not become softer for it. D-083 was put the case the sentence was invented for — a parent keeping access to everything about their child except the medical file, by court order — and declined it on the merits: there is no medical record inside the person record to be excepted from, because everything other than name, gender and age is descriptive data of one class. So the right level is what it always was, none, view or write, applied to the record whole — and the thing that was given up is not a half-built feature but an arrangement the organization decided not to want.

    Not to be confused with guardianship permission, which was the deleted eighteen-code list (dependent.profile.view, dependent.medical.admin, …). Those codes are gone; do not write them into a role, a query, or a screen.

    Household — No longer a thing in this system (D-057). A person's address is a person_contact row of type postal, which is temporal like any other contact. Two people at one address are related by a query, not by a table. The word is kept here only to answer "where did the household go?": shared address never granted access, and still does not.

    Acting on behalf — Performing an operation as another person, under that guardianship row's right level (view to read it, write to change it). Every such operation records both the acting person and the subject person.

    Authorization¶

    Permission — The atomic unit of authority: a coded capability such as membership.request.approve or unit.manage. Permissions are the only things the authorization layer actually checks.

    Role — A named bundle of permissions (unit_leader, registrar, treasurer, system_admin). Roles exist so that operationally meaningful sets can be assigned and reviewed as a unit.

    Role assignment — The period during which a person holds a role. Carries a scope: organization-wide, or limited to a specific org unit. Same mechanism for both, because "treasurer of Pack 12" and "treasurer of the organization" differ only in scope.

    Scope authority — The principle that authority over a unit originates at that unit and cannot be claimed from outside it. A parent unit — including the organization itself — can never decide permissions over a nested unit. Coverage of subordinate units exists only where the unit itself has granted it, as an explicit scope_exposure row. The single exception is system_admin. See domain/06-authorization-model.md.

    Access grant — The item-scoped layer you asked for: a period during which a person may act on one specific record — this event, this chat thread, this document, this unit's data — with a level (revoked < view < contribute < manage < admin). This is what "per-item permission" means in this design.

    It may also target an audience (D-042), which is how "open this event to everyone" or "to the whole pack" is expressed. A grant names either a person or an audience, never both.

    Audience — A grant target that stands for a set of people rather than one. Two kinds: built-in audiences, which are rules the resolver evaluates rather than stored lists (everyone, all_members, the guardians of a given person, the participants of a given unit, the leaders of a given unit), and named groups, which are stored membership lists someone administers. The built-in kind is the important one: a new pack member automatically receives what the pack was granted, with no rows to maintain and no stale-group problem.

    Membership gate — The rule that authority held through a role is honoured only while the holder has a current membership. Two carve-outs: guardianship (a legal relationship, not a membership) and roles marked requires_membership = false (appointed counselors and local leaders, who must be able to serve regardless of their own fee). Ownership is exempt too, so a lapse cannot lock someone out of the screen they renew on. See domain/06-authorization-model.md.

    Locality — The rule that a grant on the specific item always beats a grant from a broader scope. A per-event grant outranks a unit-scoped one, which outranks an organization-wide one. Locality decides between competing allows only — a deny wins over everything, at any scope. See domain/06-authorization-model.md.

    Scope — What a grant applies to. Either an org unit, a concrete item (via scope_type + scope_id), or the whole organization.

    admin — Was ambiguous three ways; two deletions later, two remain. Say which you mean:

    Meaning What it does Status
    Grant level — access_level.admin, rank 40 How much authority a grant carries. revoked < view < contribute < manage < admin, compared by integer rank, never by string. A check asks "at least level X?" Real, untouched
    Resource wildcard — person.admin, event.admin A permission code implying every sibling capability on that resource, matched by prefix in the resolver Deleted — all sixteen codes and the prefix match (D-068)
    Edit action — dependent.medical.admin Means "edit", paired against .view inside a sub-resource. Implies nothing Deleted — with the whole dependent.* namespace (D-074)

    What survives of "can do everything": system.admin (the only wildcard permission, held through a role, never an item grant) and roles — a bundle the organization composes, which is where "someone who can do everything about events" now lives, written out as thirteen rows rather than implied by a resolver rule.

    Scope exposure — An explicit row by which a unit accepts one named role assignment as authority over itself. This is the only way authority crosses a unit boundary downward, and it is created at the subordinate unit's own level, never assumed from the hierarchy. It names an assignment — a particular person in a particular role for a particular period — which is why a unit is approving a person rather than opening a door any future holder may walk through. An exposure extends that assignment and nothing else: not the role, and never a direct access grant (D-078). See data-model/05-authorization.md.

    Channel — Which of the resolver's four questions answers a check: ownership, guardianship, item grants, roles, with an absolute deny in front of all of them. How a check reaches a channel changed in round 6 and it is easy to state backwards. It used to be the permission's namespace — a code beginning dependent. went to guardianship. That silently skipped every guardian act whose code was an ordinary one, like a parent editing their child's address under person.address.edit. Now guardianship is reached when the person the act is about is not the person performing it, which nobody has to remember, because the shape of the call decides (D-079). Corollary, and the part people get wrong: the guardianship channel can only say yes. When there is no guardianship, or the right is too low, it says nothing and the person's own roles are still tried — otherwise a parent who is also the event registrar would be blocked from the roster by their own view over their child. Prohibitions are not made here; they are explicit denies.

    Effective permission — The union of everything a person may do right now, computed from role assignments and access grants that are currently valid. Never stored, always derived, so it cannot drift out of sync with the grants that produce it.

    Delegation — Temporarily granting someone your authority (e.g. a leader going on leave). Modeled as an access grant with an explicit end date and a delegated_from reference, so the provenance of authority is visible.

    Events¶

    Event — A dated occasion that people register for: a camp, a trip, a hike, a jamboree. It belongs to exactly one unit, has a start and an end, an optional capacity, an optional fee, and a set of organizers. It has no status column — visibility is the permission model, and cancellation is a recorded fact rather than a state (D-064).

    A weekly meeting is not an event. It is participation. A Thursday group meeting forty times a year is one unit_participation row, not forty events — so there is no recurrence, no series, and no occurrence table (D-058). This boundary is deliberate and easy to miss: "was Jan at the meeting on 14 March?" is not answerable from the events module.

    Organizer — Someone running an event. A roster entry on the event, with an optional label ('lead', 'cook', 'driver') and an order — not an access_grant (D-062). It is the event-scoped analogue of a position assignment: an appointment on the thing being run, not a permission bundle. Authority to act on the event comes from the owning unit's roles, which reach the unit's own events because an event is an artifact of its unit rather than a child of it (D-063).

    Registration — One person's answer about one event: attending, or not_attending. "Unanswered" is not a value — it is the absence of a registration row, so "who has not replied yet?" is the eligible set minus the registered set (D-059).

    Eligibility — Who may register. This is capability, not a rule engine: event.register_self requires contribute on the event, and opening a camp to members is one access_grant naming an audience. An age-restricted camp is a camp opened to a unit's participants (D-060).

    Waitlist — What happens when a full event gets a self-service registration: the person is written down and ordered, never refused. Organizers may exceed capacity outright, and the overrun is reported rather than prevented — the leader who knows there are two spare tents is trusted (D-065).

    Presence — Whether someone actually turned up, marked by an organizer after the fact. Three-valued: NULL (no register taken), true, false. For a camp this is the record of who was in the organization's care, and it cannot be reconstructed from registrations (D-067). Not the same as registration — a no-show is a registration with was_present = false.

    Money¶

    Payment request — A liability rendered payable: an amount, a recipient account, a start, and an optional expiry. The request, not the payment, is the entity the finance module is built around, because it is the only thing that connects "we expect money" to "money arrived" (D-075). It is not an invoice; the organization may also owe people invoices, which is still open.

    Variable symbol — The number a payer types into their bank, and the number the bank reports back against the money. Unique across every symbol ever issued, not merely across live ones, and taken from a sequence — never derived from a birth date, a member number, or anything else a stranger could guess. Anyone can type eleven digits into a payment form; guessing a symbol puts a stranger's money into a child's financial record.

    A QR code is a rendering, not data. It encodes the symbol, the amount and the recipient that already exist. Nothing stores it and nothing invalidates it.

    There is no card gateway. The organization collects, but by bank API rather than checkout: no card tokens, no disputes, no chargebacks, no processor reconciliation, and no pending or failed payment status — a bank transfer the bank has reported does not later fail. A refund is a second payment going the other way, never a reversal of the first.

    Unmatched money — A transfer whose symbol resolves to nothing live: mistyped, belonging to a cancelled event, already settled, or the wrong amount. It waits for a person (D-076). It is never auto-applied by payer identity — a parent with two children pays one amount is the normal case, not the edge case, and the fallback would be wrong on the common path.

    Communication¶

    Direct message — One person to one person. Everyone can send one; there is no permission for it (D-077).

    Broadcast — The only messaging permission, and an ordinary scoped one: granted on a unit it reaches that unit, granted organization-wide it reaches everyone. There is deliberately no global broadcast flag, because the alternative leaves a pack leader choosing between an organization-wide power and typing the same sentence twelve times.

    Delivery channel — Email, push, or in-app, chosen by the recipient, not the sender. The sender chooses who; the recipient chooses how it arrives. This is why the system is the medium rather than a mail relay: a bounced email is a failed delivery on one channel, not a lost message.

    Age is not a sender concept — and it is not a recipient concept either, which is the part worth saying out loud. Nothing restricts who may address a child (D-077); what stops a private message to a child from being unnoticed is mirroring (D-082): the guardians get a copy, the sender is told before sending that they will, and no check in the resolver ever looks at an age.

    Mirroring — A copy of a direct message addressed to a minor, delivered to that minor's guardians whose right level is view or write, fixed to that set at the moment of sending. Visibility, not approval: nobody has to agree before the message is sent. A guardian at none receives nothing, because none is the organization having already decided that this party sees nothing about the child — and a minor whose guardians are all none is the same alertable shape as a minor with no guardian at all, so it is reported together with it.

    Time¶

    Temporal row — Any row with valid_from / valid_to. valid_to IS NULL means "still true". All permission checks and history queries filter on these. The full pattern is specified in data-model/00-conventions.md.

    Point-in-time query — A question of the form "who/what was true on date D?" — "Who was in Pack 12 on 1 June 1998?", "Was this person a member when they attended camp?" These are first-class requirements, not an afterthought, because you have four decades of history to preserve.

    As-of vs audit time — Two different times, both needed. As-of is the date the fact was true about the world (a membership that ran 1990–2010). Audit time is when the row was written in the system. A membership backdated during a 2026 data-entry session has as-of 1990–2010 and audit time 2026. Do not conflate them.

    Roles in conversation (informal terms — not names in the model)¶

    The words you and the organization will naturally use, and what they map to:

    Informal term What it is in the model
    "child member" a person with an open membership whose birth_date makes them a minor — age is a fact about the person, not a kind of membership
    "counselor" / "leader" a position_assignment held by a member — never a membership type
    "parent" / "caretaker" party to an active guardianship row
    "leader of the Thursday group" role_assignment of a leadership role, scoped to that org_unit
    "in the Thursday group" an open unit_participation row for that sub-unit
    "ex-member" person with a membership whose valid_to has passed
    "lapsed but can still renew" valid_to passed, within the renewal window
    "registered but not a member" person with an account and no open membership — they may sign in and see their own data, but hold no member permissions
    "they're allowed to attend" access_grant on that event at level view or above, naming them or an audience they belong to
    "open to everyone" an access_grant whose audience is the built-in everyone
    "the treasurer" role_assignment of treasurer, scope = org or unit
