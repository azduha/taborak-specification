    Glossary — Ubiquitous Language¶

    Terms used consistently across every document in this folder. Where everyday language is ambiguous, the definition here wins and the schema follows it. Each entry says what a term means in the system as designed today; how a term came to mean that is in the decision log.

    The three-way split that everything else depends on¶

    These three words are frequently conflated in real organizations. This design keeps them strictly apart, because conflating them is the single most common source of unfixable data models in this domain.

    Term Definition Table
    Person A human being the organization knows about, now or ever. Exists independent of whether they have ever logged in, paid, or applied. person
    Account A set of login credentials attached to a person. Optional — a person imported from a 1994 paper roster has no account. account
    Member Not a noun in this system. A person is "a member" at a moment in time if they hold a qualifying membership. It is always a question with a date attached. membership

    A fourth, related distinction:

    Term Definition Table
    User Avoid entirely. When you mean the account, say account; when you mean the human, say person. Most systems use "user" for the wrong one and it never gets fixed. —

    Identity¶

    Person — A human. Stores given name, family name, birth date, and gender. Nothing else: no member status, no child/adult flag, no group, no office, and no deceased flag — the organization does not record death. What the organization records about the human is not on this table at all: it is descriptive data, reached by the same permission as the rest of the person record, because none of it is a different kind of object. Specified in data-model/01 §2.

    Account — Credentials and login state for a person. A person may have zero or more accounts over their lifetime (a childhood account abandoned in 2010, a new one created in 2024), but at most one live one — closed rows aside. Accounts are never shared between people, not even between a parent and child.

    Account activation gate — The rule that an account may not reach active while an unresolved identity hypothesis names its person. A sign-up that strong-matches an existing person — exact legacy member number, or exact birth date together with a normalized name, never the fuzzy band — raises a hypothesis. Its own rows are created and stay pending_verification: nothing is merged, nothing is linked, nothing is activated. The contact point the person typed survives as the credential it was offered as, and identity_reviewer decides it with person.merge. The holding message confirms nothing, since a sign-up form that can answer "yes, that person exists" is an existence oracle over a roster of children; and a hypothesis that is blocking an account gets an age check that alerts, because a stalled queue is not a data-quality problem but a specific person who cannot log in (D-089). "I have a new e-mail address" is not this case and is not routed through it: account_credential holds many credentials for one account.

    Contact point — A single email address, phone number, or postal address belonging to a person, one row per point in person_contact rather than a column, so that a person can have five email addresses, or a new kind invented later, without a migration. Each has a type, a purpose, and a verification timestamp. A postal address is a contact point of type postal: two people at one address are related by a query, not by a table, and a shared address grants nothing to anybody.

    Identity hypothesis — A proposed but unconfirmed link between two person rows that may actually be the same human. Created during legacy import, by the nightly matcher, when someone registers claiming a historical identity, or by a sign-up that strong-matches a person who already exists. Confirming one triggers a merge; while one is unresolved it also holds the account of any person it names out of active — see account activation gate.

    Merge — The act of declaring two person rows to be one human, and collapsing them. Never a delete: the absorbed row survives as a tombstone pointing at the survivor, so that historical foreign keys keep resolving. Always a human's decision (person.merge), including at the highest score — and refused outright when the chosen survivor turns out, on resolution, to be the person being absorbed.

    Merge chain — What forms when a survivor is itself later absorbed. The pointer is not rewritten forward: A→B→C stays a chain and resolve_person() walks it to the live row. Chains persist because reversing a merge would otherwise be a reconstruction of facts nobody recorded. Two rules come with the chain, neither on the read: a cycle is impossible because the merge is refused, never because a hop limit gave up; and a chain longer than three hops is reported, which is a statement about the matcher's queue, not about the row.

    Unmerge — Reversing a merge a human approved. The tombstone comes off, rows created before the merge go back to the revived person, and rows created inside the window are flagged for a human rather than split by a rule — every access_grant from that window flagged, not guessed. The merge is reversible; the merged window is not: what two people did while they were one row stays one row's history, because the record cannot separate who did it. No permission of its own — undoing an identity correction is the same act as making one, so it is person.merge.

    Membership export — One of the two files the legacy corpus actually is, and neither of them is a roster. A row says: a person, a birth date, and the period they were a member of the unit. A people batch turns it into person rows and bounded membership rows — both ends dated, data_confidence = 'low', batch id set — and no fee obligation of any kind. It names no group either, so it answers neither "who was in the Thursday group in 2011" nor "were they a child or a rover", and nothing is created in person_group from it: a corpus that names no groups means every roster row is typed by a person, which is also the only way one can be true.

    Attendance export — The other file: events, and which people came. It becomes event rows with no owner — there is no column for an imported event to have (D-102) — with one event_registration per attendee at attending and was_present = true, and no not_attending rows, because an absence row would say the system asked someone who was never asked. Attendance never becomes a membership, and it never becomes a group roster either: attendance is read through the ATTENDED and PLACED selectors, and no rows are written into person_group_member from it.

    Personal data¶

    Everything the system knows about a person once you subtract what they are called and counted as. The module is domain/08-person-data.md, the tables are data-model/09-attributes.md.

    Descriptive data — Everything the system holds about a person other than their name, their gender and their age: one class of data, with no privileged corner inside it. Allergies, medication, insurance numbers, languages, measurements, clothing sizes, dietary habits, a grandparent's phone manner — the same kind of fact, held in fields rather than files, none of it a different kind of object from any other, and all of it reached by the permission that already reads the person. The term exists to rule a category out: there is no sensitive sub-record with its own codes. Why: a sub-record of its own was declined on the merits, and its cost — one read rule over the whole record — is stated in D-083. It is the reason person.view is graded in the catalog's top tier.

    Attribute definition — One thing the organization has decided to collect: a code, a label, a description of what belongs in the field, a value type, and whether several values may be current at once. The type draws the form and raises the warning; it never refuses a save a human insists on — 2019 recorded as the year of a checkup is a truer record than an empty field behind a validation error. Creating, renaming, merging or retiring a definition is attribute.define; recording a value against one needs nothing beyond person.edit.

    Person attribute — The value one person currently has for one code. Overwritten in place: the row is the organization's present belief, and the sequence of earlier beliefs lives in the audit trail, values included, for 24 months — logged on the same terms as a contact point or a note, because a list of person fields whose values must not be logged would be a privileged corner, and would leave nothing behind for an overwritten value. What guards the trail is who may read it, not what it omits — and it omits, on a clock: those logged values are emptied 24 months after they were written (see payload reduction), so the trail is the record of what changed, who did it and when for the life of the system and the record of the values for two years. The honest name for the whole stack is therefore no long history, not merely no temporal columns: the value lives in one overwritten row, the copy of it depends on a write path remembering to log, and the copy ages out. If a case ever needs the long version, the fix is temporal columns on the value — D-085 reversed — and not a longer trail. No valid_from / valid_to, deliberately (data-model/09 §3).

    Proposed code — A definition that came into existence because somebody recorded a value against a code that did not exist. It waits in the curation queue: kept out of data entry forms and reports, yet readable by anyone already entitled to read the person, because the organization's data quality problem is not the family's confidentiality problem. It is where an improvised need at 23:00 goes instead of into a notes box that nobody will read again — improvised by a person at a desk, which is the whole meaning. It is emphatically not "whatever the legacy import did not understand": the codes a batch's column→code map names arrive live, because a curator read that map as one object before the batch applied, and several hundred proposals landing on day one would make this queue's age signal meaningless before it had ever meant anything.

    Retired code — A definition taken out of data entry and left alone everywhere else: readable, reportable, revivable, its values untouched. Deleting a label must never delete the facts filed under it, or the badly-named label is never retired and the vocabulary accretes instead of being curated. Redaction is the opposite operation: retiring keeps the values, erasing a person deletes them, immediately and whatever their age. The other thing that removes stored values is payload reduction, below, and the distinction is that redaction answers to a person and is absolute, while reduction answers to a calendar and is bounded.

    Payload reduction — Emptying the old_values / new_values out of an audit row 24 months after it was written, leaving everything else standing: actor, on-behalf-of, operation, table, record id, changed fields, transaction, session, channel. The trail goes on answering who changed this row, when, and were they allowed to for the life of the system, and stops answering and to what after two years; twelve months is the floor the window may be set to. Not redaction, which is a named person's erasure, immediate and absolute, and reaches payloads of any age; not an import revert, which deletes the rows a batch created. Reduction is the one case in this design that deliberately unwrites later than the write, so its failure mode is not running at all — hence a scheduled job with a check that alerts on any row past its window still carrying a payload (D-088).

    The two clocks — The accounting skeleton (membership, fee_obligation, payment, and the anonymised person row an erasure leaves behind) has no clock at all, justified as the organization's own history. Assumption: that this indefinite retention of rows about a named person has a legal basis — organizational history plus accounting obligations, against a redacted person row that keeps the skeleton non-identifying — is unverified, and a decision log is not the document that grants it. The audit trail runs the opposite direction: rows forever, payloads for 24 months. Erasure sits outside both, immediate and absolute. The same window is proposed for access_log and is not yet confirmed (F-8).

    Curation — Keeping the vocabulary meaning one thing: merging near-duplicates, promoting or discarding proposals, retiring codes nobody uses, and writing the description that stops one code meaning four things to four people. It is attribute.define plus somebody who reads what they are given, and the documents treat it as a job rather than an outcome — an open vocabulary left unwatched decays into allergy, Allergy, alergie and allergies (2), after which the layer still stores everything and answers nothing.

    Not to be confused with identity, which stays structured in its own tables: a fact keeps a table of its own when there is a rule about it that an open layer physically cannot hold — one primary postal contact, one current name, a birth number that may not repeat. The boundary is that test, not sensitivity (domain/08 §1).

    Organization¶

    The unit — There is exactly one, and it is the organization. It is a scope, not a row: no table, no id, no parent, no type, no name history, no path. In grant_scope_type its code is unit — the one row there with is_item_scoped = false — and a grant written at the unit carries no scope_id. Say the unit: not "the root unit", not "the organization unit", not "org"; there is no second unit for the word root to distinguish this one from, and a name that implies a second one is how a hierarchy creeps back into a sentence. Membership, a role, and a grant at non-item scope are all held at the unit, and a grant at it reaches every item of every type. There are no unit kinds either: pack, troop and section name nothing in the model, and where these documents need a set of people they say group (D-097). What one unit costs — there is no body between a person and a unit-scope read, and no per-unit report — is stated in domain/04 §1.

    Group — A person_group: a named set of people with explicitly stated, dated members and a lifetime of its own. A name, a description, valid_from / valid_to and the audit columns; its roster is person_group_member(person, valid_from, valid_to, who wrote it, note), dated rows that may not overlap counted over all rows, half-open. Company, not a body: the Thursday group, the kitchen crew, this year's camp staff — in the organization's own words "a temporary set of members that do e.g. events together". What a group is not: no parent and no children, no type and no vocabulary of types, no code, no path, no nesting rules, no rename history, no split or merger edge, no leadership of its own, no roles, and no fee. Being in a group authorizes nothing — person_group.member.manage writes a roster and nothing else; participation is not authority, and tidier storage does not promote it (D-098). A group is an item (registered in grant_scope_type), so "these two may work this group's roster" is a grant on one group. Its two dated facts are different facts: the group's own period says it existed, the member rows say who was in it, and "who was in schuzka2026-1 on 14 March 2026" is a point-in-time read over the roster — which is why the rows are dated, and why that answer survives the audit trail's payload expiry. A group is not an audience: a group states its members and an audience picks them (Audience). Nobody writes themselves onto a roster — person_group.member.manage has no self-scoped variant and none will be added, and no group is seeded. Moving a person between groups closes one dated row and opens another. Specified in domain/04 §2 and §3.

    Office (a role) and role_holder — An office is a small, visible set of holders — the leaders, the finance people, IT — where a group is company. The difference decides who may edit the membership: role.assign / role.unassign are 🔒🔒, the most sensitive family in the catalog, while writing a group's roster is person_group.member.manage at ⚠. role keeps code, name, description, is_system, sort_order; role_holder(role, person, valid_from, valid_to, granted_by, note) is the holder row and names exactly one person — one grantee per row, always, because F-12 is closed and the answer is no. A role confers nothing: the codes an office carries are the live grants whose expression names ROLE(x), which is what makes "what may a treasurer do?" a query over rows a person wrote (D-100). An office is held at the unit, always; a role is an item, so a grant can be written on one. Nothing forces a holder row to end — the report is the control (D-071), and an empty office is not a reportable vacancy: absence of rows has no start date (F-18).

    Membership¶

    Membership — The period during which a person is a member of the unit. Holds valid_from, valid_to (null = open/current), and a status in an approval workflow. A person accumulates multiple memberships over time; they never overwrite one another. "Everyone in the organization" is one query against membership. There is exactly one kind of it and it is ageless: membership does not distinguish children from adults, has no age ceiling, and does not end because a person got older. Being a counselor or a leader is an office held through role_holder, never a membership type.

    Membership type — A table kept for the future, currently holding one row: member. It survives as a table rather than collapsing into a constant so that a second kind of membership (a supporting or honorary one) would be a data change rather than a migration. The fee is flat and per person, so the type does not affect pricing either.

    Membership request — A person's application to become a member of the unit. Separate from the membership itself, because a request has its own lifecycle (submitted, under review, rejected, withdrawn) and can be rejected without ever producing a membership. Nothing gates a request except identity and payment — there is no checklist to satisfy. Which group a new member lands in is a write on the group, never a preference on the request.

    Lapse — A membership ending because the yearly fee was not paid or the person withdrew. Distinct from termination for cause, which requires a reason code. A lapsed person is not a member and holds no member permissions, though a renewal window lets them rejoin without re-applying.

    Renewal window — The fixed period after a membership expires during which payment restores it without re-applying, and — importantly — opens the new period at the expiry date, so the record shows continuous coverage and the person is not misclassified as a returning member. Tracked as dates, not a status: during the window the person is simply expired.

    Returning member — A person whose new membership follows a gap. The system detects this rather than treating them as new: 1990–2010, gap, 2024–present.

    Imported membership — An imported membership is bounded at both ends, always. A membership is never inferred from attendance (D-093).

    Relationships¶

    Guardianship — A period during which one person is responsible for another. Directional: guardian → dependent. Carries relationship kind (parent, legal_guardian, foster_parent, step_parent, grandparent, sibling, other) and an end date that a person sets. Not a birthday: the system takes no position on majority, and no job ends one (D-069).

    Party to a guardianship — The guardian and the subject, both. They, and not staff, run the arrangement: either may end it, a guardian may add another guardian, and no request has to go through the organization (D-073). Staff hold the same powers and are a route, never the route. A guardianship is a relationship between two people who are both principals on the row, and that is why they run it.

    Guardian's right — One of exactly three values on the guardianship row: none, view, or write. Nothing more granular exists (D-074). A right is measured against permission.right_kind (read | write): none covers nothing, view covers read codes, write covers both. A guardian is a channel, not a set and not a grant: a guardian's act is checked under the ordinary code for the thing being touched, so this is one vocabulary and not a second authorization model. Being a guardian still grants nothing by itself; none is a real value and the common starting point for anything the guardian did not assert themselves.

    How the channel answers: a check looks for a live guardianship row linking the person asking to the person the action is about, and reads right_level off it — a lookup over a relationship, not a set anything resolves; the channel returns an allow or says nothing at all. The history of that value is guardianship_permission (right_level, valid_from, valid_to, changed_reason, changed_by) — no grantee, no permission code, no audience. A guardian is neither a grantee nor an operand, and write means any action about this child, which is wider than any expression could assemble and narrower in the direction that matters: it reaches no item that is not about the child. There is no guardian-shaped permission code anywhere in the catalog and none scoped to "my ward", so nothing has to be granted for a guardian to act as a guardian (D-113). The system computes no guardian list from these rows; "send these six children's parents the newsletter" is a person_group somebody maintains, aimed at through GROUP(x).

    What the short vocabulary gives up is recorded in the catalog: it is not possible to say "read everything except the medical record." Why: the case that sentence was invented for — a parent keeping access to everything about their child except the medical file, by court order — was declined on the merits, because there is no medical record inside the person record to be excepted from: everything other than name, gender and age is descriptive data of one class (D-083). So the right level is none, view or write, applied to the record whole, and what was given up is not a half-built feature but an arrangement the organization decided not to want.

    Acting on behalf — Performing an operation as another person, under that guardianship row's right level (view to read it, write to change it). Every such operation records both the acting person and the subject person (data-model/04 §5).

    Authorization¶

    The authorization vocabulary — permission, scope, grant, allow and deny, channel, audience, selector, the expression grammar, the compilation, and the states a named thing gets instead of deletion — is defined on its own page: glossary-authorization.md.

    Events¶

    Event — A dated occasion that people register for: a camp, a trip, a hike, a jamboree. It has a start and an end, an optional capacity, an optional fee, and a set of organizers. It has no status column — visibility is the permission model, and cancellation is a recorded fact rather than a state. Nothing owns an event: there is no event.org_unit_id and no can_event(); the people connected to an event are named by selectors that bind to it (Bound operand), and an event is addressed by being the item of an item-scope grant, which is the only way anything reaches it (D-064, D-102).

    A weekly meeting is not an event. Forty meetings a year are not forty events, so there is no recurrence, no series and no occurrence table. What the meeting has is a group to be in — a dated roster, not a calendar of evenings. "Was Jan at the meeting on 14 March?" is answerable by nobody: a group says who is in it between two dates, not who walked in on a given evening.

    Organizer — Someone running an event. A roster entry on the event (event_organizer) with an optional label ('lead', 'cook', 'driver') and an order — not an access_grant, and analogous only to being a member of a group. Authority to act on the event arrives from one seeded grant template — event.answer.view, scoped to an event, aimed at the bound operand ORGANIZERS — instantiated per event by whoever creates it. An event created without that grant has organizers who can do nothing, which is why the events with no answer-view grant report exists (D-062).

    Registration — One person's answer about one event: attending, or not_attending. "Unanswered" is not a value — it is the absence of a registration row, so "who has not replied yet?" is the eligible set minus the registered set.

    Eligibility — Who may register. This is capability, not a rule engine: event.register_self is an ordinary permission, allowed by a live allow grant and blocked by any live deny, granted either on the unit or on the event itself; opening a camp to members is one access_grant naming an audience whose expression contains MEMBERS; an age-restricted camp is a camp opened to a group (D-060).

    Waitlist — What happens when a full event gets a self-service registration: the person is written down and ordered, never refused. Organizers may exceed capacity outright, and the overrun is reported rather than prevented — the leader who knows there are two spare tents is trusted (D-065).

    Presence — Whether someone actually turned up, marked by an organizer after the fact. Three-valued: NULL (no register taken), true, false. For a camp this is the record of who was in the organization's care, and it cannot be reconstructed from registrations. Not the same as registration — a no-show is a registration with was_present = false. Because it is NULL until somebody marks it, there is no PRESENT selector: a selector that meant "came" would silently mean "was not marked absent" on every event nobody took a register for.

    The four attendance tiers — Four argument-less selectors that bind to the event a grant is scoped to, so a sentence written once reads correctly on every event it is copied to. From widest to narrowest:

    Selector Who it reaches, on the bound event
    ATTENDED anyone whose response is attending — asked, said yes, whether or not there was room
    PLACED those attending who were not waitlisted — the set the capacity actually let through
    CONFIRMED PLACED and is_confirmed — somebody has since looked at each of them
    ON_WAITLIST written down, ordered, not yet placed — waiting, not attending
    ORGANIZERS the event's organizer roster — the reason a per-event answer-view grant is seeded

    They are not one set with flags: ATTENDED is wider than PLACED precisely because a full event says yes to more people than it can seat, and CONFIRMED is narrower than PLACED because a seat is not a headcount. WHITELISTED is not one of these, and is not a selector at all — see EV-6.

    Money¶

    Payment request — A liability rendered payable: an amount, a recipient account, a start, and an optional expiry. The request, not the payment, is the entity the finance module is built around, because it is the only thing that connects "we expect money" to "money arrived". It is not an invoice; the organization may also owe people invoices, which is still open (D-075).

    Variable symbol — The number a payer types into their bank, and the number the bank reports back against the money. Unique across every symbol ever issued, not merely across live ones, and taken from a sequence — never derived from a birth date, a member number, or anything else a stranger could guess. Anyone can type eleven digits into a payment form; guessing a symbol puts a stranger's money into a child's financial record.

    A QR code is a rendering, not data. It encodes the symbol, the amount and the recipient that already exist. Nothing stores it and nothing invalidates it.

    There is no card gateway. The organization collects, but by bank API rather than checkout: no card tokens, no disputes, no chargebacks, no processor reconciliation, and no pending or failed payment status — a bank transfer the bank has reported does not later fail. A refund is a second payment going the other way, never a reversal of the first.

    Unmatched money — A transfer whose symbol resolves to nothing live: mistyped, belonging to a cancelled event, already settled, or the wrong amount. It waits for a person. It is never auto-applied by payer identity — a parent with two children pays one amount is the normal case, not the edge case, and the fallback would be wrong on the common path (D-076).

    Communication¶

    Direct message — One person to one person. Everyone can send one; there is no permission for it.

    Broadcast — The only messaging permission, and an ordinary scoped one: a grant like any other. Held at the unit, message.broadcast reaches everyone; the narrower reading is a grant naming a group, reaching that roster. There is no global broadcast flag: a permission that has to be granted is one that can be given to some people and refused to others, whereas the flag would be all-or-nothing and would leave a section leader choosing between an organization-wide power and typing the same sentence twelve times. Whether broadcast should ever be grantable on an item is messaging's open question, not the model's (D-077).

    Delivery channel — Email, push, or in-app, chosen by the recipient, not the sender. The sender chooses who; the recipient chooses how it arrives. This is why the system is the medium rather than a mail relay: a bounced email is a failed delivery on one channel, not a lost message.

    Age is not a sender concept — and it is not a recipient concept either. Nothing restricts who may address a child; what stops a private message to a child from being unnoticed is mirroring, and no check in the resolver ever looks at an age.

    Mirroring — A copy of a direct message addressed to a minor, delivered to that minor's guardians whose right is view or write — the three-value right of the guardianship channel, not a level — fixed to that set at the moment of sending. Visibility, not approval: nobody has to agree before the message is sent. A guardian at none receives nothing, because none is the organization having already decided that this party sees nothing about the child — and a minor whose guardians are all none is the same alertable shape as a minor with no guardian at all, so it is reported together with it (D-082).

    Time¶

    Temporal row — Any row with valid_from / valid_to. valid_to IS NULL means "still true". All permission checks and history queries filter on these. The full pattern is specified in data-model/00-conventions.md.

    Point-in-time query — A question of the form "who/what was true on date D?" — "Who was in the group schuzka2026-1 on 1 June 1998?", "Was this person a member when they attended camp?" These are first-class requirements, not an afterthought, because there are four decades of history to preserve. The answer depends on which named set the question is about, and there are only two kinds to be about: a group answers it from its dated roster, and an audience cannot answer it at all — its expression is evaluated live, and its one past is the audit trail. That includes a name typed straight into the expression: a PERSON(x) leaf carries no dates of its own, so "was she in this audience on 1 June 2024?" is a recomputation over audit payloads that expire after 24 months (D-088).

    As-of vs audit time — Two different times, both needed. As-of is the date the fact was true about the world (a membership that ran 1990–2010). Audit time is when the row was written in the system. A membership backdated during a 2026 data-entry session has as-of 1990–2010 and audit time 2026. Do not conflate them.

    Roles in conversation (informal terms — not names in the model)¶

    The words you and the organization will naturally use, and what they map to:

    Informal term What it is in the model
    "child member" a person with an open membership whose birth_date makes them a minor — age is a fact about the person, not a kind of membership
    "counselor" / "leader" an office held through role_holder — never a membership type, and conferring nothing by itself: authority comes from the grants that name ROLE(counselors)
    "parent" / "caretaker" party to an active guardianship row
    "leader of the Thursday group" there is no such thing: an office is held for the unit and has no scope column, so this is a member of that group, plus whatever a grant aimed at them or at it allows
    "in the Thursday group" an open person_group_member row in that person_group — company, not authority
    "ex-member" person with a membership whose valid_to has passed
    "lapsed but can still renew" valid_to passed, within the renewal window
    "registered but not a member" person with an account and no open membership — they may sign in and see their own data, but hold no member permissions
    "they're allowed to attend" a live allow grant naming event.register_self, aimed at them or at a set they belong to, and no live deny anywhere naming them — not a level, and nothing inherited from a unit that owns the event
    "open to everyone" a grant naming the seeded audience everyone, whose expression is the operand EVERYONE — or all_members, whose expression is MEMBERS. A bare name in an expression is not a reference to either: audiences are named only as AUDIENCE(code)
    "the treasurer" role_holder of treasurer, held for the unit — there is no narrower place to hold it, and holding it answers no permission question on its own
    "put me in the Thursday group" a person_group_member row somebody else writes under person_group.member.manage, which has no self-scoped variant — there is no self-service path and no permission with which to build one
    "the kitchen crew" a person_group if the answer is a list of people somebody keeps up by hand, dated and quotable years later; an audience if the answer is a sentence recomputed each time it is asked. "Whom did this grant reach on 1 June 2024?" is answerable for the first and not the second
    "the permission group for the kitchen crew" one or the other of the row above; an expression names a group through GROUP(code) and another audience through AUDIENCE(code)
    "the old Pack 12 came back" there are no units to bring back. The closest thing left is a group whose name repeats a dead one, and that is only a reused label: groups have no code, no succession and no rename history
    "the organizers" the event's own event_organizer roster, reached as ORGANIZERS on a grant scoped to that event — an appointment on the thing being run
